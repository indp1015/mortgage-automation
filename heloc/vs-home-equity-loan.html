HELOC vs. Home Equity Loan: Which Is Right for You?

Both let you tap home equity, but they work very differently. Choose based on how you need the money and how much certainty you want on rates.

Quick Comparison

Feature HELOC Home Equity Loan
Rate type Variable (Prime + margin) Fixed
How funds are disbursed Revolving line — draw as needed One lump sum
Typical draw period 10 years (interest-only) N/A — fully amortized
Repayment 20 years (after draw period) 5–30 years fixed
Best for Ongoing projects, emergency reserve, staged renovations One-time large expense (debt consolidation, major renovation)
Rate certainty Low (rate can rise) High (locked at closing)
Closing costs Often $0–$500 (some lenders waive) Typically 2–5% of loan amount

When a HELOC Wins

  • You want flexibility — draw only what you need, when you need it
  • You're staging renovations — kitchen now, bathroom next year
  • You want an emergency reserve — a HELOC as a safety net (rather than an emergency fund in cash)
  • You plan to repay quickly — HELOCs often allow interest-only payments during the draw period

When a Home Equity Loan Wins

  • You need one large lump sum — a specific renovation or debt payoff
  • You want a fixed rate — predictable payment for the full term
  • You plan to stay put long-term — fixed rates protect against rising Prime Rate
  • You're refinancing high-interest debt — fixed-term payoff is simpler

Tax Treatment (2026)

Both HELOC and home equity loan interest may be tax-deductible only if the funds are used to buy, build, or substantially improve the home securing the loan. Personal expenses (debt consolidation, vacations, cars) do not qualify for the mortgage interest deduction under current IRS rules. See IRS Publication 936 for details, or consult a tax professional.

Risks Both Share

  • Your home is collateral — default can lead to foreclosure
  • Variable rates on HELOCs can rise — a $100,000 line at 8% becomes $130,000 of annual interest at 10.4%
  • Lump-sum temptation — drawing more than you need costs more

FAQ

Can I convert a HELOC to a fixed rate?

Many lenders offer a "fixed-rate advance" option where you lock a portion of your HELOC balance at a fixed rate. Ask your lender — not all HELOCs have this.

Which has lower closing costs?

HELOCs typically have lower or zero closing costs (some lenders cover them in exchange for a fee if you close the line within 3 years). Home equity loans usually cost 2–5% of the loan amount in closing costs.

Can I have both a HELOC and a home equity loan?

Technically yes, but lenders usually cap total combined LTV at 80–85%. Most borrowers choose one.
