How HELOC Borrowing Works

  1. Home value × max LTV (typically 85%) = maximum total debt allowed
  2. Minus your current mortgage balance = available HELOC credit
  3. Your rate = Prime Rate + credit-based margin (0.5% to 2.5%)

HELOC vs. Home Equity Loan

A HELOC is a revolving line of credit (like a credit card) during the draw period, then converts to an amortized payment. A home equity loan is a one-time lump sum with a fixed rate. See our full comparison →

Frequently Asked Questions

How much can I borrow with a HELOC?

Most lenders allow a combined loan-to-value (CLTV) of 80% to 85%. If your home is worth $400,000 and you owe $250,000, at 85% CLTV you could borrow up to $90,000 (85% × $400,000 = $340,000; $340,000 − $250,000 = $90,000).

What credit score do I need for a HELOC?

Most lenders require a score of at least 620, and prefer 680+ for the best rates. Scores above 760 typically receive the lowest margins (Prime + 0.5%).

Are HELOC interest payments tax-deductible?

Interest may be deductible if funds are used to buy, build, or substantially improve the home securing the loan. Other uses (debt consolidation, vacations) generally do not qualify. Consult a tax professional. IRS Publication 936 has the details.

Is a HELOC the same as a home equity loan?

No. A HELOC is a revolving line with variable rates. A home equity loan is a fixed lump sum with a fixed rate. See our detailed comparison.
