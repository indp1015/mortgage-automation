VA Loans: The 2026 Complete Guide

VA loans are mortgages guaranteed by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs for eligible veterans, active-duty service members, and surviving spouses. In 2026, VA loans remain the most powerful mortgage product available: 0% down payment, no mortgage insurance, and no loan limit with full entitlement.

Down Payment 0%
Mortgage Insurance None
Loan Limit None*
Funding Fee 0-3.3%

What Is a VA Loan?

A VA loan is a mortgage guaranteed by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. The VA does not lend money directly—it guarantees a portion of the loan, which allows private lenders to offer favorable terms.

Because the VA assumes part of the risk, lenders can offer:

  • 0% down payment (for most borrowers)
  • No monthly mortgage insurance
  • No prepayment penalty
  • Competitive interest rates
  • Flexible credit requirements

VA Loan Eligibility (2026)

You may be eligible for a VA loan if you fall into one of these categories:

  • Veterans who served 90+ days active duty during wartime, or 181+ days during peacetime
  • Active-duty service members with 90+ days of continuous active service
  • National Guard and Reserve members with 6+ years of service
  • Surviving spouses of veterans who died in service or from service-related causes
  • Certain U.S. citizens who served in WWII-era allied forces

VA Entitlement

The VA guarantees a portion of your loan (your "entitlement"). As of 2020, full entitlement means you can borrow without a down payment regardless of loan amount, provided your lender approves.

If you have used VA entitlement before (for a prior loan that is still open), your remaining entitlement may be reduced. This may require a down payment in some cases.

VA Funding Fee (2026)

Most VA borrowers pay a one-time funding fee, which can be financed into the loan. The fee varies based on down payment and service history:

Down Payment First Use Subsequent Use
0% 2.15% 3.30%
5-9.99% 1.50% 1.50%
10%+ 1.25% 1.25%

Exempt from funding fee: Veterans with service-connected disabilities, surviving spouses, and active-duty Purple Heart recipients.

How to Use a VA Loan

  1. Get your Certificate of Eligibility (COE) — Apply online through the VA or through your lender
  2. Choose a VA-approved lender — Most major banks and mortgage companies participate
  3. Get pre-approved — Lenders will verify income, credit, and COE
  4. Find a home — Any primary residence qualifies (condos need VA approval)
  5. VA appraisal — Required; ensures property meets VA minimum standards
  6. Close — Funding fee may be financed or paid upfront

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a VA loan more than once?

Yes. VA loans can be reused. If your previous VA loan is paid off, your full entitlement is typically restored. If not, you may use remaining entitlement, which may require a down payment.

Can I use a VA loan to buy a duplex or multi-family home?

Yes, up to 4 units, as long as you occupy one unit as your primary residence.

Do VA loans require a down payment?

No, in most cases with full entitlement. Some situations (reduced entitlement, jumbo loan amounts) may require a down payment.

Are VA loan rates lower than conventional?

VA loan rates are typically 0.25-0.50% lower than conventional rates for the same borrower profile. Combined with no PMI, VA loans often have the lowest total monthly cost.
