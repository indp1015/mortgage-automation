FHA Loans: The 2026 Complete Guide

FHA loans are government-insured mortgages designed for first-time buyers and borrowers with limited credit history. In 2026, FHA loans remain one of the most accessible paths to homeownership—requiring just 3.5% down and accepting credit scores as low as 580.

What Is an FHA Loan?

An FHA loan is a mortgage insured by the Federal Housing Administration, a division of the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD). The FHA does not lend money directly—instead, it insures loans made by approved lenders, protecting them against borrower default.

This insurance allows lenders to offer more flexible terms than they would otherwise: lower credit score minimums, smaller down payments, and higher debt-to-income ratio allowances.

FHA Loan Requirements (2026)

Credit Score

  • 580+: 3.5% minimum down payment
  • 500-579: 10% minimum down payment
  • Below 500: Not eligible for FHA financing

Down Payment

FHA requires at least 3.5% down for borrowers with a 580+ credit score. The down payment can come from savings, a gift from a family member, or an approved down payment assistance program.

Debt-to-Income Ratio

FHA typically allows a DTI up to 43% with compensating factors (higher credit score, larger reserves, longer employment history). Some lenders approve up to 50% with strong compensating factors.

Mortgage Insurance Premium (MIP)

FHA loans require two types of mortgage insurance:

  • Upfront MIP: 1.75% of the loan amount, paid at closing (can be financed)
  • Annual MIP: 0.55% to 0.85% of the loan amount per year, paid monthly

Unlike conventional PMI, FHA MIP typically lasts for the life of the loan if the down payment is less than 10%. With 10%+ down, MIP drops off after 11 years.

FHA is best for: First-time buyers with limited savings, borrowers with credit scores between 580-660, and buyers in markets where conventional loans are harder to qualify for.

Conventional is best for: Borrowers with 700+ credit scores, buyers with 20% down (avoiding PMI), and those purchasing higher- priced homes above FHA limits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I remove FHA mortgage insurance?

If you put down less than 10%, FHA MIP lasts for the life of the loan. The only way to remove it is to refinance into a conventional loan once you have 20% equity.

Can I use FHA for a second home or investment property?

No. FHA loans are for primary residences only. Investment properties and second homes are not eligible.

Does FHA require a home inspection?

FHA requires an FHA appraisal, which includes a health and safety inspection. This is not a full home inspection—buyers should still hire their own inspector.
