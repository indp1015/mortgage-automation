Why California Home Prices Are Falling in 2026

Analysis · Published 2026-10-08 · 7 min read

California's housing market, long a symbol of relentless price growth, has shown signs of cooling. Recent FHFA data shows year-over-year declines in several major metros. Here's a data-driven look at what's happening.

The Data Snapshot

According to the latest FHFA House Price Index, California's statewide HPI shows a modest year-over-year decline. This follows a strong run from 2020-2022 that saw prices rise 30%+ in some counties.

What's Driving the Cooling

Several factors contribute to the shift:

1. Higher Mortgage Rates

From historic lows of 2.65% in 2021 to 6-7% in 2024-2025, mortgage rates have roughly doubled. On a $700,000 home, this adds over $1,200/month to the payment—crushing affordability for many buyers.

2. Outmigration

California has seen net domestic outmigration for several years, with residents moving to Texas, Arizona, Nevada, and Florida. This reduces housing demand, particularly for expensive coastal markets.

3. Tech Sector Layoffs

The Bay Area's tech-heavy economy has seen significant layoffs since 2022. Reduced employment reduces demand for high-end housing.

4. Insurance Crisis

Wildfire risk has led many insurers to exit California or dramatically raise premiums. In some markets, insurance costs have tripled, making ownership more expensive even as prices fall.

5. State Policy Shifts

New state laws (SB 9, SB 10, ADUs) have increased housing supply in some markets, and property tax rules (Prop 19) have changed inheritance incentives.

Which Counties Are Most Affected?

The cooling isn't uniform. Based on FHFA and ACS data:

  • Bay Area: Significant declines, especially in tech-heavy Santa Clara, San Mateo, and San Francisco
  • Coastal SoCal: Moderate declines in LA and Orange County
  • Inland Empire: More stable, benefiting from coastal outmigration
  • Central Valley: Holding up better, still affordable by state standards

Is This a Buying Opportunity?

For buyers, the cooling is welcome news after years of brutal competition. But several factors argue for caution:

  • Insurance: Verify insurability before making an offer
  • Property taxes: California's 1% base rate plus assessments can reach 1.5-2%
  • Interest rates: Even at lower prices, high rates make payments expensive
  • Time horizon: If you're staying 10+ years, buying can make sense
  • Alternative markets: Consider whether inland counties or out-of-state options offer better value

The Broader Picture

California's experience mirrors a national trend: affordability has declined sharply since 2021, and markets are adjusting. The pace of correction varies by region, but the era of double-digit annual appreciation appears to be over—at least for now.

The Bottom Line

California home prices are cooling after a historic boom, driven by high rates, outmigration, and insurance challenges. For buyers, this is a more balanced market than any since 2019. For sellers, price expectations may need to reset. The trend is real, but its depth and duration remain uncertain.
