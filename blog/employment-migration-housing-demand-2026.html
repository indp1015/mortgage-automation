How Employment and Migration Data Predict Local Housing Demand

Economy · Published 2026-10-10 · 8 min read

Most housing market analysis focuses on prices—median home values, year-over-year appreciation, price-to-rent ratios. But prices are a lagging indicator. By the time prices rise, the demand shift has already happened. To spot emerging markets early, you need to look at the underlying drivers: employment and migration.

Why Jobs and Migration Lead Prices

Housing demand comes from people who need a place to live, and people move where the jobs are. When a county adds jobs and attracts new residents, housing demand rises. If supply doesn't keep up, prices rise. This sequence typically plays out over 1-3 years.

By tracking employment and migration before prices shift, you can identify markets at the start of a growth cycle—long before the price data catches up.

Employment Data: BLS QCEW

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes the Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages (QCEW), a comprehensive count of jobs and wages at the county level. Two metrics matter most:

  • Total Employment: Number of private-sector jobs in the county
  • Average Weekly Wage: Average earnings per worker

Rising employment with rising wages creates household formation, which drives housing demand. Falling employment precedes housing weakness.

Example: A Tale of Two Counties

Consider two counties with similar median home values of $300,000:

  • County A: Employment +4% YoY, wages +6% YoY
  • County B: Employment -2% YoY, wages flat

The price data might look identical today. But within 2 years, County A is likely to see rising prices, while County B may face declines.

Migration Data: IRS Statistics of Income

The IRS publishes county-to-county migration data derived from individual tax returns. It shows how many households moved into and out of each county, along with their average income. This is not an estimate—it's actual migration, with a 2-year lag.

Key metrics:

  • Net Migration: Inflow minus outflow (positive = growing)
  • Inflow Average Income: Earnings of new residents
  • Outflow Average Income: Earnings of departing residents

What High-Income Inflow Signals

When a county attracts new residents with average incomes higher than the county median, it signals:

  1. Rising local spending power
  2. Pressure on mid-to-high-end housing
  3. Likely future price appreciation

Conversely, if departing residents earn more than incoming ones, the county may face downward pressure on housing prices.

Combining Employment and Migration

The strongest signal comes from combining both datasets:

EmploymentMigrationSignal
RisingNet inflow🚀 Strong growth market
RisingNet outflow⚠️ Commuter market, watch closely
FallingNet inflow🏠 Affordable destination, retirees
FallingNet outflow❌ Weak market, caution

Case Study: The Southeast U.S. (2020-2024)

Between 2020 and 2024, several Southeastern counties showed strong employment growth combined with high-income migration inflow. Counties in Tennessee, the Carolinas, and Florida saw:

  • Employment growth of 3-8% annually
  • Net migration of thousands of households
  • Median home values rising 40-60% over 5 years

Meanwhile, several coastal California counties showed the opposite pattern: falling employment in tech-related sectors and net outflow to other states. Their FHFA HPI data confirmed price softening starting in 2023.

How to Use This on US Housing Data

Every county page on our site now shows:

  • Employment data from the latest BLS QCEW release
  • Net migration and income data from IRS Statistics of Income
  • Housing supply from Census Building Permits Survey

Look for counties where employment is growing, migration is positive, and new housing supply is limited. These are the markets most likely to see price appreciation.

Limitations to Keep in Mind

  • Data lag: IRS migration data lags by about 2 years; BLS by about 6 months
  • Not predictive on its own: These indicators show probability, not certainty
  • Local context matters: A single large employer opening or closing can skew county data
  • Wage data can be skewed: A few high earners can distort averages

The Bottom Line

Employment and migration data are among the most reliable leading indicators of housing demand. By tracking them alongside price data, you can identify emerging markets before prices reflect the shift. For detailed data on any U.S. county, explore our county rankings and individual county pages.
