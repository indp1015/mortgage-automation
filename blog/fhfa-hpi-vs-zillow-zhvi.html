FHFA HPI vs. Zillow ZHVI: Which Housing Index Should You Trust?

Comparison · Published 2026-09-29 · 6 min read

Two of the most cited U.S. home price indices—the FHFA House Price Index and the Zillow Home Value Index—often tell different stories. Understanding why helps you interpret headlines and make better decisions.

FHFA House Price Index (HPI)

The FHFA HPI is published by the Federal Housing Finance Agency and is based on actual mortgage transactions from Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac.

  • Method: Repeat-sales index measuring price changes on the same homes over time
  • Coverage: National, state, and county level (about 2,800 counties)
  • Frequency: Quarterly
  • Sample: Conforming loans only (under Fannie/Freddie limits)

Zillow Home Value Index (ZHVI)

The Zillow ZHVI is a smoothed, seasonally adjusted measure of typical home values, based on Zillow's proprietary valuation models.

  • Method: Median estimated home value across all properties in an area
  • Coverage: National, state, metro, county, ZIP, and neighborhood
  • Frequency: Monthly
  • Sample: All homes, regardless of financing

Key Differences

Here's how they diverge in practice:

  1. Sample selection: FHFA uses only conforming loans (typically < $766,550 in 2024). Zillow uses all homes. In high-cost markets where jumbo loans dominate, FHFA may miss a large share of transactions.
  2. Price measurement: FHFA compares the sale price of the same home over time, removing composition bias. Zillow estimates what every home is worth, including homes that haven't sold recently.
  3. Timing: FHFA is quarterly and lags by 1-2 months. Zillow is monthly and lags by ~2 weeks.
  4. Revision behavior: FHFA revises historical data infrequently. Zillow revises monthly as new data arrives.

Why They Sometimes Disagree

During 2020-2022, the two indices diverged significantly in some markets. This happens when:

  • Jumbo loans dominate: Coastal markets often see FHFA missing high-end transactions, understating price growth.
  • New construction: Zillow includes new builds; FHFA focuses on repeat sales.
  • Model assumptions: Zillow's valuation model can shift with changes in their algorithm.

Which Should You Use?

It depends on your goal:

  • Long-term trends: FHFA is highly reliable because it's based on verified Repeat-Sales transactions.
  • Recent momentum: Zillow updates monthly and offers granular neighborhood-level signals.
  • Cross-county comparison: FHFA provides strong consistency across standard county boundaries.
  • Data Integration: Public domain data like FHFA serves as a transparent baseline, while private datasets add high-frequency depth.

Practical Advice

Never rely on a single index. When you see a headline saying 'home prices rose 5%,' check:

  1. Which index is being cited
  2. What time period it covers
  3. Whether the market has unusual financing patterns
  4. What the long-term trend looks like from multiple sources

Two indices that disagree by 2-3 percentage points are both probably right—they're just measuring slightly different things.

The Bottom Line

FHFA and Zillow are both useful, but for different purposes. For structural analysis and long-term trends, FHFA offers a solid baseline. For recent momentum and neighborhood-level detail, private indices excel. As our site grows, we look forward to bringing both perspectives directly into your housing market research.
