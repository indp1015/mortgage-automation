📝 Mortgage Market Notes

In Yolo County, the median home value of $620,700 presents a distinct financing landscape where loan limits significantly influence buyer options. The FHA limit for a one-unit property stands at $541,287, covering only 87% of the median price. Consequently, purchasing a typical median-priced home with an FHA loan is not feasible without exceeding the limit, necessitating a shift to conventional financing. The conforming limit of $832,750 covers 134% of the median value, ensuring that standard conventional loans can accommodate the average property without requiring jumbo loan structures. This coverage gap effectively excludes FHA financing for the majority of the market, positioning conventional mortgages as the primary vehicle for homeownership in the region. The affordability burden is substantial, driven by the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%. For a median-priced home, a conventional loan with 20% down payment results in a principal and interest payment of $3,438 per month. This amount represents 45.0% of the median household income of $91,752. Even with a 10% down payment, the monthly obligation rises to $3,867, consuming nearly half of typical earnings. These figures exceed the standard 30% debt-to-income threshold recommended by most lenders, indicating that the median household in Yolo County faces significant challenges in qualifying for a mortgage on a median-priced home. The high interest rate amplifies this strain compared to lower-rate environments, where the same loan would carry a materially lower monthly cost. Down payment requirements further complicate entry into the market. A 20% down payment on a $620,700 home requires $124,140 in liquid savings, a sum that is difficult for many households to accumulate given the local income levels. While lower down payment options exist, they increase the monthly payment burden, exacerbating affordability issues. The five-year price CAGR of 10.9% suggests a rapidly appreciating market, which may outpace income growth. For prospective buyers, the practical takeaway is that purchasing at the median price likely requires income well above the county median or a substantial down payment to reduce the loan amount. Buyers should consider properties priced closer to the FHA limit or explore shared equity programs to improve their debt-to-income ratios and secure financing in this high-cost environment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Yolo County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Yolo County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Yolo County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Yolo County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Yolo County qualifies.
