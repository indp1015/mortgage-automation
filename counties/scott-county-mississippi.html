📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Scott County, Mississippi, presents a distinct financing landscape where loan limits significantly exceed local property values. The 2026 FHA one-unit limit of $541,287 covers 507% of the median home value of $106,700, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 780%. Consequently, no typical buyer in this market faces restrictions based on loan ceilings. With a median household income of $48,786, the monthly payment burden remains manageable for most residents. An FHA loan at 3.5% down requires a principal and interest payment of $712, representing 17.5% of median monthly income. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces this to $591, or 14.5% of income, both falling within standard debt-to-income guidelines. Given the low median home price, the FHA option offers the most accessible entry point due to its lower down payment requirement. Buyers need only $3,735 in cash for a 3.5% down payment on the median-priced home, a threshold that is feasible for many local households compared to the $21,340 required for a 20% conventional down payment. While USDA loans may also be viable depending on specific rural zoning, the FHA’s broad availability and lower upfront capital requirements make it the primary recommendation for typical buyers in Scott County. The current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% introduces sensitivity to affordability. Compared to historical lower-rate environments, this rate increases the monthly payment burden by approximately 15-20% relative to rates seen in previous cycles. For instance, a $100,000 loan at 7.40% costs roughly $712 per month, whereas a 5.0% rate would have cost approximately $537. This rate differential highlights the importance of financing structure over loan type alone. The practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that while the absolute cost of entry is low, the high interest rate environment necessitates careful budgeting. Buyers should prioritize securing the lowest possible down payment to preserve liquidity, as the monthly savings from a larger down payment are less significant than the long-term interest savings from a lower rate. However, since the rate is fixed, the focus should shift to minimizing initial cash outlay. With the median home value at $106,700 and strong price appreciation of 8.3% annually, the market remains attractive for equity accumulation, but the 7.40% rate requires buyers to ensure their debt-to-income ratios remain below 43% to maintain financial stability. The combination of high loan limit coverage and moderate price points ensures that financing availability is not the barrier; rather, personal credit and income verification are the primary determinants of access.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Scott County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Scott County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Scott County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Scott County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Scott County qualifies.
