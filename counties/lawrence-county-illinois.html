📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA loan limit for a one‑unit property in Lawrence County ($541,287) is **546 % of the median home price** ($99,100), while the conforming ceiling ($832,750) is **840 %** of that price. Both limits comfortably exceed the typical purchase price, confirming that virtually any buyer in the county can obtain a loan that is not constrained by statutory caps. Consequently, financing coverage is not a barrier for median‑priced homes. At the current 30‑year fixed rate of **7.24 %**, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) component for a median‑priced home is $651 with an FHA loan (3.5 % down) and $540 with a conventional loan (20 % down). The median household income of **$55,324** translates to a monthly gross income of **$4,610**. The FHA payment represents **14.1 %** of gross income, while a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment is **11.7 %** of income. Both ratios fall well below the conventional affordability threshold of 28 % for housing costs, indicating that, despite the high interest rate, the monthly debt service remains manageable for a typical household. Given the low median price relative to loan limits, a **conventional loan with a 10–20 % down payment** is the most cost‑effective option. It avoids mortgage‑insurance premiums required for FHA loans and yields a lower P&I payment. For borrowers lacking the cash for a 10 % down payment, the FHA program provides a viable alternative with only 3.5 % down, though it adds an upfront and annual MIP that modestly raises total cost. The minimum cash outlay to purchase at the median price is **$3,468** (3.5 % down for FHA) or **$9,910** (10 % down conventional). For many local households, the FHA down‑payment requirement is feasible, while the 10 % conventional threshold may be a modest hurdle. The 7.24 % rate inflates monthly payments by roughly $80–$100 compared with a 5 % environment, but the impact is muted because the loan amounts are small relative to national averages. **Practical takeaway:** Prospective buyers in Lawrence County can comfortably finance a median‑priced home with either FHA or conventional financing; the decisive factor is down‑payment availability, where a 3.5 % FHA contribution often unlocks homeownership without compromising affordability.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Lawrence County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Lawrence County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Lawrence County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Lawrence County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Lawrence County qualifies.
