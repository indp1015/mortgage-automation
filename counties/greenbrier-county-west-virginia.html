📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Greenbrier County, West Virginia, presents a highly accessible mortgage landscape where federal loan limits significantly exceed local market valuations. The 2026 FHA limit for a one-unit property is set at $541,287, which covers 363% of the county’s median home value of $149,000. Similarly, the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 559% of the median price. Consequently, virtually all residential properties in the county fall well below these thresholds, eliminating high-cost loan restrictions and ensuring that standard financing products are universally applicable to the typical buyer. This ample coverage means that loan type selection is driven primarily by credit profile and down payment capability rather than price ceilings. The primary constraint on affordability is the current interest rate environment. At a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, the principal and interest payment for a median-priced home using an FHA loan with 3.5% down is approximately $995 per month. This figure represents 24.8% of the median household income of $48,218. While this ratio is within the standard 28% debt-to-income guideline, it leaves limited margin for other housing costs such as taxes, insurance, and maintenance. In contrast, a conventional loan with 20% down reduces the monthly P&I to $825, or 20.5% of income, offering greater financial flexibility. The sharp 9.3% year-over-year price increase in 2024 has outpaced income growth, making the high interest rate the dominant factor in payment stress rather than the home price itself. For typical buyers, the FHA loan remains the most practical entry point due to its low 3.5% down payment requirement, which amounts to roughly $5,215 for a median-priced home. This barrier is significantly lower than the $29,800 required for a 20% conventional down payment. Given that the median home value is well within FHA limits, buyers with credit scores above 580 can access this financing without needing to save for a substantial initial equity stake. However, the higher monthly payment associated with FHA loans compared to high-down conventional options requires careful budgeting. Prospective buyers should prioritize securing a rate lock or exploring fixed-rate options with lower points if available, as the 7.40% rate significantly impacts long-term total cost. The data indicates that while entry into homeownership is feasible, the margin for error in monthly budgeting is tight, necessitating strict adherence to debt-to-income limits to ensure long-term sustainability in this appreciating market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Greenbrier County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Greenbrier County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Greenbrier County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Greenbrier County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Greenbrier County qualifies.
