📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Iron County, Utah, presents a financing landscape where standard loan limits offer substantial coverage relative to local property values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 143% of the median home value of $379,000, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 220%. Consequently, no buyer purchasing at the median price faces a high-cost county restriction or the need for a jumbo loan. This ample headroom ensures that typical transactions remain within standard agency guidelines, simplifying the underwriting process for both lenders and borrowers in this non-high-cost jurisdiction. However, the affordability burden is significant due to the combination of rising home values and elevated interest rates. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, a median-priced home financed with an FHA loan requires a monthly principal and interest payment of $2,532. This figure represents 45.9% of the median household income of $66,247, far exceeding the conventional 28-30% debt-to-income threshold typically preferred by underwriters. Even with a 20% down payment on a conventional loan, the monthly payment remains $2,099, or 38.0% of median income. This high payment-to-income ratio indicates that the median household in Iron County is currently stretched to the limit of its borrowing capacity, making qualification challenging without supplemental income or lower debt obligations. For typical buyers, the FHA loan likely offers the most accessible entry point due to its lower down payment requirement of 3.5%, amounting to approximately $13,265. While this is a lower barrier than the 20% conventional requirement of $75,800, it still represents a substantial upfront cost. The recent 15.7% five-year price CAGR has outpaced income growth, creating a gap that higher interest rates have widened. The current 7.40% rate significantly reduces purchasing power compared to the sub-4% environments of previous years, effectively increasing the required income by nearly 20% for the same loan amount. Prospective buyers should focus on reducing other monthly debt obligations before applying, as the primary constraint in Iron County is not loan limit availability, but rather the debt-to-income ratio imposed by high monthly payments relative to local median earnings.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Iron County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Iron County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Iron County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Iron County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Iron County qualifies.
