📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Campbell County, Kentucky, presents a favorable financing landscape where loan limits significantly exceed local market values. The 2026 FHA single-family limit of $541,287 covers 215% of the $252,000 median home value, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 330%. Consequently, virtually all residential transactions in the county fall well within standard loan caps, eliminating the need for jumbo financing for typical buyers. This ample coverage ensures that creditworthy applicants have access to diverse lending options without facing high-cost county restrictions. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels, though the current 7.40% interest rate exerts noticeable pressure. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with 3.5% down results in a principal and interest payment of $1,683, equating to 26.0% of the $77,567 median household income. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces this to $1,395, or 21.6% of income. While these ratios are within the 28% guideline for front-end debt-to-income ratios, they leave limited room for other monthly obligations. The significant price appreciation, with a five-year CAGR of 9.4%, has outpaced income growth, making entry-level affordability tighter than in prior years. Given the low median price point, conventional financing is likely the optimal choice for buyers with sufficient savings, as it avoids FHA mortgage insurance premiums. However, for first-time buyers with limited capital, FHA loans provide a critical entry point, requiring only $8,820 in down payment for the median home. This barrier is feasible for households saving consistently, though it remains a hurdle for those with minimal assets. The current interest rate environment significantly impacts affordability; at 7.40%, monthly payments are approximately 20% higher than they would have been at a 6.0% rate. Prospective buyers should prioritize building down payment reserves to reduce loan principal, as this offers a more immediate reduction in monthly costs than waiting for potential rate adjustments. Ultimately, Campbell County remains accessible for qualified buyers, provided they structure their financing to mitigate the impact of elevated interest rates on long-term debt service.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Campbell County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Campbell County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Campbell County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Campbell County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Campbell County qualifies.
