📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Chatham County, Georgia, presents a housing market where federal loan limits provide substantial coverage relative to local median home values. The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home stands at $541,287, which is 179% of the county’s median home value of $302,700. Similarly, the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 275% of the median price. Consequently, no typical buyer in this market faces a financing cap barrier. The median household income of $71,097 supports these transactions, though the high 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% significantly impacts monthly obligations. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with 3.5% down requires a principal and interest payment of $2,022, consuming 34.1% of median monthly income. This ratio approaches the upper threshold of conventional debt-to-income guidelines, suggesting that while financing is available, the payment burden is tight for average-income households. Conventional financing offers a more favorable payment structure for buyers with sufficient liquidity. With a 20% down payment, the monthly P&I payment drops to $1,676, representing 28.3% of median income. This lower ratio provides greater financial cushion and aligns better with standard underwriting preferences. However, the initial capital requirement remains a significant hurdle. An FHA buyer needs approximately $10,595 in cash for the 3.5% down payment, while a conventional buyer with 20% down requires $60,540. Given the median income level, accumulating the latter sum is a considerable barrier, making lower-down-payment options more accessible for first-time buyers. The 11.2% five-year price CAGR indicates rapid appreciation, which has outpaced income growth, further straining affordability. The current interest rate environment is the primary driver of reduced affordability compared to historical norms. At 7.40%, monthly payments are notably higher than they would be in a sub-5% rate environment, effectively reducing purchasing power. For prospective buyers, the practical takeaway is clear: those with limited savings should prioritize FHA or VA loans to minimize upfront cash requirements, accepting the higher monthly payment burden. Conversely, buyers with substantial reserves should consider conventional loans with 20% down to avoid private mortgage insurance and reduce total monthly outlays. In either scenario, buyers must ensure that their total debt-to-income ratio, including the 34.1% or 28.3% housing expense, remains within lender-specific limits to secure approval in this high-rate, high-appreciation market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Chatham County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Chatham County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Chatham County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Chatham County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Chatham County qualifies.
