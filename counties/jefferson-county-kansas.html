📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in Jefferson County, Kansas, is characterized by substantial loan limit coverage relative to local property values. The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home stands at $541,287, which is 250% of the median home value of $216,900. Similarly, the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 384% of the median price. Consequently, the typical buyer in this market will not encounter loan limit restrictions, as the median home value falls well within both FHA and conventional thresholds. This ample coverage ensures that financing availability is not a primary constraint for standard residential transactions in the county. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable for the median household, which earns $81,278 annually. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, a conventional loan with 20% down payment on a median-priced home results in a principal and interest payment of $1,201. This represents 17.7% of the median monthly income, a figure comfortably within standard debt-to-income guidelines. Even with an FHA loan requiring only 3.5% down, the payment rises to $1,449, or 21.4% of income. While higher, this ratio remains within acceptable limits for qualified borrowers. The significant price appreciation of 9.8% annually over the last five years has increased home values, but the corresponding income levels have maintained affordability ratios. For the typical buyer, conventional financing appears to be the optimal choice given the low median home price and high owner-occupancy rate of 85.7%. Conventional loans generally offer lower interest rates and no mortgage insurance premiums compared to FHA options, making them more cost-effective for buyers with sufficient down payment funds. The minimum down payment barrier is relatively low; an FHA buyer needs only $7,592 (3.5% of $216,900), while a conventional buyer with 5% down requires $10,845. These amounts are feasible for many households, particularly given the median household income. However, the current 7.40% interest rate significantly impacts total cost compared to historical lower-rate environments, increasing the total interest paid over the life of the loan. Prospective buyers should focus on securing the lowest possible rate and consider making a larger down payment to reduce the principal balance, thereby mitigating the impact of high interest rates on long-term affordability.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Jefferson County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Jefferson County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Jefferson County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Jefferson County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Jefferson County qualifies.
