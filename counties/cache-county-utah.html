📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Cache County, Utah, presents a distinct financing landscape where loan limits significantly exceed local market values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 122% of the $444,400 median home value, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 187%. This ample coverage ensures that typical buyers in the county are not restricted by maximum loan amounts, allowing full access to both government-backed and conventional financing options for median-priced properties. Consequently, the primary barrier to entry is not loan cap eligibility, but rather the cost of capital and down payment requirements. The monthly payment burden remains substantial relative to local income levels. With the 30-year fixed rate at 7.40%, an FHA loan on the median home requires a 3.5% down payment, resulting in a principal and interest payment of $2,969. This figure consumes 43.6% of the $81,665 median household income, a ratio that exceeds standard underwriting guidelines for most borrowers. Even with a 20% conventional down payment, the payment of $2,461 represents 36.2% of median income. Given the rapid appreciation, with a 17.0% five-year CAGR and a 13.1% increase in 2024, the gap between income and housing costs has widened considerably. The current interest rate environment significantly impacts affordability; compared to lower-rate periods, the 7.40% rate increases the monthly payment burden by hundreds of dollars, effectively reducing the maximum affordable loan size for a typical household. For most buyers, conventional financing with a 20% down payment offers the most favorable payment structure, though it requires $88,880 in liquidity. FHA loans remain accessible for those with limited savings, as the minimum down payment of $15,554 is more attainable, although the resulting higher monthly obligation strains budgets. VA and USDA loans may offer zero-down advantages for eligible veterans or rural residents, potentially improving cash flow, but their availability depends on specific property eligibility and borrower status. The practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that income level is the critical determinant of affordability. To maintain a sustainable debt-to-income ratio, buyers should target homes priced below the current median or secure higher income verification, as median-priced properties currently demand a disproportionate share of household earnings under prevailing interest rates.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Cache County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Cache County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Cache County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Cache County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Cache County qualifies.
