📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in Gray County, Texas, is characterized by significant excess loan capacity relative to local home values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 526% of the median home value of $102,900, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 809%. This indicates that loan limits are not a restrictive factor for typical buyers in this market. Consequently, the choice of loan type is driven more by eligibility criteria and down payment requirements than by price caps. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable within the context of local income levels. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with 3.5% down results in a principal and interest payment of $687 per month. This represents 13.8% of the median monthly household income, derived from an annual median income of $59,614. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces the payment to $569, or 11.5% of income. These ratios are well below the standard 28% front-end debt-to-income threshold, suggesting that debt service is not a primary barrier to entry for median-income households. Regarding loan type recommendations, FHA loans offer the most accessible entry point due to the low 3.5% minimum down payment requirement. The required down payment for a median-priced home is approximately $3,602. While this amount is modest in absolute terms, it still represents a significant upfront capital requirement for first-time buyers with limited savings. Conventional loans, requiring 10% to 20% down, demand $10,290 to $20,580, which may be prohibitive for many local residents. USDA loans may also be viable if the property is in an eligible rural area, potentially offering zero-down options, though specific property eligibility must be verified. The current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% significantly impacts affordability compared to historical low-rate environments. At this rate, the monthly payment on a $99,298 FHA loan is substantially higher than it would be at rates below 5%. However, the rapid appreciation of home values, with a 5-year CAGR of 7.5%, has outpaced the increase in interest rates. The median home value grew from $71,700 in 2017 to $102,900 in 2024, a 43.5% increase. This appreciation reduces the loan-to-value ratio for new buyers, improving their equity position from day one. A practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that while interest rates remain elevated, the low absolute price points in Gray County mitigate the impact of high rates. Buyers should prioritize FHA or USDA financing to minimize initial cash outlay, as the low down payment requirements align better with local income levels. The combination of low home prices and manageable debt-to-income ratios suggests that affordability remains robust despite the current interest rate environment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Gray County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Gray County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Gray County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Gray County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Gray County qualifies.
