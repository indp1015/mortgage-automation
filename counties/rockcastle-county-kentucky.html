📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA and conforming loan limits both sit far above the median home price in Rockcastle County. The FHA 1‑unit ceiling of $541,287 is **438 %** of the $123,600 median value, while the conforming limit of $832,750 is **674 %** of that price, confirming that a typical home is comfortably within either program’s maximum loan size. At the current 30‑year fixed rate of **7.24 %**, a median‑priced home financed with an FHA loan (3.5 % down, $119,274 loan) generates a principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment of **$812 per month**. That amount equals **19.9 %** of the county’s median household income ($48,862 annual or $4,072 monthly). A conventional loan with a 20 % down payment ($98,880 loan) reduces P&I to **$673 per month**, or **16.5 %** of monthly income. Both ratios fall well under the conventional affordability threshold of 28 % for housing costs, indicating that, despite the high interest rate, mortgage service remains manageable for median‑income families. Given the low median price relative to loan limits, conventional financing with a 20 % down payment offers the lowest monthly burden and avoids mortgage‑insurance premiums inherent to FHA loans. However, the required **$24,720 down payment** (20 % of $123,600) may be prohibitive for many local buyers, especially when the median household net savings are limited. An FHA loan’s 3.5 % down payment ($4,326) is far more attainable, albeit with added insurance costs. USDA or VA loans could be viable for eligible rural or veteran purchasers, providing zero‑down options and competitive rates that further reduce the monthly outlay. Rate sensitivity analysis shows that a 1 % drop in the 30‑year rate would lower the conventional 20 % P&I payment by roughly **$70 per month**, while the FHA payment would decline by about **$80**. In a lower‑rate environment, the affordability gap between FHA and conventional narrows, making conventional loans even more attractive. **Practical takeaway:** For most median‑income buyers in Rockcastle County, an FHA loan with a modest 3.5 % down payment is the most realistic path to homeownership today; those who can secure the 20 % down payment should prefer a conventional loan to minimize total monthly costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Rockcastle County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Rockcastle County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Rockcastle County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Rockcastle County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Rockcastle County qualifies.
