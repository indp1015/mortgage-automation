Mortgage Guide: King County, Washington

In King County, Washington, the 2026 FHA loan limit for a single-family home is $691,437, and the conforming loan limit is $1,063,750. The median home value in this county is $859,900, and median household income is $124,746.

📝 Mortgage Market Notes

King County’s mortgage financing landscape presents significant structural challenges for median-income households. The 2026 FHA loan limit of $691,437 covers only 80% of the median home value of $859,900, creating a coverage gap that forces buyers to seek conforming or jumbo financing. While the conforming limit of $1,063,750 adequately covers 124% of the median price, the resulting loan structures impose substantial monthly burdens. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, a conventional loan with 20% down requires a principal and interest payment of $4,763. This amount consumes 45.8% of the median household income of $124,746, a ratio that exceeds standard debt-to-income guidelines for most lenders. Even with a 10% down payment, the payment rises to $5,358, further straining household budgets. The primary barrier to entry is the down payment requirement. To secure a conforming loan at the median price, buyers must provide $171,980 in cash for a 20% down payment, or at least $85,990 for a 10% down payment. While FHA loans offer a lower 3.5% down payment threshold, the median home price exceeds the FHA limit, meaning a standard FHA loan cannot finance the full purchase without a second mortgage or a higher down payment to bring the loan amount under the cap. This effectively eliminates the lowest-down-payment option for typical buyers, making conventional financing the most viable path despite its higher capital requirements. Rate sensitivity significantly impacts affordability in this high-cost environment. The current 7.40% rate amplifies the cost of leverage compared to historical lows; a drop to 6.0% would reduce the 20% down conventional payment by approximately $500 monthly, improving the debt-to-income ratio to a more manageable level. However, at current rates, the financial strain is acute. The practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that purchasing at the median price requires substantial liquidity reserves. Buyers should prioritize saving for a 20% down payment to avoid private mortgage insurance and ensure their debt-to-income ratio remains within lender approval thresholds, as the combination of high home values and elevated interest rates leaves little margin for error in monthly budgeting.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in King County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in King County is $691,437. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in King County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,063,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in King County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm King County qualifies.
