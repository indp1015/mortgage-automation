📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA 1‑Unit limit of $541,287 and the conforming 1‑Unit limit of $832,750 both exceed the median home value of $213,700, meaning the typical property is comfortably within financing coverage; the FHA limit covers 253% of the median price while the conforming limit covers 390%. The median household income of $76,382 translates to $6,365 per month, and the estimated FHA payment of $1,427 represents 22.4% of that income, while the conventional 20% down payment of $1,183 equals 18.6% of income. Given that the median price is well under both limits, a conventional loan with 10% down (requiring $21,370) offers a balanced approach, keeping the payment at $1,331 (17.5% of income) while avoiding FHA mortgage insurance premiums. Down payment barriers are 3.5% ($7,480) for FHA, 10% ($21,370) for conventional 10% down, and 20% ($42,740) for conventional 20% down; the 3.5% down payment is attainable, while the 20% requirement is prohibitive for typical households. At the current 7.40% rate, the FHA payment represents 22.4% of median monthly income, which strains affordability; lower rates would proportionally reduce the payment, improving feasibility. The 8.4% rise in median home value from 2023 to 2024 means buyers must accumulate larger down payments to maintain loan‑to‑value ratios, making the 10% down option more critical than in a flat‑price market. Additionally, a 0.5% reduction in the interest rate would lower the FHA payment to about $1,350 per month, cutting the income share to roughly 21.2%, and a 1% drop would bring the payment to about $1,250, or 19.5% of income, highlighting the effect of rate changes on affordability. If the rate were to fall to 5.5%, the FHA payment would drop to roughly $1,200 per month, reducing the income share to about 18.8% and making the purchase more attainable. Practical takeaway: prospective buyers should target a 10% down conventional loan to stay within conforming limits while keeping the monthly payment under 20% of income, thereby optimizing affordability and preserving cash reserves.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Monroe County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Monroe County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Monroe County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Monroe County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Monroe County qualifies.
