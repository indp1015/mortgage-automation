📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Multnomah County’s mortgage landscape presents a distinct mismatch between available loan limits and median home values. The 2026 FHA limit for a one-unit property stands at $541,287, covering approximately 98% of the $552,700 median home value. Consequently, the median-priced home technically exceeds the FHA ceiling, forcing buyers to utilize conventional financing or seek jumbo loans for higher-priced properties. In contrast, the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 151% of the median value, ensuring ample coverage for standard transactions. This structural gap means that while FHA loans remain an option for homes near the lower end of the market, the typical buyer must rely on conventional products, which generally require higher credit scores and larger down payments than FHA alternatives. The monthly payment burden remains significant relative to local income levels. At a current 7.40% interest rate, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment on the median home results in a principal and interest payment of $3,061. This figure represents 41.4% of the median monthly household income, derived from an annual income of $88,766. For buyers opting for lower down payments, such as the 10% conventional option, the payment rises to $3,444, or 46.7% of monthly income. These ratios exceed the traditional 28% debt-to-income guideline often recommended by lenders, indicating that median-income households face substantial affordability constraints. The high cost of borrowing amplifies this pressure; compared to lower-rate environments, the 7.40% rate significantly increases monthly obligations, reducing the maximum loan amount a borrower can sustainably support. Given these dynamics, conventional financing is the most viable path for typical buyers, as it aligns with the conforming limit and avoids the FHA cap issues. However, the minimum down payment barrier remains a critical hurdle. A 20% down payment on the median home requires $110,540 in cash, a sum that is difficult for median-income households to accumulate without assistance programs. Even a 10% down payment requires $55,270. Prospective buyers should prioritize increasing their down payment reserves or exploring local down payment assistance programs to reduce the loan-to-value ratio. By lowering the loan amount, buyers can mitigate the impact of high interest rates and improve their debt-to-income ratios, making approval more likely in this competitive, high-cost market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Multnomah County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Multnomah County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Multnomah County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Multnomah County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Multnomah County qualifies.
