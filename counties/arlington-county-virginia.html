📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Arlington County’s housing market presents significant financing constraints for typical buyers, primarily due to the divergence between median home values and federal loan limits. The 2026 FHA limit for a one-unit property is set at $811,931, which covers only 91% of the $895,000 median home value. Consequently, the median-priced home exceeds the FHA ceiling, forcing most purchasers to seek conforming loans. The conforming limit of $1,249,125 comfortably covers 140% of the median price, ensuring that standard financing options remain available without requiring jumbo loans. This structural gap suggests that while FHA financing is accessible for lower-priced properties, the typical Arlington transaction necessitates conventional financing to maintain full price coverage. The monthly payment burden remains elevated relative to local income levels. At a current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, a median-priced home financed with a 20% down payment results in a principal and interest payment of $4,957. This figure represents 41.9% of the median monthly household income of $142,114. For buyers utilizing FHA financing with a 3.5% down payment, the payment rises to $5,979, consuming 50.5% of median income. These ratios exceed the standard 28-36% debt-to-income thresholds typically preferred by lenders, indicating that median-income households face substantial affordability challenges. The high cost of capital at 7.40% significantly amplifies these payments compared to lower-rate environments, making the entry barrier more rigid. Conventional loans are the most suitable financing vehicle for the typical Arlington buyer, given the median home price exceeds FHA limits. The minimum down payment for a conforming loan is generally 5% to 10%, requiring $44,750 to $89,500 at the median price. While feasible for high-income households, this capital requirement poses a barrier for those near the median income level. The low owner-occupancy rate of 41.3% reflects this tension between high property values and financing costs. Prospective buyers should prioritize increasing down payment reserves to reduce loan-to-value ratios, thereby improving qualification odds and lowering total interest costs in this high-cost, high-rate environment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Arlington County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Arlington County is $1,249,125. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Arlington County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,249,125 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Arlington County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Arlington County qualifies.
