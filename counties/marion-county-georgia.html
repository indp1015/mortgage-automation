📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Marion County's financing limits provide substantial coverage for the local market. The FHA 1-unit limit of $541,287 exceeds the median home value of $154,100 by 351%, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 540% of median price, eliminating loan-limit constraints for virtually all buyers. At current rates, monthly principal and interest payments consume a moderate share of income: an FHA loan with 3.5% down ($148,706 loan) requires $1,029 monthly, representing 23.9% of the $4,306 median monthly household income. A conventional loan with 20% down ($123,280 loan) reduces the payment to $853, or 19.8% of income—well within standard 28% front-end DTI thresholds. Given the rural character (population 7,509) and 78.9% owner-occupancy rate, USDA loans merit primary consideration for eligible buyers, offering 100% financing with no down payment and reduced mortgage insurance costs. FHA remains a strong alternative for buyers with credit scores below 640 or higher debt loads, requiring only $5,394 down at 3.5%. Conventional financing becomes advantageous for buyers with 20% down ($30,820), eliminating mortgage insurance entirely. The minimum barrier to entry is effectively zero with USDA, or $5,394 with FHA—feasible for many households given the area's income levels. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Marion County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Marion County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Marion County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Marion County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Marion County qualifies.
