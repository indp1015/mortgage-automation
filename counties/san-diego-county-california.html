📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA and conforming loan limits in San Diego County ($1,104,000 for a one‑unit residence) exceed the 2024 median home value of $854,700 by roughly 29 %, meaning both loan programs can fully finance a typical purchase without requiring a jumbo loan. Consequently, the median‑priced home is comfortably within the high‑cost‑county limit, and lenders can offer conventional or FHA financing at the standard limit rather than resorting to higher‑priced non‑conforming products. At a 30‑year fixed rate of 7.18 %, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment on a conventional loan with 20 % down ($683,760 loan) is $4,632 per month, representing 52.3 % of the median household income ($106,268 / 12 = $8,856). The FHA scenario with a 3.5 % down payment ($824,786 loan) yields a $5,587 P&I payment, or 63.1 % of income. Both ratios exceed the conventional affordability benchmark of 28‑30 % of gross monthly income, indicating a substantial payment burden for an average household. The higher down‑payment option reduces the burden by roughly 11 percentage points, underscoring the importance of equity. Given the price‑to‑limit relationship and the income‑to‑payment ratios, a conventional loan with at least a 20 % down payment is the most suitable product for typical buyers; it avoids mortgage‑insurance premiums, yields a lower P&I amount, and stays within the conforming limit. FHA financing remains viable for borrowers with limited cash, but the required 3.5 % down ($29,900) still leaves a payment share well above the 30 % threshold, making it less affordable without supplemental income or assistance. The 7.18 % rate inflates monthly costs by roughly $1,200 compared with a 5 % environment, directly widening the income‑payment gap. Buyers who can secure a lower rate through points or discount‑loan programs will improve affordability markedly. **Practical takeaway:** securing a 20 % down payment (approximately $170,900) reduces the monthly principal‑and‑interest obligation to a level that, while still high, aligns more closely with conventional affordability standards and eliminates mortgage‑insurance costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in San Diego County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in San Diego County is $1,104,000. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in San Diego County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,104,000 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in San Diego County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm San Diego County qualifies.
