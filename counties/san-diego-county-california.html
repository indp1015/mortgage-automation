📝 Mortgage Market Notes

San Diego County presents a complex financing landscape where the median home value of $854,700 exceeds the 2026 FHA loan limit of $717,600. Consequently, the FHA limit covers only 84% of the typical property price, rendering it insufficient for standard purchases without significant price adjustments. In contrast, the conforming loan limit of $1,104,000 covers 129% of the median price, providing ample headroom for most transactions. This discrepancy forces typical buyers toward conventional financing, as FHA loans are generally unviable for median-priced homes. The market’s designation as a high-cost county reflects this structural mismatch between federal loan caps and local valuation trends, which have grown at a 12.0% five-year compound annual growth rate. The monthly payment burden for a median-priced home is substantial relative to local incomes. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment requires a principal and interest payment of $4,734. This amount consumes 53.5% of the median household income of $106,268, well above the standard 30% debt-to-income threshold for qualified borrowers. Even with a 10% down payment, the payment rises to $5,325, representing 59.9% of median income. For those considering FHA financing on lower-priced homes, the payment burden reaches 64.5% of income, indicating severe affordability constraints. The current interest rate environment significantly amplifies these costs; compared to historical lower-rate periods, the higher interest expense reduces purchasing power by thousands of dollars annually, effectively lowering the maximum affordable home price for a given income level. Down payment barriers further limit access to homeownership. Purchasing at the median price with a conventional 20% down payment requires $170,940 in cash, a figure that is challenging for households earning the median income. While a 10% down payment reduces the cash requirement to $85,470, it does not alleviate the high monthly payment burden. Given these constraints, conventional loans remain the most appropriate loan type for typical buyers in San Diego, as they align with local price points and limit structures. However, the combination of high home values and elevated interest rates creates a significant affordability gap. A practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that purchasing at the median price requires either substantial cash reserves for down payments or incomes significantly above the median to meet standard qualification metrics. Buyers should prioritize properties priced below $717,600 to access FHA financing, or seek higher-income brackets and larger down payments to qualify for conventional loans at the current 7.40% rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in San Diego County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in San Diego County is $717,600. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in San Diego County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,104,000 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in San Diego County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm San Diego County qualifies.
