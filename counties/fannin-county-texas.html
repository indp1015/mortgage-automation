📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Financing coverage in Fannin County is adequate for the typical home. The Federal Housing Administration’s 1‑unit limit of $541,287 exceeds the 2024 median home value of $233,700 by 232%, and the conforming loan limit of $832,750 exceeds it by 356%, confirming that both programs can underwrite a median‑priced property. Monthly payment burden is significant relative to household income. Using the median‑priced home, a 3.5% down FHA loan on a $225,520 balance requires a principal‑and‑interest payment of $1,561 per month, representing 25.9% of the median household income of $72,295. A conventional loan with 20% down on a $186,960 balance yields a payment of $1,294 per month, or 17.9% of income. Even the 10% down conventional option at $1,456 per month consumes 20.1% of income, indicating that financing costs approach or exceed the commonly recommended 28% threshold for affordable housing. Given the price level and loan limits, an FHA loan is likely the most suitable for typical buyers, as it requires the lowest down payment (3.5%) and stays within the FHA ceiling. Conventional financing becomes attractive only for purchasers able to provide at least 10% down, which translates to $23,370, a barrier for many households. USDA and VA options are not referenced in the data but would similarly depend on eligibility criteria. The minimum down payment to purchase at the median price is 3.5% ($8,180) for FHA, $23,370 for 10% conventional, and $46,740 for 20% conventional. While the 3.5% requirement is generally feasible, it still demands a substantial cash outlay. Current 7.40% interest rates increase affordability pressure; the $1,561 FHA payment is roughly 30% higher than it would be at a 5% rate, reducing the proportion of income available for other expenses. Prospective buyers should prioritize securing an FHA loan to minimize upfront cash needs while monitoring rate trends to assess long‑term payment capacity.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Fannin County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Fannin County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Fannin County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Fannin County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Fannin County qualifies.
