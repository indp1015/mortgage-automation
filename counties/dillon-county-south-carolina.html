📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Dillon County’s housing market is characterized by significant affordability relative to national financing standards, as evidenced by the extreme coverage of loan limits. The 2026 FHA single-family limit of $541,287 covers 634% of the median home value of $85,400, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 975% of that same value. Consequently, no typical buyer in this market faces a barrier due to loan ceiling restrictions. The primary constraint is not the maximum loan amount but rather the monthly debt-to-income ratio. With a median household income of $46,605, the estimated principal and interest payment for an FHA loan at 3.5% down is $570, representing 14.7% of monthly income. This figure remains well within the standard 28% housing expense ratio, indicating that the median-priced home is financially accessible for the average local household without requiring income stretching. Regarding loan type recommendations, the FHA option presents the most accessible entry point for typical buyers due to its low down payment requirement. At the median price of $85,400, the minimum down payment is approximately $2,989. This sum is feasible for many households, particularly when compared to the $17,080 required for a conventional 20% down payment. While USDA loans may also be applicable given the rural nature of the county, the FHA’s broad eligibility and low entry cost make it a strong default recommendation. The current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% significantly impacts total cost of ownership, though the low loan balances mitigate the absolute dollar impact of high interest rates. For instance, the difference between a 20% down conventional payment of $473 and the FHA payment of $570 is modest in absolute terms, but the initial capital outlay difference is substantial. The practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that the primary hurdle in Dillon County is not the monthly payment capacity, which is comfortably low at 12.2% to 14.7% of income, but rather the upfront cash required for down payments and closing costs. Buyers should focus on saving for the initial $3,000 to $5,000 barrier rather than worrying about loan limits or high monthly obligations. The market’s stability, reflected in a 5-year price CAGR of 4.9%, suggests that while values have grown, they remain decoupled from the high-cost environments that typically strain buyer finances. Therefore, financing accessibility in Dillon County is high, with the median buyer facing minimal structural barriers to homeownership compared to higher-priced markets.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Dillon County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Dillon County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Dillon County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Dillon County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Dillon County qualifies.
