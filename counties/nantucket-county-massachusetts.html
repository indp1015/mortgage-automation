📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Nantucket County presents a significant structural mismatch between standard mortgage limits and local property values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers only 34% of the median home value of $1,593,800, rendering FHA financing impractical for the typical market transaction. Even the conforming limit of $1,249,125 covers merely 78% of the median price. Consequently, the majority of buyers seeking median-priced homes must utilize jumbo loans, as their financing needs exceed conventional agency guidelines. This market dynamic effectively excludes buyers relying on government-backed low-down-payment programs from the central segment of the housing market. The affordability burden for typical buyers remains severe under current market conditions. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, the principal and interest payment for a 20% down payment on a median-priced home is $8,828 per month. This figure represents 75.8% of the median household income of $139,688, far exceeding the standard 28% debt-to-income ratio threshold. Even with a 10% down payment, the monthly obligation rises to $9,931, consuming approximately 85% of median income. The minimum down payment required to avoid private mortgage insurance on a conforming loan is $318,760 (20% of $1,593,800), a substantial capital requirement that is difficult for median-income households to accumulate. The 9.9% five-year price CAGR further compounds this barrier, as rising home values outpace income growth, widening the gap between asset prices and purchasing power. Given these constraints, conventional jumbo loans are the primary financing vehicle for median-priced purchases, though they typically require higher credit scores and larger down payments than agency loans. The current 7.40% interest rate significantly amplifies payment burdens compared to historical low-rate environments; a 100 basis point decrease would reduce the 20% down payment scenario to approximately $8,350, yet still leave the payment burden above 60% of median income. For prospective buyers, the practical takeaway is that purchasing at the median price in Nantucket County requires substantial liquid assets and income well above the local median. Buyers relying on median household income should target properties priced below the conforming limit or consider alternative financing structures, as the combination of high price points, elevated interest rates, and limited loan coverage creates a high barrier to entry for the average resident.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Nantucket County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Nantucket County is $811,931. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Nantucket County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,249,125 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Nantucket County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Nantucket County qualifies.
