📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in Pushmataha County, Oklahoma, is characterized by substantial regulatory headroom relative to local property values. The 2026 FHA loan limit of $541,287 covers 409% of the median home value of $132,300, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 629%. Consequently, standard loan limits do not constrain typical transactions in this low-cost market. The median household income of $47,940 supports monthly FHA payments of $883, which represent 22.1% of gross income. This ratio falls within the conventional 28% debt-to-income guideline, indicating that median-priced homes remain accessible to local earners despite recent price appreciation. Regarding loan type suitability, FHA financing appears most aligned with the demographic profile of the county. With an owner-occupancy rate of 74.9% and a median income of $47,940, many prospective buyers likely lack the 20% down payment required for optimal conventional terms. The minimum FHA down payment of 3.5% on a median-priced home totals $4,630.50, a threshold that is more attainable for local households than the $26,460 required for a 20% conventional down payment. While USDA loans may be available in eligible rural areas, the broad applicability of FHA limits and lower credit requirements make it the primary recommendation for the typical buyer in this market. The current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% significantly impacts affordability compared to historical averages. At this rate, the monthly principal and interest payment for a median home is $883 under FHA terms. Had rates been lower, such as 6.0%, the payment would decrease by approximately $100, further easing the burden. However, the 22.1% income ratio remains manageable, suggesting that while higher interest rates increase total borrowing costs, they do not currently exclude median-income households from homeownership. The rapid price appreciation, with a five-year CAGR of 12.5%, has compressed affordability margins, yet the low absolute price point of $132,300 provides a buffer against rate volatility. For prospective buyers, the most critical insight is that down payment savings, rather than income sufficiency, are the primary barrier to entry. Accumulating the $4,630 minimum for an FHA loan is a more immediate and achievable goal than increasing income or waiting for rate declines. Buyers should prioritize securing pre-approval for FHA financing to lock in their purchasing power, as the low entry price point remains the county’s strongest asset in the current high-rate environment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Pushmataha County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Pushmataha County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Pushmataha County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Pushmataha County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Pushmataha County qualifies.
