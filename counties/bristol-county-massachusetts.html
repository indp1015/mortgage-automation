📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Bristol County’s median home value of $451,200 sits comfortably within both the 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 and the conforming limit of $832,750. The FHA limit covers 120% of the median price, while the conforming limit covers 185%, ensuring that standard financing options are accessible for the typical buyer. This adequate coverage means that most residents purchasing at the median price point will not need to seek jumbo loans or high-cost alternatives, as the county is not designated as high-cost. Consequently, the primary constraint on affordability is not loan eligibility but rather the monthly payment burden relative to local income levels. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, the monthly principal and interest payment for an FHA loan with 3.5% down is approximately $3,014. This amount represents 42.2% of the median household income of $85,625, a ratio that exceeds the conventional 28% guideline for front-end debt-to-income ratios. Even with a 20% down payment on a conventional loan, the payment of $2,499 still consumes 35.0% of median income. These figures indicate that the typical household in Bristol County faces significant strain when servicing a mortgage at the median price point. The high interest rate environment amplifies this burden, as a 7.40% rate significantly increases monthly obligations compared to the sub-4% rates seen in previous years, making the current pricing structure less accessible for average-income earners. For typical buyers, conventional loans with substantial down payments offer the best path to affordability, though they require larger upfront capital. The minimum down payment for an FHA loan at the median price is $15,792, which is feasible for many households but leaves less room for closing costs and reserves. However, because the FHA payment burden is so high relative to income, buyers may find that conventional loans with 10% or 20% down payments are necessary to bring monthly costs into a more manageable range, even if the total upfront cost is higher. The 10% down conventional option results in a $2,811 payment, which is still high but slightly better than the FHA alternative. Prospective buyers should recognize that at current rates, purchasing at the median price requires either above-median income or a substantial down payment to maintain a healthy debt-to-income ratio. The practical takeaway is that buyers should prioritize increasing their down payment to offset the high interest rate, as reducing the loan principal is the most effective way to lower the monthly payment burden in this specific market context.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Bristol County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Bristol County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Bristol County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Bristol County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Bristol County qualifies.
