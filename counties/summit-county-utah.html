📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Summit County, Utah, presents a distinct financing challenge where the median home value of $1,067,700 exceeds the FHA 1-unit limit of $541,287. Consequently, FHA loans cover only 51% of the typical transaction value, rendering them impractical for median-priced properties without significant gap financing. In contrast, the conforming 1-unit limit of $1,150,000 covers 108% of the median price, making conventional financing the primary viable option for most buyers in this market. Because the median home price surpasses the FHA threshold, prospective purchasers are effectively excluded from low-down-payment FHA programs unless they target significantly below-median inventory. The monthly payment burden for a median-priced home is substantial relative to local income levels. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, a conventional loan with 20% down results in a principal and interest payment of $5,914 per month. This amount represents 51.4% of the median household income of $138,114, far exceeding the standard 28% debt-to-income guideline. Even with a 10% down payment, the payment rises to $6,653, or 57.5% of median income. These figures indicate that households earning the median income face extreme affordability constraints, as the required down payment of $213,540 (20% of the median price) is a significant capital barrier. The high entry cost, combined with elevated monthly obligations, suggests that only households with incomes well above the median or substantial savings can comfortably secure financing at current market conditions. Rate sensitivity further compounds these challenges. At 7.40%, the cost of capital is a major driver of the payment burden. A reduction to a more historical 6.0% rate would lower the 20% down payment to approximately $4,900, reducing the income requirement to 42.6%. However, at current rates, the 51.4% income ratio remains prohibitive for the average resident. The practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that purchasing at the median price point in Summit County requires either a household income significantly higher than the $138,114 median or a larger down payment to reduce the loan principal. Buyers targeting the median value should prioritize conventional loans with at least 20% equity to avoid private mortgage insurance, while recognizing that the current interest rate environment necessitates robust cash reserves to meet both down payment and closing cost requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Summit County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Summit County is $1,150,000. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Summit County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,150,000 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Summit County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Summit County qualifies.
