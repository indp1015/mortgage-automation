📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Dickson County’s housing market presents a distinct financing profile where standard loan limits offer substantial headroom above typical property values. The 2026 FHA single-family limit of $541,287 covers 177% of the median home value of $306,400, while the conforming limit of $1,029,250 covers 336%. Consequently, the vast majority of properties in the county fall well within conventional financing parameters. This ample coverage ensures that typical buyers are not constrained by loan ceilings, allowing for broader inventory access compared to high-cost markets where limits frequently restrict purchasing power. The primary financial constraint is not the loan limit itself, but rather the current interest rate environment and local income levels. Monthly payment burdens reveal significant pressure on median-income households. With a 30-year fixed rate at 7.40%, the principal and interest (P&I) payment for an FHA loan on the median-priced home is $2,047. This amount represents 32.8% of the median household income of $75,003, exceeding the standard 30% debt-to-income threshold often cited by lenders. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces the P&I to $1,697, or 27.2% of income, bringing the burden back into a more conventional range. However, achieving this lower payment requires a substantial upfront capital outlay. The minimum down payment for an FHA loan at the median price is 3.5%, totaling approximately $10,724. While this is lower than conventional requirements, it remains a significant barrier for first-time buyers in a market where the five-year price CAGR has reached 15.9%. The rapid appreciation from $146,300 in 2017 to $306,400 in 2024 has outpaced income growth, making the entry point increasingly difficult despite the low minimum down payment percentage. The 7.40% interest rate significantly amplifies monthly costs compared to historical averages. In lower-rate environments, the same loan amount would yield substantially lower P&I payments, potentially keeping the FHA option within the 30% income threshold. For prospective buyers, the most practical insight is that conventional financing with a higher down payment is currently the most viable path to maintaining manageable debt-to-income ratios. Buyers should prioritize saving for a 10% to 20% down payment to reduce monthly obligations, as the current rate environment makes high-leverage options like FHA loans financially risky for median-income households. Additionally, the high owner-occupancy rate of 80.3% suggests a stable market, but the rapid price appreciation necessitates careful budgeting to avoid overextending credit in a high-rate cycle.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Dickson County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Dickson County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Dickson County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,029,250 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Dickson County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Dickson County qualifies.
