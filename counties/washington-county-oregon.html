📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Washington County, Oregon, presents a complex financing landscape where the median home value of $588,000 sits near the upper boundary of standard loan limits. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers only 92% of the median price, meaning buyers purchasing at the market median cannot utilize FHA financing without exceeding the cap. Consequently, the conforming limit of $832,750 is the primary vehicle for typical transactions, covering 142% of the median value. This structural gap forces most median-priced buyers toward conventional financing, as the FHA product is effectively unavailable for the average property in this county. The monthly payment burden remains significant relative to local income levels. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment on a median-priced home requires a principal and interest payment of $3,256. This amount consumes 36.3% of the median household income of $107,772, a ratio that exceeds the standard 30% debt-to-income guideline used by many lenders. Even with a 10% down payment, the payment rises to $3,664, further straining affordability. The high interest rate environment amplifies this burden; compared to lower-rate periods, the current 7.40% rate significantly increases the cost of carrying debt, reducing the maximum loan amount a median-income household can qualify for. Down payment barriers represent a critical hurdle for prospective buyers. To secure a conventional loan at the median price, a minimum 20% down payment of $117,600 is required to avoid private mortgage insurance, though a 10% down payment of $58,800 is a lower initial threshold. For a household earning the median income, saving $58,800 to $117,600 requires substantial time and discipline, especially given that the median monthly rent of $1,849 competes directly with savings goals. The rapid appreciation of 12.1% annually over the past five years has outpaced income growth, widening the gap between asset accumulation and price increases. The practical takeaway for buyers in Washington County is that conventional financing is the standard requirement, making the accumulation of a substantial down payment the primary determinant of market entry. Prospective buyers should prioritize building liquidity to meet the 10% to 20% down payment thresholds, as FHA options are largely inaccessible at the median price point. Additionally, maintaining a debt-to-income ratio below 36% is essential to qualify for the necessary loan amounts in this high-rate environment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Washington County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Washington County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Washington County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Washington County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Washington County qualifies.
