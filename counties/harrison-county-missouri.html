📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The mortgage landscape in Harrison County, Missouri, is characterized by significant financing headroom relative to local property values. The 2026 FHA single-family limit of $541,287 covers 494% of the median home value of $109,500, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 761%. Consequently, price-based restrictions do not constrain typical buyers, as the median home falls well within all standard loan limits. This ample coverage ensures that financing availability is not a barrier for the average transaction, allowing lenders to underwrite based primarily on borrower credit and income rather than property value caps. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable for the median household. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan requiring 3.5% down on a median-priced home results in a principal and interest payment of $731. This amount represents 16.1% of the median household income of $54,367. Alternatively, a conventional loan with 20% down yields a payment of $606, or 13.4% of income. Both scenarios fall within conventional debt-to-income guidelines, indicating that monthly cash flow is not a primary obstacle for qualified applicants. However, the down payment requirement presents a more significant hurdle. An FHA buyer must secure $3,833 in cash, while a conventional buyer with 20% down needs $21,900. Given the median income, the FHA down payment is more feasible, particularly when paired with assistance programs, whereas the conventional down payment requires substantial savings accumulation. The current 7.40% interest rate significantly impacts affordability compared to historical lows. A 500-basis-point reduction would lower the FHA payment to approximately $625, reducing the income burden by nearly 1,000 dollars annually. This rate sensitivity highlights that while local prices have stabilized after a sharp 2021-2023 appreciation cycle, the cost of borrowing remains the dominant variable affecting long-term wealth accumulation. For prospective buyers, the most practical insight is that purchasing at the median price is highly accessible due to low capital requirements relative to income. Buyers should prioritize FHA financing to minimize initial cash outlay, leveraging the county’s non-high-cost status to avoid additional fees, and focus on building creditworthiness to secure the best possible rate in the current environment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Harrison County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Harrison County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Harrison County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Harrison County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Harrison County qualifies.
