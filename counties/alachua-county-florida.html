📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA and conforming loan limits both comfortably exceed the median home price of $288,800 in Alachua County. The FHA 1‑unit limit of $541,287 covers 187 % of the median price, while the conforming ceiling of $832,750 covers 288 %, confirming that a typical buyer can obtain a fully amortizing loan without resorting to jumbo financing. At the current 30‑year fixed rate of 7.24 %, the estimated principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment for an FHA loan (3.5 % down, $278,692 loan) is $1,899 per month, representing 36.8 % of the median household income ($61,949 / 12 = $5,162). A conventional loan with 20 % down ($231,040 loan) yields a $1,574 P&I payment, or 30.5 % of income. Both ratios sit near the conventional 28‑30 % affordability threshold, indicating that even with a modest down payment, the monthly debt service is a sizable portion of earnings. For most buyers, a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment is the most cost‑effective option because it avoids FHA mortgage insurance premiums and delivers the lowest P&I burden. However, the $57,760 required for a 20 % down payment (20 % × $288,800) may be prohibitive for many households, given the median savings rates in the area. An FHA loan reduces the upfront cash need to $10,108 (3.5 % down), but the higher loan balance and required mortgage insurance increase the monthly outlay. USDA or VA financing could be viable for eligible rural or veteran borrowers, as those programs also allow low down payments and often feature reduced insurance costs. The 7.24 % rate inflates monthly payments by roughly $200–$300 compared with a 5 % environment, compressing affordability margins and raising the income‑to‑payment ratios by 4–5 percentage points. Consequently, the primary barrier for median‑price purchasers is the down‑payment requirement; securing at least a 10 % contribution ($28,880) brings the P&I payment to $1,771, a level that aligns more closely with the 30 % affordability benchmark. **Takeaway:** Prospective buyers who can marshal a 10 %–20 % down payment will achieve the most sustainable mortgage terms, while those limited to the FHA minimum must budget for a higher monthly debt load that approaches the upper end of conventional affordability limits.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Alachua County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Alachua County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Alachua County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Alachua County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Alachua County qualifies.
