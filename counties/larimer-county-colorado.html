📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Larimer County’s housing market presents a distinct financing challenge, as the median home value of $569,100 exceeds the 2026 FHA loan limit of $541,287. Consequently, the FHA limit covers only 95% of the typical property price, rendering it insufficient for standard purchases without a second mortgage. In contrast, the conforming loan limit of $832,750 covers 146% of the median value, providing ample headroom. This discrepancy forces the majority of local buyers toward conventional financing, as FHA options are largely restricted to lower-priced properties or require complex piggyback structures. The conforming limit’s superior coverage ensures that standard loan products remain accessible for the vast majority of transactions in this high-growth market, which has seen a 13.2% annual price increase over the last five years. The affordability burden is significant under current market conditions. At a 7.32% interest rate, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment on the median home results in a principal and interest payment of $3,127 per month. This figure represents 40.0% of the median household income of $93,765. For buyers opting for a 10% down payment, the payment rises to $3,518, consuming a larger share of disposable income. While these ratios approach the upper thresholds of traditional underwriting guidelines, they remain within the realm of feasibility for households at or above the median income level. The high price appreciation has outpaced income growth, creating a widening gap between asset values and purchasing power. Conventional financing emerges as the primary recommendation for typical buyers due to limit constraints and the absence of high-cost county designations. The minimum down payment of $113,820 (20% of the median price) is a substantial barrier, though 10% down options at $56,910 offer a more accessible entry point. The current 7.32% rate significantly impacts affordability compared to historical lows; a drop to 6.0% would reduce the 20% down payment to approximately $2,850, improving cash flow. Prospective buyers should prioritize larger down payments to mitigate monthly debt-to-income ratios, as the combination of high home values and elevated interest rates leaves limited margin for error in household budgets.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Larimer County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Larimer County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Larimer County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Larimer County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Larimer County qualifies.
