📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA 1‑unit loan limit of $541,287 covers roughly 95 % of Larimer County’s median home price of $569,100, leaving a $27,800 gap that must be financed through a conforming or jumbo loan. By contrast, the 2026 conforming ceiling of $832,750 is 146 % of the median price, fully encompassing the typical purchase and allowing borrowers to stay within the conventional market without triggering jumbo pricing. Consequently, the FHA limit is marginal for median‑priced homes, while the conforming limit provides ample headroom. At a 7.18 % 30‑year fixed rate, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment on a conventional loan with 20 % down ($455,280 loan) is $3,084 per month, representing 39.5 % of the median household income ($93,765 / 12 = $7,814). The FHA scenario (3.5 % down, $549,182 loan) yields a $3,720 P&I payment, or 47.6 % of income, exceeding the conventional benchmark by $636 per month. Both ratios are above the traditional 28 % housing‑cost threshold, indicating that even with a 20 % down payment the median buyer would face a substantial debt‑service burden. For most buyers, a conventional loan with at least a 20 % down payment is the most viable option because it stays within the conforming limit, avoids the higher FHA mortgage insurance premiums, and yields a lower P&I payment relative to income. VA‑eligible borrowers could obtain similar terms with no down payment, but the market lacks a significant veteran population to shift the overall profile. USDA financing is unavailable because the median home price exceeds the program’s $585,000 ceiling for eligible rural tracts. The minimum down payment to purchase at the median price is $56,910 (10 %); however, a 10 % down conventional loan still produces a $3,469 P&I payment—44 % of income—still above the 28 % guideline. Raising the down payment to 20 % reduces the burden to a more acceptable level but may be challenging for households without sizable savings. Because the current 7.18 % rate adds roughly $400–$600 per month to P&I payments versus a 5 % environment, affordability is markedly tighter. Prospective buyers should prioritize saving for a 20 % down payment or seek a VA loan if eligible, as these paths most effectively align monthly costs with median household earnings in Larimer County.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Larimer County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Larimer County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Larimer County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Larimer County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Larimer County qualifies.
