📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in Franklin County, Idaho, demonstrates strong alignment between available loan limits and local property values. The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home stands at $541,287, which covers 145% of the median home value of $372,600. Similarly, the conforming limit of $832,750 exceeds the median price by 223%. Consequently, typical buyers face no structural barriers regarding loan size, as both primary loan types comfortably accommodate the average transaction. This coverage ensures that financing capacity is not a constraint for the majority of the market, allowing lenders to extend credit without requiring jumbo loan structures. Monthly payment burdens remain significant relative to local income levels. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan on the median-priced home results in a principal and interest payment of $2,489 per month. This figure represents 41.5% of the median monthly household income of $71,950, exceeding the standard 30% debt-to-income guideline for optimal qualification. Even with a 20% down payment on a conventional loan, the monthly P&I payment of $2,063 accounts for 34.4% of median income. These ratios indicate that while financing is accessible, affordability is tight for households earning the median income, requiring careful budgeting or higher income levels to maintain financial stability. For typical buyers, conventional financing with a 20% down payment offers the most manageable monthly burden, though it requires an initial outlay of $74,520. FHA loans lower this barrier to $12,981 (3.5% down), making them the most accessible option for first-time buyers with limited savings. However, the lower down payment does not alleviate the high monthly payment percentage. The current 7.40% interest rate significantly impacts affordability; had rates been lower, the monthly payment burden would decrease proportionally, potentially bringing the FHA payment ratio below the 36% threshold often preferred by underwriters. The rapid appreciation of home values, with a 17.2% five-year CAGR, further compresses affordability by increasing the required loan principal. Prospective buyers should prioritize maximizing down payments to reduce loan balances, as this directly lowers monthly obligations and improves qualification odds in a high-rate environment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Franklin County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Franklin County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Franklin County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Franklin County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Franklin County qualifies.
