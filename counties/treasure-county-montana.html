📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Treasure County’s housing finance landscape is characterized by significant headroom between local home values and federal loan limits. The median home value of $230,300 sits comfortably within the 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 and the conforming limit of $832,750. Specifically, the FHA limit covers 235% of the median price, while the conforming limit covers 362%. This ample coverage ensures that no buyer in this market faces financing caps as a barrier to entry, allowing for standard loan structures without requiring jumbo loan products. Consequently, the primary constraint for affordability is not loan availability but rather the monthly debt-to-income ratio relative to local earnings. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, the monthly principal and interest payment for a median-priced home varies significantly based on down payment size. An FHA loan with a 3.5% down payment requires a $1,538 monthly payment, which consumes 27.2% of the median household income of $67,917. In contrast, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment reduces the payment to $1,275, or 22.5% of income. While these figures fall within typical lender guidelines of 28-36% for front-end ratios, the high interest rate amplifies the cost of borrowing compared to historical averages. The 16.9% five-year price CAGR has outpaced income growth in many rural contexts, meaning that even moderate price increases can quickly push borrowers toward the upper limits of their debt service capacity. For typical buyers, conventional financing with a 20% down payment offers the most sustainable long-term affordability profile, though it requires a substantial upfront capital outlay of $46,060. This barrier is significant given the median income level, potentially necessitating the use of gift funds or savings accumulation strategies. Conversely, FHA loans lower the entry barrier to $8,061 but result in higher monthly obligations and mandatory mortgage insurance, which further strains cash flow. The 7.40% rate environment makes the choice of down payment critical; a larger equity stake directly mitigates the impact of high interest rates on monthly payments. Prospective buyers should prioritize maximizing their down payment to reduce the loan principal, as this remains the most effective lever for improving monthly affordability in a high-rate, appreciating market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Treasure County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Treasure County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Treasure County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Treasure County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Treasure County qualifies.
