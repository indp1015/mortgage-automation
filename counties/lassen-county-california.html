📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA and conforming loan limits both comfortably exceed the median home price of $265,300 in Lassen County. The FHA 1‑unit limit of $541,287 covers 204 % of the median value, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 314 %. Consequently, a typical buyer can obtain a loan that fully finances the property without resorting to jumbo products, and the loan‑to‑value (LTV) ratios required for FHA (96.5 % LTV) or conventional (80‑90 % LTV) financing are readily attainable. Using the current 30‑year fixed rate of 7.18 %, the estimated principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment for an FHA loan (3.5 % down, $256,014 loan) is $1,734 per month, representing 30.9 % of the median household income ($67,403 annual, $5,617 monthly). A conventional loan with a 20 % down payment ($212,240 loan) yields a $1,437 P&I payment, or 25.6 % of monthly income. Both ratios fall below the conventional affordability threshold of 28‑30 % for housing costs, but the FHA scenario is marginally higher because of the larger loan balance and mortgage‑insurance premium. When property taxes, insurance, and utilities are added, total monthly housing costs likely approach or exceed the 30 % ceiling for many households, especially renters paying $1,000 per month. For most buyers, a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment is the most cost‑effective option, eliminating mortgage‑insurance premiums and reducing the interest burden. However, the required down payment of $53,060 (20 % of $265,300) is a significant barrier in a market where median savings are modest. An FHA loan lowers the down payment to $9,286 (3.5 %), making homeownership attainable for a broader segment, albeit at a higher monthly cost. USDA or VA loans could further reduce down payments to 0 % for eligible rural or veteran borrowers, but eligibility must be verified. The 7.18 % rate inflates monthly payments by roughly $150–$200 compared with a 5 % environment, compressing affordability margins. Buyers who can secure a lower rate through points or a shorter loan term will see a proportionate reduction in P&I. **Practical takeaway:** Prospective buyers should prioritize saving for a 20 % down payment to access conventional financing, which offers the lowest monthly outlay; if that target is unattainable, an FHA loan remains viable but will consume a larger share of income and may require careful budgeting for ancillary housing costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Lassen County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Lassen County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Lassen County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Lassen County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Lassen County qualifies.
