📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Cheboygan County’s housing market presents a distinct affordability profile where federal loan limits significantly exceed local price points. The 2026 FHA single-unit limit of $541,287 covers 281% of the median home value of $192,500, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 433%. Consequently, virtually all residential transactions in the county fall well below both ceilings, ensuring that financing availability is not constrained by loan caps. This ample coverage allows borrowers to access standard government-backed and conventional products without requiring jumbo loans or alternative financing structures, a common necessity in high-cost metropolitan areas. The primary challenge for buyers lies in monthly payment burdens relative to local income levels. With a median household income of $62,875, the estimated principal and interest payment for an FHA loan at the median price is $1,286, representing 24.5% of monthly income. While this is near the upper threshold of conventional debt-to-income guidelines, it remains manageable for many households. A conventional loan with a 20% down payment reduces this burden to $1,066, or 20.3% of income. The current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% significantly impacts these figures; compared to lower-rate environments, the higher interest cost adds substantial monthly pressure. For instance, a 100-basis-point drop in rates would reduce the FHA payment by approximately $80, highlighting the sensitivity of affordability to interest rate fluctuations. Regarding loan type and down payment barriers, the FHA option is likely the most accessible for typical buyers. It requires only a 3.5% down payment, amounting to $6,738 for a median-priced home. This is far more feasible than the $38,500 required for a 20% conventional down payment. Given the high owner-occupancy rate of 85.6% and the steady appreciation of 10.5% annually over the past five years, equity accumulation is a strong motivator. However, the rapid price growth from $116,900 in 2017 to $192,500 in 2024 has outpaced income growth, tightening the margin for entry. Prospective buyers should prioritize securing the lowest possible interest rate, as the current 7.40% rate makes the difference between a 20% and 25% debt-to-income ratio critical for loan approval. Ultimately, the low entry price relative to national averages remains the county’s strongest asset for first-time buyers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Cheboygan County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Cheboygan County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Cheboygan County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Cheboygan County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Cheboygan County qualifies.
