📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Brewster County’s housing market remains well within federal loan limits, ensuring broad access to standard financing options. The 2026 FHA one-unit limit of $541,287 covers 238% of the $227,000 median home value, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 367%. Consequently, no buyer purchasing a median-priced property faces high-cost county restrictions or limit-related barriers. This ample coverage indicates that the typical home in this market is fully accessible through both government-backed and conventional channels, with no need for jumbo loan structures or alternative financing mechanisms to close on a standard sale. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels, though interest rates significantly impact affordability. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan with 3.5% down results in a principal and interest payment of $1,516 per month. This figure represents 32.4% of the median household income of $56,212, which is near the upper threshold for conventional debt-to-income ratios but remains within FHA guidelines. In contrast, a conventional loan with 20% down yields a payment of $1,257, or 26.8% of median income, offering a more comfortable margin for additional housing costs. The 7.40% rate is notably higher than historical averages, meaning that a buyer who would have qualified easily in a 3-4% rate environment now faces a payment that is roughly 40-50% higher for the same loan amount, effectively reducing purchasing power by approximately $50,000 to $70,000 in total loan capacity. For the typical Brewster County buyer, the FHA loan is likely the most practical fit given the low down payment requirement of 3.5%. This translates to a minimum cash requirement of $7,945 for the median home, a feasible barrier for many households with savings. While conventional loans offer lower monthly payments, the 20% down payment requirement of $45,400 is a significant hurdle for the median income earner. USDA loans may also be viable depending on specific rural tract designations, but FHA provides the most consistent availability. Prospective buyers should note that while the median home value has grown at a 14.9% five-year CAGR, the deceleration to 5.1% in 2024 suggests a stabilizing market. The concrete takeaway is that securing an FHA pre-approval with 3.5% down allows median-income households to maintain a sustainable debt-to-income ratio despite elevated interest rates, whereas waiting for rate drops without adjusting down payment strategies may not improve overall affordability if prices continue to rise.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Brewster County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Brewster County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Brewster County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Brewster County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Brewster County qualifies.
