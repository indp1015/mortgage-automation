📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA 1‑unit loan limit of $541,287 covers only 82 % of Valley County’s median home price of $657,000, leaving a $115,713 gap that must be financed with a conventional or jumbo loan. By contrast, the 2026 conforming ceiling of $832,750 exceeds the median price by 27 %, fully accommodating the typical purchase without resorting to a jumbo product. Consequently, the FHA limit is insufficient for most buyers, while the conforming limit provides ample coverage. At a 7.18 % 30‑year fixed rate, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment on a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment ($525,600 loan) is $3,560 per month, representing 56.2 % of the median household income ($76,078 / 12 = $6,340). Even the lower‑down‑payment conventional scenario (10 % down, $4,005 P&I) consumes 63.2 % of monthly income, and the FHA option (3.5 % down, $4,294 P&I) reaches 67.7 %. All exceed the conventional affordability benchmark of 30 % of gross income, indicating a substantial payment burden for the median household. Given the price‑to‑limit dynamics and the high income‑to‑payment ratios, a conventional loan with at least a 20 % down payment is the most viable path for typical buyers; it avoids the FHA loan‑size shortfall and reduces the P&I component enough to approach the 30 % threshold. For borrowers unable to meet a 20 % down payment, a 10 % conventional loan remains the next best alternative, though it still imposes a heavy debt service load. USDA or VA financing could be considered for eligible veterans or rural‑qualified households, as they often require lower down payments and may offer more favorable rate spreads, but the median home price still exceeds the FHA limit, limiting their applicability. The 7.18 % rate inflates monthly payments by roughly $800–$1,000 compared with a 5 % environment, pushing affordability well below pre‑2020 levels. Prospective buyers should therefore prioritize saving for a 20 % down payment or seek alternative financing programs that can bridge the gap between FHA limits and local median prices.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Valley County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Valley County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Valley County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Valley County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Valley County qualifies.
