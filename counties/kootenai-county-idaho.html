📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in Kootenai County, Idaho, presents a complex affordability challenge driven by rapid home price appreciation. The 2026 FHA loan limit for one-unit properties is set at $541,287, which covers 104% of the local median home value of $518,700. This indicates that the typical median-priced home falls within the FHA limit, allowing buyers to access this government-backed option without requiring a jumbo loan. Similarly, the conforming loan limit of $832,750 covers 161% of the median price, providing substantial headroom for conventional financing. Consequently, loan limits are not a primary barrier to entry for the average buyer, as both major loan types adequately cover the typical market price. However, the monthly payment burden remains significant relative to local income levels. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan on a median-priced home requires a 3.5% down payment, resulting in a principal and interest payment of $3,465 per month. This amount represents 50.8% of the median household income of $81,861, far exceeding the standard 28-30% debt-to-income ratio threshold for qualified mortgages. Even with a 20% down payment on a conventional loan, the monthly payment of $2,873 consumes 42.1% of median income. These figures suggest that the median household income is insufficient to comfortably service debt on a median-priced home at current interest rates, indicating a severe affordability gap between home prices and earning power. Given these constraints, conventional loans with higher down payments are the most viable path for qualified buyers, as they reduce the loan principal and lower monthly obligations, though they require substantial upfront capital. The minimum down payment for an FHA loan on a median-priced home is approximately $18,155, while a 20% conventional down payment requires $103,740. The high interest rate environment exacerbates this barrier; compared to lower-rate periods, the current 7.40% rate significantly inflates monthly costs, making the 50.8% income ratio for FHA buyers particularly prohibitive. For prospective buyers, the practical takeaway is that purchasing at the median price likely requires either a household income well above the median of $81,861 or a substantial down payment to mitigate the impact of high monthly payments, as standard financing structures are misaligned with current local income levels.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Kootenai County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Kootenai County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Kootenai County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Kootenai County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Kootenai County qualifies.
