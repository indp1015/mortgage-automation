📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Fairfax County’s housing market presents a complex financing landscape where loan limits provide ample coverage, yet high interest rates significantly constrain monthly affordability. The 2026 FHA limit of $811,931 covers 111% of the median home value of $732,800, while the conforming limit of $1,249,125 covers 170%. Consequently, no typical buyer in this high-cost county faces loan limit restrictions. However, the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% creates substantial payment burdens. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with 3.5% down requires a principal and interest payment of $4,896 per month. This amount consumes 38.2% of the median household income of $153,637, a ratio that exceeds the conventional 36% debt-to-income guideline often used by lenders. Even with a 20% down payment on a conventional loan, the monthly payment of $4,059 still represents 31.7% of median income, leaving limited room for other financial obligations. Given these constraints, conventional financing is generally the most suitable option for qualified buyers in Fairfax County, as it avoids FHA mortgage insurance premiums that would further elevate the total monthly outlay. While FHA loans offer lower down payment requirements, the associated insurance costs make them less competitive for buyers with sufficient savings. The minimum down payment barrier for an FHA purchase is approximately $25,648 (3.5% of $732,800), which is a significant hurdle despite being lower than conventional minimums. The feasibility of this down payment depends heavily on individual savings, as it represents roughly 1.6 months of median household income. The 7.40% interest rate is the primary driver of reduced affordability compared to historical low-rate environments. A 100 basis point decrease in rates would reduce the 20% down conventional payment by approximately $250 per month, directly improving debt-to-income ratios. Prospective buyers should prioritize maximizing their down payment to reduce the principal balance and mitigate the impact of high interest rates. At the median price point, increasing the down payment from 20% to 25% would lower the monthly payment to roughly $3,780, reducing the income burden to 29.5%. This strategic adjustment provides a more sustainable financial profile for long-term homeownership in a market characterized by steady price appreciation of 6.5% annually over the last five years. Buyers must carefully assess their total debt load, ensuring that the mortgage payment remains well below 30% of gross income to maintain financial resilience against potential rate fluctuations or unexpected expenses. The combination of high home values and elevated interest rates necessitates a disciplined approach to savings and debt management for successful entry into the Fairfax County market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Fairfax County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Fairfax County is $811,931. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Fairfax County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,249,125 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Fairfax County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Fairfax County qualifies.
