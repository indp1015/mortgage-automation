📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA loan limit for a single‑unit dwelling in Boise County ($541,287) exceeds the 2024 median home value of $476,100 by 14 %, while the conforming ceiling ($832,750) is 175 % of that price. Consequently, both FHA and conventional financing comfortably cover the typical property, and the market is not constrained by loan‑size caps. At the current 30‑year fixed rate of 7.24 %, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment on a median‑priced home is $3,131 under an FHA loan (3.5 % down, $459,436 loan) and $2,595 under a conventional loan with 20 % down ($380,880 loan). Relative to the median household income of $78,774 (≈$6,564 monthly), the FHA payment represents 47.7 % of monthly earnings, whereas the 20 % conventional payment is 39.5 % of earnings. Both ratios exceed the conventional 28 % affordability benchmark, indicating a substantial debt‑service burden for an average family. For most buyers, a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment yields the lowest monthly outlay and the most favorable debt‑to‑income (DTI) profile. However, the required $95,220 down payment (20 % of $476,100) is a significant barrier; the median renter’s monthly cost of $850 suggests limited cash‑on‑hand for many households. An FHA loan reduces the down‑payment requirement to $16,664 (3.5 %), making entry more attainable, though the higher P&I and mortgage‑insurance premiums increase the overall cost. USDA or VA options could be viable for eligible rural or veteran buyers, as they also allow low down payments and often waive mortgage insurance, but eligibility is limited to specific applicant groups. The 7.24 % rate inflates monthly payments by roughly $400–$500 compared with a 5 % environment, shrinking affordability margins and pushing many borrowers above the 30 % DTI threshold. **Practical takeaway:** Prospective purchasers should prioritize saving for a 20 % down payment to secure a conventional loan; the reduced monthly payment and absence of mortgage‑insurance premiums markedly improve affordability in a market where even the FHA‑qualified payment consumes nearly half of median household income.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Boise County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Boise County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Boise County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Boise County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Boise County qualifies.
