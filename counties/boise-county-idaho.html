📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Boise County’s housing market presents a complex financing landscape where loan limits remain comfortably above median home values, yet affordability is severely constrained by high interest rates and rapid price appreciation. The 2026 FHA one-unit limit of $541,287 covers 114% of the median home value of $476,100, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 175%. Consequently, typical buyers face no structural barrier regarding loan ceilings. However, the median household income of $78,774 creates a significant burden. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, a standard FHA loan with 3.5% down results in a principal and interest payment of $3,181 per month. This figure equates to 48.5% of the median monthly income, a ratio that exceeds traditional underwriting guidelines for qualified borrowers. Even with a 20% down payment on a conventional loan, the payment of $2,637 consumes 40.2% of income, leaving limited room for taxes, insurance, and living expenses. Given these constraints, conventional financing with a substantial down payment is likely the most viable path for median-income households, as it reduces the loan principal and overall payment burden compared to FHA options. The minimum down payment for an FHA loan at the median price is approximately $16,661, which is feasible for many, but the resulting monthly obligation remains prohibitive. Conversely, a 20% down payment requires $95,220, a significant barrier for local residents. The current 7.40% rate dramatically reduces affordability compared to lower-rate environments; a 5% rate would have lowered the FHA payment to approximately $2,430, improving the income ratio to roughly 37%. This sensitivity highlights that interest rates, not loan limits, are the primary determinant of market access in Boise County. Prospective buyers must recognize that the median price point is increasingly out of reach for households earning the area median income without substantial down payments or income above the median. The 20.8% five-year price CAGR has outpaced income growth, creating a structural affordability gap. For those entering the market, opting for a lower-priced home or securing a co-signer to qualify for a lower interest rate may be necessary strategies. The data indicates that while financing limits are adequate, the combination of high rates and elevated home values restricts the pool of qualified buyers to those with above-median incomes or significant equity reserves.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Boise County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Boise County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Boise County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Boise County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Boise County qualifies.
