📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Financing coverage is strong: the FHA 1‑unit limit of $541,287 exceeds the median home price of $279,600 by 194%, and the conforming limit of $832,750 exceeds it by 298%, confirming that both programs can underwrite a typical purchase. Because the median home price is well below both the FHA and conforming limits, borrowers have multiple financing pathways, but the choice hinges on down‑payment capacity and acceptable debt‑to‑income ratios. For a median‑priced home, the Federal Housing Administration loan requiring a 3.5% down payment on a $269,814 principal results in a monthly principal‑and‑interest payment of $1,868, which equals 31.0% of the median household’s monthly income of $6,018. A conventional loan with 20% down on a $223,680 loan carries a payment of $1,548, or 25.7% of income, while a 10% down conventional loan on a $251,640 loan is $1,742 per month (28.9% of income). Given the size of the median home relative to loan limits, a conventional mortgage with a 10% down payment appears to offer the best balance of affordability and accessibility for typical buyers, as it keeps the payment below 30% of income while staying well within the conforming ceiling. The minimum down payment required is 3.5% ($9,788) for FHA, 10% ($27,960) for conventional, and 20% ($55,920) for the most favorable conventional terms; the 3.5% option is the most feasible for households with limited savings, though it entails a higher payment‑to‑income ratio. The current 7.40% interest rate raises the monthly cost compared with lower‑rate environments; at 6% the same loan would reduce the FHA payment to roughly $1,620 and the 20% conventional payment to about $1,350, improving affordability by 10‑15%. Practical takeaway: prospective buyers should prioritize a low‑down‑payment FHA loan to stay within the 31% income threshold or target a 10% down conventional loan to maintain a payment under 27% of income, both of which are sustainable under the present rate environment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Windham County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Windham County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Windham County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Windham County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Windham County qualifies.
