📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Charlevoix County’s financing landscape is characterized by substantial regulatory headroom relative to local market valuations. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 196% of the median home value of $276,800, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 301%. Because the county is not designated as high-cost, these standard limits provide ample capacity for typical transactions. Consequently, loan type selection is driven less by limit constraints and more by credit profiles and down payment capabilities. For the median buyer, the FHA option offers the lowest barrier to entry, requiring only 3.5% down, whereas conventional loans typically require 10% to 20% to avoid private mortgage insurance premiums. The primary affordability challenge stems from the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan entails a principal and interest payment of $1,849 per month. This figure represents 29.0% of the median household income of $76,531, a ratio that approaches the upper threshold of conventional underwriting guidelines. In contrast, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment reduces the monthly P&I to $1,533, or 24.0% of income, offering a more comfortable debt-to-income profile. The significant price appreciation, evidenced by a 5-year CAGR of 11.8% and a 14.0% year-over-year increase in 2024, has compressed affordability margins. Compared to lower-rate environments, the current 7.40% rate increases the monthly burden by hundreds of dollars, effectively requiring higher incomes to service the same debt. Down payment feasibility remains a critical variable. An FHA buyer needs approximately $9,688 for 3.5% down, a feasible sum for many households with savings. However, a conventional 20% down payment requires $55,360, a substantial capital outlay that may exclude first-time buyers. Given the high owner-occupancy rate of 83.5%, the market is stable, yet the rapid price growth outpacing income growth suggests that leverage is necessary for typical buyers. The most practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that while loan limits are not restrictive, the combination of elevated interest rates and rising home values necessitates careful budgeting. Buyers should prioritize maximizing down payments where possible to mitigate the impact of the 7.40% rate, or consider FHA financing to minimize initial cash requirements, ensuring that total monthly obligations remain within sustainable income limits.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Charlevoix County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Charlevoix County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Charlevoix County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Charlevoix County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Charlevoix County qualifies.
