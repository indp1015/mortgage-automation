📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA 1‑unit loan limit of **$541,287** covers only **73 %** of Ouray County’s median home price of **$739,800**, leaving a gap of roughly **$198,500** that must be financed through a conforming or jumbo loan. By contrast, the 2026 conforming ceiling of **$832,750** exceeds the median price by **13 %**, fully accommodating a typical purchase without resorting to a jumbo product. Consequently, the FHA limit is insufficient for most buyers, while the conforming limit provides adequate coverage for the median‑priced home. At the prevailing 30‑year fixed rate of **7.24 %**, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment on a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment ($591,840 loan) is **$4,033/month**. This represents **53.2 %** of the county’s median monthly household income (**$7,585**). Even the more aggressive 10 % down scenario ($665,820 loan) yields a P&I of **$4,537/month**, or **59.8 %** of income. The FHA option (3.5 % down, $713,907 loan) costs **$4,865/month**, consuming **64.1 %** of income. All scenarios exceed the conventional affordability benchmark of 30 % of gross income, indicating a substantial payment burden for median earners. Given the price‑to‑limit dynamics and the high income‑to‑payment ratios, a **conventional loan with at least a 20 % down payment** is the most viable path for typical buyers. This down payment amounts to **$147,960**, which, while sizable, aligns with the county’s high owner‑occupancy rate (82.5 %) and suggests that many buyers possess sufficient equity or savings. FHA financing is only appropriate for borrowers unable to meet the 20 % threshold, but they must either accept a higher debt‑to‑income ratio or seek a lower‑priced property. The 7.24 % rate inflates monthly obligations by roughly **$600–$800** compared with a 5 % environment, tightening affordability margins. A modest rate decline to 6 % would lower the 20 % conventional P&I to about **$3,650/month**, reducing the income share to **48 %** and moving closer to conventional affordability standards. **Practical takeaway:** Prospective buyers should plan for a minimum **$148 k down payment** and evaluate whether their post‑tax cash flow can sustain a P&I payment that exceeds half of their gross income; otherwise, targeting homes below the median price or increasing the down payment are the only ways to achieve a financially sustainable mortgage in Ouray County today.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Ouray County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Ouray County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Ouray County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Ouray County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Ouray County qualifies.
