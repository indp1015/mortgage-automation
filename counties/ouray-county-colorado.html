📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in Ouray County presents significant structural challenges due to the divergence between local home values and federal loan limits. The median home value of $739,800 exceeds the 2026 FHA limit for a one-unit property, which stands at $541,287. Consequently, the FHA limit covers only 73% of the median price, rendering it insufficient for typical transactions without a higher loan amount. In contrast, the conforming loan limit of $832,750 covers 113% of the median value, indicating that conventional financing is the primary viable option for most buyers. This dynamic effectively excludes FHA-backed purchases for median-priced homes, forcing buyers into the conventional market where underwriting standards and down payment requirements are typically stricter. Monthly payment burdens are substantial relative to local income levels. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.32%, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment on the median-priced home results in a principal and interest payment of $4,065 per month. This figure represents 53.6% of the median household income of $91,020, far exceeding the standard 28% debt-to-income ratio threshold for qualified borrowing. Even with a 10% down payment, the payment rises to $4,573, or 60.1% of monthly income. The high cost of capital significantly erodes affordability, as the 7.32% rate amplifies the monthly obligation compared to lower-rate environments. For context, the median monthly rent is $1,786, highlighting that housing costs for owners are more than double the cost for renters, further straining household budgets. Down payment barriers remain a critical hurdle. A 20% down payment on a $739,800 home requires $147,960 in cash, while a 10% down payment requires $73,980. Given the median household income, accumulating such reserves without sacrificing other financial goals is difficult for the average resident. Consequently, conventional financing is the most appropriate loan type for this market, as it is the only category that adequately covers the median price point. Prospective buyers should prioritize building substantial cash reserves and consider purchasing below the median price point to improve their debt-to-income ratios. The combination of high home values, limited FHA coverage, and elevated interest rates creates a restrictive environment where affordability is largely dependent on higher-than-median incomes or significant equity contributions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Ouray County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Ouray County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Ouray County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Ouray County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Ouray County qualifies.
