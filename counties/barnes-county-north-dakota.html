📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Financing coverage is strong: the FHA 1‑unit limit of $541,287 exceeds the median home price of $173,700 by 312%, and the conforming 1‑unit limit of $832,750 exceeds it by 479%, confirming that both loan programs comfortably cover typical purchases. Monthly payment burden is moderate; a 3.5% down FHA loan on the median‑priced home requires a $167,620 principal and incurs a P&I payment of $1,160 per month, representing 20.5% of the median household income of $68,038. A conventional 20% down loan on a $138,960 principal costs $962 per month, or 17.0% of income, both well below the 30% threshold commonly used to gauge affordability. The current 7.40% interest rate raises the payment relative to lower‑rate environments, yet the payment‑to‑income ratios remain manageable. If the rate were 5%, the FHA payment would fall to roughly $900 per month, illustrating the sensitivity to interest rate changes. For typical buyers, an FHA loan is the most suitable option because it requires only 3.5% down, stays within the FHA limit, and aligns with the income share observed. Conventional 10% down loans are also viable, requiring a $17,370 down payment that equals about 25% of annual income, while 20% down ($34,740) presents a greater barrier. VA and USDA programs are unnecessary given the price level and lack of high‑cost designations. The minimum down payment to purchase at the median price is $6,080 (3.5% of $173,700), which is feasible for households with modest savings. Given the median home price of $173,700 and the FHA limit that exceeds this value by more than threefold, an FHA loan with the minimum 3.5% down payment remains the most accessible financing option for typical buyers. Conventional 10% down loans are also feasible but require a $17,370 down payment, equivalent to roughly 25% of the median household’s annual income, which may be challenging for many households. The 8.2% five‑year price appreciation suggests continued upward pressure on values, reinforcing the benefit of securing a loan now. Practical takeaway: prospective buyers can secure homeownership with as little as $6,080 down using an FHA loan, making the median home affordable despite the elevated interest rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Barnes County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Barnes County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Barnes County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Barnes County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Barnes County qualifies.
