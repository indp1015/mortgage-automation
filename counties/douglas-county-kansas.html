📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA loan limit of $541,287 for a single‑unit property is **175 % of the median home price** ($309,400) in Douglas County, while the conforming ceiling of $832,750 reaches **269 %** of that price. Both limits therefore comfortably exceed the typical purchase price, and the median home is well within the FHA ceiling, eliminating any “high‑cost” restriction for conventional financing. At a 7.24 % 30‑year fixed rate, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) component for a median‑priced home is $2,034 with an FHA loan (3.5 % down) and $1,686 with a conventional loan (20 % down). The median household income of $69,746 translates to a monthly gross income of $5,812; the FHA payment consumes **35.0 %** of that income, while a 20 % conventional payment consumes **29.0 %**. Both ratios sit at the upper edge of the conventional 28‑30 % affordability guideline, indicating a modest but manageable burden for average earners. Given the price level and loan limits, a conventional loan with a 10‑% down payment ($278,460 loan) yields a P&I of $1,897, representing **32.6 %** of monthly income—still above the ideal threshold but lower than the FHA option. For first‑time buyers lacking substantial savings, the FHA’s 3.5 % down requirement ($10,829) is the smallest cash outlay, though the higher P&I and mortgage‑insurance premiums raise the overall cost. VA‑eligible borrowers would benefit from zero down and no mortgage insurance, producing the lowest P&I of roughly $1,900, while USDA financing is unavailable because the county is not designated rural. The 7.24 % rate inflates monthly payments by roughly $300–$400 compared with a 5 % environment, shrinking purchasing power and pushing many buyers toward larger down payments to keep the debt‑to‑income ratio below 30 %. **Practical takeaway:** For a median‑priced home, a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment keeps the P&I below 30 % of gross income and avoids mortgage‑insurance costs, making it the most financially efficient path for buyers who can secure the required $61,880 down payment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Douglas County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Douglas County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Douglas County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Douglas County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Douglas County qualifies.
