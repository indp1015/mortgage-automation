📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in Macon County, Tennessee, is characterized by substantial regulatory headroom, as the 2026 FHA one-unit limit of $541,287 covers 240% of the median home value of $225,700. Similarly, the conforming limit of $1,029,250 exceeds the median price by 456%. Consequently, loan limits do not constrain typical transactions in this market. Buyers are not facing high-cost county restrictions, and the median property value remains well below the thresholds that would trigger higher mortgage insurance premiums or limit eligibility for standard financing products. This ample coverage ensures that virtually all residential properties in the county qualify for both government-backed and conventional loan programs without requiring jumbo loan structures. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels, despite the aggressive appreciation observed in recent years. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan on a median-priced home requires a monthly principal and interest payment of $1,508. This figure represents 30.6% of the median household income of $59,177, which aligns with standard underwriting guidelines for debt-to-income ratios. For buyers opting for a conventional loan with a 20% down payment, the monthly P&I drops to $1,250, or 25.3% of median income. While the 15.1% five-year price CAGR has increased entry costs, the payment burden has not become prohibitive for the average household. The down payment barrier is the primary hurdle; an FHA loan requires 3.5%, or approximately $7,900, while a conventional 20% loan requires $45,140. The lower FHA threshold is significantly more feasible for buyers with limited savings, making it the preferred vehicle for first-time purchasers in this demographic. Rate sensitivity remains a critical factor in affordability. The current 7.40% rate significantly increases monthly obligations compared to lower-rate environments, effectively reducing purchasing power. However, because the median home value is modest, the absolute dollar impact of the rate is contained. For prospective buyers, the practical takeaway is that while price appreciation has been rapid, the combination of low median prices and generous loan limits preserves access to ownership. Buyers should prioritize FHA financing to minimize initial capital requirements, as the 3.5% down payment is a realistic barrier for households earning the local median income, whereas the 20% conventional down payment may require extended saving periods.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Macon County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Macon County is $669,012. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Macon County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,029,250 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Macon County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Macon County qualifies.
