📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in San Benito County presents a distinct structural advantage regarding loan limits. Both the FHA and conforming one-unit limits stand at $1,249,125, which covers 157% of the median home value of $793,400. This ample coverage ensures that the typical property in this high-cost county falls well within standard financing parameters, eliminating the need for jumbo loans for median-priced purchases. Consequently, buyers have access to a wide array of conventional and government-backed financing options without hitting regulatory ceilings. However, the monthly payment burden remains a significant challenge for the average household. With the 30-year fixed rate at 7.24%, the estimated principal and interest payment for an FHA loan on the median home is $5,217. This figure represents 54.7% of the median monthly household income, derived from an annual median income of $114,394. Such a ratio far exceeds the standard 28% threshold for front-end debt-to-income ratios, indicating severe affordability stress. Even with a 20% down payment on a conventional loan, the payment of $4,325 still consumes 45.4% of monthly income. These metrics suggest that the median household income is insufficient to service a mortgage on a median-priced home under current interest rate conditions, regardless of the loan type selected. Regarding loan type recommendations, conventional financing with a substantial down payment offers the lowest monthly obligation, yet it requires a $158,680 initial outlay for a 20% down payment. This sum is approximately 1.4 times the total annual median household income, making it a formidable barrier for local residents. FHA loans, while requiring only 3.5% down ($27,769), result in higher monthly payments that are equally unaffordable for the median earner. Therefore, no standard loan product currently aligns with the median income profile for the median price point. The primary practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that purchasing at the median price is currently financially unviable for typical local households. Buyers must either seek higher-income employment, purchase significantly below the median price point, or rely on non-standard financing structures to bridge the gap between current income levels and the high cost of debt service in this market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in San Benito County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in San Benito County is $1,249,125. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in San Benito County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,249,125 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in San Benito County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm San Benito County qualifies.
