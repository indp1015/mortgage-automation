📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Fremont County, Wyoming, presents a financing landscape where standard loan limits significantly exceed local property values, ensuring broad accessibility for typical buyers. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 201% of the median home value of $268,800, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 310%. Consequently, no buyer purchasing a median-priced home will encounter high-cost county restrictions or require jumbo loans. This ample coverage means that standard FHA and conventional products are fully viable options for the vast majority of transactions in this market. The primary challenge for prospective buyers is not loan eligibility but monthly payment affordability relative to income. With a median household income of $64,904, the estimated principal and interest (P&I) payment for an FHA loan at the median price is $1,795, representing 33.2% of monthly income. This ratio approaches the upper threshold of conventional lender guidelines, which typically prefer debt-to-income ratios below 36%. In contrast, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment results in a $1,488 monthly P&I payment, or 27.5% of monthly income, offering a more comfortable margin for additional housing costs such as taxes and insurance. The current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% exacerbates this burden compared to lower-rate environments; had rates been 5.0%, the FHA payment would have been approximately $1,380, reducing the income percentage to roughly 25.4%. Given these dynamics, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment is the most robust recommendation for buyers with sufficient savings, as it minimizes monthly obligations and avoids private mortgage insurance (PMI) premiums. For those with limited cash reserves, the FHA option remains a practical entry point, requiring only 3.5% down, or $9,408 at the median price. This down payment is feasible for many households, especially when considering the low absolute dollar amount relative to national averages. However, buyers must carefully model total housing costs, as the 33.2% P&I ratio leaves little room for error if interest rates remain elevated. The key practical takeaway is that while Fremont County’s low home prices make ownership accessible, the high interest rate environment demands strict budgeting to ensure that total monthly housing expenses do not exceed 30% of gross income.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Fremont County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Fremont County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Fremont County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Fremont County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Fremont County qualifies.
