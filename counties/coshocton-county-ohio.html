📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Financing coverage is adequate: the FHA 1‑unit limit of $541,287 equals 348 % of the median home price of $155,500, and the conforming 1‑unit limit of $832,750 equals 536 % of the median price, so both programs fully cover typical purchases. Monthly payment burden is significant. For a median‑priced home, the FHA loan (3.5 % down, $150,058 principal) requires a P&I payment of $1,038, which consumes 22.4 % of the median household income of $55,577. A conventional loan with 10 % down ($139,950 loan) yields a $968 payment (20.8 % of income), while a 20 % down loan ($124,400 loan) costs $861 (18.6 % of income). Thus, even the lowest‑payment conventional option still represents nearly one‑fifth of monthly earnings. Given the price level and loan limits, FHA financing is likely the best fit for typical buyers, as it permits a low down payment and its limit comfortably exceeds the median home value. Conventional loans remain viable for those able to provide at least 10 % down, and USDA or VA options could be considered only if eligibility criteria are met. The minimum down payment to purchase at the median price is 3.5 % ($5,438) for FHA, 10 % ($15,550) for conventional, and 20 % ($31,100) for a conventional 20 % down loan. A 3.5 % down payment is challenging but may be achievable with savings or assistance; higher down payments are generally infeasible for median‑income households. At the current 7.40 % interest rate, the FHA payment of $1,038 already represents over 22 % of income, reducing affordability compared with lower‑rate environments where the same loan at 5 % would drop to roughly $860, a 17 % reduction in monthly cost. Higher rates therefore increase the payment‑to‑income ratio and may limit qualification. Practical takeaway: prospective buyers should prioritize FHA financing to minimize upfront cash requirements while budgeting for a monthly payment that already consumes more than one‑fifth of their household income.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Coshocton County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Coshocton County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Coshocton County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Coshocton County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Coshocton County qualifies.
