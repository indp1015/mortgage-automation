📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in Decatur County, Kansas, is characterized by an extreme mismatch between federal loan limits and local property values. The 2026 FHA limit for a one-unit property stands at $541,287, which covers 605% of the local median home value of $89,500. Similarly, the conforming limit of $832,750 provides coverage equivalent to 930% of the median price. Consequently, credit limit constraints do not impede access to financing for the typical residence in this market. Buyers are not restricted by high-cost county designations or ceiling caps, ensuring that standard loan products remain fully accessible for the vast majority of housing stock. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels, despite the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan requiring a 3.5% down payment results in a principal and interest payment of $597 per month. This figure represents 13.3% of the median household income of $53,870, well within conventional underwriting guidelines. A conventional loan with a 20% down payment reduces the monthly P&I to $495, or 11.0% of income. While interest rates significantly higher than the 2020-2021 averages have increased absolute payment amounts, the low baseline home price in Decatur County mitigates this impact. The affordability ratio remains healthy, indicating that rate sensitivity is less acute here than in higher-cost metropolitan areas where the same rate would push payments into distress zones. Regarding loan type recommendations, conventional financing is generally optimal for buyers with sufficient cash reserves, as it avoids mortgage insurance costs and offers slightly lower monthly payments. However, FHA loans are highly competitive for first-time buyers due to the low 3.5% down payment requirement. The minimum down payment for the median home is approximately $3,132 for an FHA loan or $17,900 for a 20% conventional purchase. Given the median household income, saving $3,132 is a feasible short-term goal, whereas the $17,900 requirement may present a longer-term barrier. The practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that the primary hurdle is not loan eligibility or payment capacity, but rather the accumulation of initial capital for down payments and closing costs. With home values rising at a 7.1% five-year CAGR, early entry into the market is advantageous, as delayed purchases will require progressively larger down payments to secure the same price point.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Decatur County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Decatur County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Decatur County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Decatur County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Decatur County qualifies.
