📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Charleston County’s financing landscape is characterized by robust loan limit coverage, ensuring that standard mortgage products remain accessible for the typical buyer. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 111% of the $489,100 median home value, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 170%. Consequently, the median-priced home falls well within both FHA and conventional loan limits, eliminating the need for jumbo financing for the majority of transactions. This structural alignment means that pricing constraints, rather than loan availability, primarily dictate market entry for average-income households. The primary challenge lies in monthly payment burden relative to local income levels. With a 30-year fixed rate at 7.40%, the estimated principal and interest payment for a median home purchased with a 20% down payment is $2,709 per month. Against a median household income of $88,494, this payment consumes 36.7% of monthly earnings. For FHA buyers using the minimum 3.5% down payment, the loan amount rises to $471,982, increasing the monthly P&I to $3,267. This represents 44.3% of median monthly income, a level that exceeds the typical 30-35% debt-to-income threshold used by most lenders. While the 10% down conventional option reduces the payment to $3,047 (42.2% of income), it still presents a significant affordability hurdle for median-income earners in this market. Given these dynamics, conventional financing with a substantial down payment is the most viable path for qualifying, as FHA options push debt ratios too high for average earners. The minimum down payment barrier is substantial: a 3.5% FHA down payment requires $17,119, while a 20% conventional down payment requires $97,820. The latter is particularly difficult for first-time buyers without significant asset accumulation. The current 7.40% interest rate significantly exacerbates this burden; compared to a 4.0% rate environment, the monthly payment increases by approximately $1,000, effectively reducing the affordable price point by roughly $20,000. Prospective buyers should prioritize saving for a larger down payment to lower the loan principal, as this strategy is more effective at improving debt-to-income ratios than seeking lower-interest-rate products, which are currently limited in availability at this price point.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Charleston County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Charleston County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Charleston County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Charleston County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Charleston County qualifies.
