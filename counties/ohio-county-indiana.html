📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in Ohio County, Indiana, is characterized by substantial regulatory headroom relative to local property values. The 2026 FHA loan limit of $541,287 covers 254% of the median home value of $213,100, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 391% of that figure. Consequently, no typical buyer in this market faces a ceiling issue; standard loan programs accommodate the median-priced home with significant margin. This ample coverage ensures that financing constraints are driven by borrower qualification rather than loan limit restrictions, allowing for full access to both government-backed and conventional financing options. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable for the median household, though they represent a notable portion of income. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with a 3.5% down payment results in a principal and interest payment of $1,423, which equates to 24.3% of the median monthly household income derived from the $70,392 annual median. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces this burden to $1,180, or 20.1% of monthly income. These ratios fall within traditional affordability guidelines, suggesting that the median household can sustain these payments without exceeding standard debt-to-income thresholds. The primary barrier to entry is the initial capital required. The FHA minimum down payment of $7,459 is feasible for many buyers, whereas the conventional 20% requirement of $42,620 presents a significant hurdle. Given the local price point, FHA financing is likely the optimal fit for typical buyers, as it minimizes upfront cash requirements while maintaining reasonable monthly obligations. The current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% significantly impacts affordability compared to lower-rate environments. At this rate, the monthly payment for a median home is approximately 15-20% higher than it would be at historical lows of 3-4%. This rate sensitivity means that while the loan limits are generous, the cost of borrowing is the primary constraint on purchasing power. Prospective buyers should note that the 8.4% five-year price CAGR indicates a rapidly appreciating market, which may outpace income growth. A concrete takeaway for buyers is that securing a rate lock is critical; given the high interest rate environment, even a modest increase in rates will further erode the 24.3% payment-to-income ratio, potentially pushing typical buyers into higher risk categories. Buyers should prioritize FHA options to leverage the low down payment requirement while managing the higher monthly cost associated with the current interest rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Ohio County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Ohio County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Ohio County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Ohio County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Ohio County qualifies.
