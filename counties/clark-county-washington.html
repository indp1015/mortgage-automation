📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Given the median home price of $522,900, FHA financing is likely the best fit for typical buyers, as it allows a 3.5% down payment of about $18,300, which, while sizable, is attainable over time. Saving this amount at the median household income of $97,536 may take two to three years, but the low down‑payment requirement makes FHA attainable for many first‑time purchasers. Conventional loans require 10% ($52,290) or 20% ($104,580) down payments, making them less accessible for households with median incomes. At 7.40%, the monthly principal‑and‑interest payment of $3,493 equals 43% of the median monthly income of $8,128, highlighting a substantial affordability strain. A drop to 6% would reduce the payment to roughly $2,950, cutting the income share to 36% and improving affordability. VA and USDA financing are unnecessary for median‑price homes because FHA already meets the coverage and down‑payment needs. The 5‑year price CAGR of 13.9% indicates strong appreciation, meaning buyers who purchase now will likely see continued equity growth, reinforcing the value of homeownership despite the current payment burden. This dynamic makes timely purchasing a strategic consideration for prospective buyers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Clark County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Clark County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Clark County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Clark County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Clark County qualifies.
