📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Bonner County’s housing market presents a complex financing landscape where standard loan limits provide ample coverage, yet income constraints limit accessibility. The median home value of $487,900 sits comfortably below both the FHA 1-unit limit of $541,287 and the conforming limit of $832,750. Consequently, the typical local property does not require jumbo financing, as the FHA limit covers 111% of the median price and the conforming limit covers 171%. This structural alignment ensures that loan availability is not a barrier to entry for the average purchase, allowing buyers to utilize standard mortgage products without exceeding regulatory caps. However, the primary challenge lies in monthly payment burden relative to local earnings. With a median household income of $66,979, the estimated principal and interest (P&I) for an FHA loan on a median-priced home is $3,259 per month. This figure represents 58.4% of monthly income, significantly exceeding the conventional 28-30% debt-to-income threshold. Even with a 20% down payment on a conventional loan, the P&I payment of $2,702 consumes 48.4% of monthly income. These ratios indicate that the median income earner is largely priced out of the median market segment, particularly given the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%. Higher interest rates have amplified payment burdens; compared to lower-rate environments, the cost of carrying debt on a $487,900 asset has risen sharply, further straining affordability for local residents. For typical buyers, the most viable path involves maximizing down payments to reduce loan principal, though the minimum 3.5% FHA down payment of approximately $17,077 remains a significant initial capital requirement. Given the high price appreciation of 17.0% over the past five years, local incomes have not kept pace with asset values. Prospective buyers should prioritize properties at the lower end of the market or consider alternative financing structures that reduce monthly outlays. The practical takeaway is that while loan limits are sufficient, income adequacy is the critical constraint; buyers must either secure higher-income positions, utilize substantial down payments, or target homes priced well below the median to achieve sustainable debt-to-income ratios in this high-growth, high-cost market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Bonner County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Bonner County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Bonner County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Bonner County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Bonner County qualifies.
