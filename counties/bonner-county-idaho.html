📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA loan limit for a one‑unit dwelling ($541,287) exceeds the median home value in Bonner County ($487,900) by 11 %, and the conforming loan limit ($832,750) is 171 % of that price. Thus both FHA and conventional financing comfortably cover a typical purchase; the primary constraint is the borrower’s down‑payment capacity rather than loan‑size caps. At a 7.18 % 30‑year fixed rate, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment on a median‑priced home is $3,189 with an FHA loan (3.5 % down) and $2,644 with a conventional loan (20 % down). Both figures translate into a debt‑service burden of 57.1 % and 47.4 % of the median household income ($66,979), respectively—well above the conventional 28 % affordability guideline. Even the conventional 10 % down scenario ($2,974/month) consumes 53 % of income, indicating that the current rate environment imposes a substantial monthly strain on average households. Given the price level and loan limits, conventional financing with a 20 % down payment yields the lowest P&I burden and avoids mortgage‑insurance premiums, making it the most cost‑effective option for buyers who can meet the $97,580 down‑payment requirement. For purchasers lacking that cash, FHA financing remains viable because the loan limit covers the median price, but the required 3.5 % down ($17,077) still represents 25.5 % of the median household income, and the resulting payment exceeds half of monthly earnings. VA or USDA loans could be advantageous for eligible veterans or rural‑area buyers, as they allow zero down and lower monthly ratios, but eligibility is limited. A 7.18 % rate is roughly 2 percentage points higher than the 5‑year historic average, inflating monthly payments by about $300–$400 compared with a 5 % rate environment. Consequently, down‑payment size becomes the decisive affordability lever. **Practical takeaway:** Prospective buyers should aim to save at least a 20 % down payment; doing so reduces the P&I payment to $2,644/month—approximately 47 % of median income—and eliminates mortgage‑insurance costs, making homeownership attainable despite the elevated interest‑rate backdrop.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Bonner County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Bonner County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Bonner County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Bonner County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Bonner County qualifies.
