📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Suffolk County’s housing market presents significant financing challenges due to the divergence between median home values and federal loan limits. The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home is set at $541,287, which covers only 77% of the county’s median home value of $705,800. Consequently, the typical buyer cannot utilize an FHA loan for a median-priced property without exceeding the limit, effectively forcing a transition to conventional financing. In contrast, the conforming loan limit of $962,550 covers 136% of the median price, ensuring that standard conventional loans remain the primary viable option for most transactions in this high-cost area. Because the county is not designated as high-cost, no additional adjustments to these limits apply, leaving a substantial gap between affordable loan products and actual market pricing. The monthly payment burden further illustrates the strain on local incomes. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, the principal and interest payment for a conventional loan with 20% down on a median-priced home is $3,909. This amount represents 49.1% of the median monthly household income, derived from an annual income of $95,631. Even with a lower 10% down payment, the payment rises to $4,398, or 54.3% of monthly income. For comparison, an FHA loan on a lower-priced property would still result in a payment burden of 59.2% of income, highlighting that even subsidized options are difficult to sustain at current interest rates. The minimum down payment for a conventional loan at the median price is $70,580 (10%), a substantial barrier for first-time buyers, while the 20% requirement totals $141,160. Given these constraints, conventional financing is the most appropriate loan type for typical Suffolk County buyers, as FHA and VA loans are structurally insufficient for median-priced homes due to limit restrictions. The current 7.40% interest rate significantly reduces affordability compared to lower-rate environments, where monthly payments would be substantially lower relative to income. The rapid price appreciation, with a five-year CAGR of 10.4%, has outpaced income growth, exacerbating the affordability gap. A practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that purchasing at the median price requires a robust down payment and a household income well above the county median to maintain a sustainable debt-to-income ratio. Buyers should consider targeting properties below the FHA limit to access lower down payment options, or they must secure pre-approval for conventional loans with at least 10% down to remain competitive in this constrained market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Suffolk County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Suffolk County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Suffolk County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $962,550 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Suffolk County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Suffolk County qualifies.
