📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Iron County, Michigan, presents a distinct financing landscape where loan limits significantly exceed local property values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 496% of the $109,100 median home value, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 763%. Consequently, no buyer in this market faces high-cost county restrictions or limit caps for standard single-family purchases. This ample coverage ensures that financing availability is not a barrier for typical transactions, allowing borrowers to access all standard loan programs without hitting maximum thresholds. The primary affordability challenge lies in the monthly payment burden relative to local income. With a 30-year fixed rate at 7.40%, an FHA loan on the median-priced home requires a principal and interest payment of $728 per month. This amount represents 15.6% of the $55,940 median household income. While this ratio is below the conventional 20% threshold often cited by underwriters, it is notably higher than the 13.0% burden associated with a conventional loan requiring a 20% down payment. The current interest rate environment significantly impacts purchasing power; had rates been lower, the same loan amount would result in a substantially reduced monthly outlay. The rapid appreciation seen in recent years, including a 7.5% increase in 2024, has outpaced income growth, tightening the effective affordability ratio despite low absolute home prices. For typical buyers, the FHA loan is likely the most accessible option due to its 3.5% minimum down payment requirement. At the median price, this requires a cash outlay of approximately $3,819, which is feasible for many households with savings or assistance programs. In contrast, a conventional loan with 10% down requires $10,910, and a 20% down payment requires $21,820. While USDA loans may be available given the rural classification, the FHA’s lower down payment barrier makes it the primary vehicle for first-time buyers. The practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that while the home price is low, the high interest rate creates a monthly payment that consumes a significant portion of median income. Buyers should prioritize lowering the down payment requirement through FHA or VA options rather than seeking higher-priced homes, as the monthly P&I remains the dominant cost factor. Additionally, the high owner-occupancy rate of 84.5% suggests a stable market, but the rising price CAGR of 7.3% indicates that delaying entry may further erode affordability margins.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Iron County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Iron County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Iron County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Iron County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Iron County qualifies.
