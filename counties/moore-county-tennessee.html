📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Moore County, Tennessee, presents a favorable financing landscape where loan limits significantly exceed local median home values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 190% of the median home value of $285,300, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 292%. This ample coverage ensures that virtually all typical transactions in this market qualify for standard government-backed or conventional financing without requiring jumbo loans. Because the county is not designated as high-cost, these limits remain stable and accessible, providing a robust safety net for buyers navigating the local housing inventory. The primary challenge for residents remains the monthly payment burden relative to income. With a median household income of $66,469, the estimated principal and interest payment for an FHA loan on a median-priced home is $1,906, representing 34.4% of monthly income. This ratio approaches the upper threshold of conventional underwriting guidelines, which typically cap total debt-to-income ratios at 43-50%. In contrast, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment reduces the monthly P&I to $1,580, or 28.5% of income, offering a more sustainable financial position. Given the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, affordability is constrained compared to lower-rate environments. A rate reduction of just one percentage point would significantly lower monthly obligations, highlighting the market’s sensitivity to interest rate fluctuations. For typical buyers, the FHA loan is likely the most accessible entry point due to its 3.5% minimum down payment requirement, which amounts to approximately $9,986 on a median-priced home. This barrier is generally more feasible than the $28,530 required for a 10% conventional down payment or the $57,060 for 20%. However, buyers with stronger credit profiles and larger savings should consider conventional financing to avoid mortgage insurance premiums, which can offset the lower initial down payment cost of FHA loans. The high owner-occupancy rate of 82.5% suggests a stable, long-term investment environment, but the recent 11.4% five-year price CAGR indicates rapid appreciation that may outpace income growth. Prospective buyers should prioritize securing pre-approval before entering bidding situations, as the combination of rising prices and elevated interest rates requires precise budgeting to maintain debt ratios within lender guidelines.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Moore County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Moore County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Moore County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Moore County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Moore County qualifies.
