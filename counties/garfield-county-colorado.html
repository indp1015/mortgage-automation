📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Garfield County, Colorado, presents a financing landscape where loan limits offer substantial coverage relative to local property values. The 2026 FHA and conforming limits for one-unit properties stand at $1,209,750, a figure that covers 229% of the median home value of $527,800. This indicates that no buyer in the typical market segment faces loan limit constraints, as the median price falls well below the maximum allowable financing amount. Consequently, the primary barrier to entry is not regulatory cap restrictions but rather the absolute cost of mortgage payments relative to household earnings. With a median household income of $91,131, the affordability metrics reveal significant strain. An FHA loan with a 3.5% down payment results in a principal and interest payment of $3,471 per month, consuming 45.7% of the median monthly income. Even a conventional loan with a 20% down payment requires a $2,877 monthly payment, which still represents 37.9% of income. These ratios exceed the conventional 30% threshold for debt-to-income ratios, suggesting that typical buyers at the median income level face considerable financial pressure. Regarding loan type recommendations, conventional financing appears more viable for buyers who can secure higher down payments, as it reduces the monthly burden compared to FHA options. However, the minimum down payment barrier remains a critical hurdle. An FHA loan requires a minimum of $18,473 (3.5% of $527,800), while a conventional loan with 20% down requires $105,560. For a household earning $91,131 annually, saving $105,560 in cash is a substantial long-term commitment. If buyers cannot accumulate this capital, FHA loans provide a lower barrier to entry, though the higher monthly payment burden persists. The current 30-year fixed rate of 7.24% significantly exacerbates these costs. Compared to lower-rate environments, this rate increases monthly payments by hundreds of dollars, further compressing the debt-to-income ratio. For instance, a 100 basis point decrease in rates would reduce the 20% down conventional payment by approximately $200, improving the income ratio to a more manageable level. The practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that purchasing at the median price point requires either a household income significantly above the $91,131 median or a substantial reduction in other monthly debt obligations. Buyers should prioritize increasing their down payment to lower the loan-to-value ratio, which may qualify them for better interest rates or reduced private mortgage insurance costs. Additionally, exploring local down payment assistance programs could bridge the gap between the required cash outlay and available savings. Given the 10.3% five-year price CAGR, waiting may not be a viable strategy for those seeking entry-level ownership, as price appreciation continues to outpace typical income growth. Therefore, immediate action combined with rigorous budgeting for high monthly payments is essential for successful homeownership in this market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Garfield County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Garfield County is $1,209,750. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Garfield County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,209,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Garfield County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Garfield County qualifies.
