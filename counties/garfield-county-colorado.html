📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA and conforming loan limits in Garfield County are both $1,209,750 for a single‑family home, which is **229 % of the 2024 median home value of $527,800**. Consequently, the typical home is comfortably within the maximum loan size for either program; the limits are not a constraint for median‑priced purchases, and borrowers can finance the full price without seeking a high‑cost or jumbo loan. Using the 30‑year fixed rate of 7.18 %, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment on a median‑priced home is $3,450 with an FHA loan (3.5 % down, $509,327 loan) and $2,860 with a conventional loan at 20 % down ($422,240 loan). The median household income of $91,131 translates to a monthly gross income of $7,594. The FHA payment therefore consumes **45.4 %** of monthly income, while a conventional loan with 20 % equity requires **37.7 %**. Both ratios exceed the conventional 28 % “affordable housing” benchmark, indicating a significant payment burden for the average household. For most buyers, a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment is the most cost‑effective option because it eliminates the FHA mortgage‑insurance premium and yields a lower P&I amount. However, the required down payment of **$105,560** (20 % of $527,800) is sizable relative to the median income; saving this amount would take many years for a typical household. An FHA loan reduces the down payment to **$18,473** (3.5 %), making entry possible for buyers with limited savings, but the higher monthly payment and insurance costs offset some of the benefit. VA or USDA loans are not broadly applicable because the county is not a designated USDA rural area and VA eligibility is limited to veterans. The 7.18 % rate inflates monthly payments by roughly 30 % compared with a 5 % environment, pushing the affordability gap wider. Even with the same down payment, a 5 % rate would lower the conventional 20 % P&I to about $2,300, bringing the payment ratio close to the 30 % threshold. **Practical takeaway:** Prospective buyers who can secure a 20 % down payment will achieve the most affordable monthly outlay; otherwise, an FHA loan provides access but will require budgeting for a payment that exceeds 45 % of gross income.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Garfield County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Garfield County is $1,209,750. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Garfield County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,209,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Garfield County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Garfield County qualifies.
