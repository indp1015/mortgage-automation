📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Garland County’s median home value of $194,300 sits comfortably within both the 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 and the conforming limit of $832,750. The FHA ceiling covers 279% of the local median price, while the conforming limit covers 429%, indicating that standard loan programs fully accommodate typical market transactions without requiring jumbo financing. This ample coverage ensures that the vast majority of buyers can access conventional or government-backed loans for properties at the median price point. The primary challenge for residents is the monthly payment burden relative to income. With a median household income of $57,181, an FHA loan on the median-priced home requires a monthly principal and interest payment of $1,298. This figure represents 27.2% of monthly income, approaching the 28% threshold often used by underwriters as a maximum limit for front-end debt ratios. A conventional loan with a 20% down payment reduces this burden to $1,076, or 22.6% of income, offering a safer margin for additional debts such as auto loans or credit cards. Given the local price levels, FHA loans are likely the most accessible fit for first-time buyers due to the low 3.5% down payment requirement, which amounts to approximately $6,800. This initial capital outlay is significantly more feasible for local households than the $38,860 required for a conventional 20% down payment, making FHA the practical choice for those with limited savings. Current interest rates significantly impact affordability in this market. At a 7.40% fixed rate, the high monthly payments strain the budget of median-income households. Compared to lower-rate environments, the current rate increases the monthly payment burden by several hundred dollars, effectively reducing the maximum loan amount a buyer can qualify for. For instance, a buyer qualifying for a $1,200 monthly payment can afford a smaller loan at 7.40% than they could at 6.0%. Prospective buyers should focus on saving for a larger down payment to reduce the loan principal, as this is the most effective way to lower monthly obligations without relying on rate fluctuations. Additionally, buyers should verify that their total debt-to-income ratio remains below 43% to ensure loan approval, as the high payment-to-income ratio leaves little room for other financial obligations.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Garland County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Garland County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Garland County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Garland County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Garland County qualifies.
