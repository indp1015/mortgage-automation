📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Loudoun County’s housing market presents a complex financing landscape where loan limits are not the primary constraint, but income-to-payment ratios are critical. The 2026 FHA limit of $811,931 covers 109% of the median home value of $743,800, while the conforming limit of $1,249,125 covers 168%. Consequently, the standard median-priced home falls well within both FHA and conventional loan limits, eliminating the need for jumbo financing for typical transactions. This adequate coverage means that loan type selection should be driven by down payment capacity and credit profiles rather than price ceilings. The affordability challenge is evident in the monthly payment burden relative to local income. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan on a median-priced home requires a 3.5% down payment of approximately $26,033, resulting in a principal and interest payment of $4,969 per month. This amount consumes 32.8% of the median household income of $181,765. In contrast, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment ($148,760) reduces the monthly P&I to $4,119, or 27.2% of income. While both figures exceed the traditional 28% debt-to-income guideline for the FHA option, the conventional 20% scenario remains closer to standard underwriting thresholds. The high cost of entry is significant; the minimum FHA down payment of roughly $26,000 is manageable for many, but the 20% conventional down payment of nearly $149,000 represents a substantial barrier for first-time buyers. Given the local market dynamics, conventional financing is likely the best fit for buyers with sufficient savings, as it avoids the higher upfront mortgage insurance costs associated with FHA loans on high-value properties. The current 7.40% interest rate significantly impacts affordability; compared to a 5% rate environment, the monthly payment on a median home increases by approximately $1,000, effectively requiring an additional $120,000 in annual household income to maintain the same debt-to-income ratio. For prospective buyers, the practical takeaway is that waiting for rates to drop may not be viable if local price appreciation continues at the observed 9.4% five-year CAGR. Instead, buyers should focus on maximizing down payments to reduce loan principal and monthly obligations, or consider fixed-rate adjustment strategies that align with their long-term income stability, as the high interest rate environment makes monthly cash flow the primary determinant of affordability rather than loan limit eligibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Loudoun County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Loudoun County is $811,931. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Loudoun County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,249,125 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Loudoun County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Loudoun County qualifies.
