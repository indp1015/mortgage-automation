📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in St. Louis County, Missouri, demonstrates robust coverage for typical home purchases. The 2026 FHA limit for a one-unit property stands at $541,287, which covers 196% of the current median home value of $276,800. Similarly, the conforming limit of $832,750 exceeds the median price by 301%. Because the county is not designated as high-cost, these standard limits provide ample headroom, ensuring that the vast majority of residents can secure financing for a median-priced home without requiring a jumbo loan. This structural adequacy means that loan limits are not a primary barrier to entry in this market. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels, though the current interest rate environment exerts significant pressure. With a 30-year fixed rate at 7.40%, an FHA loan with 3.5% down on a median-priced home results in a principal and interest payment of $1,849. This represents 26.8% of the median household income of $82,936. For buyers opting for a conventional loan with 20% down, the payment drops to $1,533, or 22.2% of income. While both figures fall within general affordability guidelines, the higher rate significantly increases the cost of capital compared to historical lower-rate periods. Specifically, the 7.40% rate adds substantial monthly costs compared to the 3-4% ranges seen in previous years, effectively reducing purchasing power by several thousand dollars in total loan amount for the same monthly budget. Regarding loan type recommendations, conventional financing is likely the optimal fit for buyers with sufficient savings, as the 20% down payment option avoids mortgage insurance and lowers the overall monthly burden. However, for those with limited capital, FHA loans remain a viable alternative given the low 3.5% down requirement, which amounts to approximately $9,688 on a median-priced home. This down payment is feasible for many households, particularly given the high owner-occupancy rate of 69.2%. The primary barrier is not the loan limit or the down payment percentage, but rather the absolute dollar cost of the down payment combined with the high interest rate. Prospective buyers should prioritize saving for a larger down payment to reduce the loan principal, as even a 10% down payment on a conventional loan lowers the monthly P&I to $1,724, offering a meaningful reduction in long-term interest costs compared to lower-equity options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in St. Louis County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in St. Louis County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in St. Louis County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in St. Louis County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm St. Louis County qualifies.
