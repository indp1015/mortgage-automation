📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Ohio County, Kentucky, presents a distinct affordability profile where federal loan limits significantly exceed local market values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 446% of the median home value of $121,400, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 686%. Consequently, financing coverage is not a constraint for typical buyers, as even the most expensive standard loans remain well below regulatory caps. This ample headroom ensures that credit availability is determined by borrower qualification rather than pricing ceilings, allowing for flexible financing structures without triggering high-cost county restrictions. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan on the median-priced home requires a minimum down payment of $4,249 (3.5%), resulting in a principal and interest payment of $811. This represents 16.8% of the median household income of $57,798. Alternatively, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment of $24,280 reduces the P&I to $672, or 14.0% of income. While the 20% down payment is a significant upfront barrier, the lower monthly outlay provides greater financial stability. The 3.5% FHA down payment is more accessible, requiring only about 7.3% of one month’s median income, making it a viable entry point for first-time buyers with limited savings. Given the low median home value, FHA financing is likely the optimal fit for the typical buyer in Ohio County. The low down payment requirement aligns well with the region’s income levels, and the loan limit provides substantial surplus capacity. USDA loans may also be relevant if specific rural tract eligibility applies, though FHA offers broader availability. Rate sensitivity remains a critical factor; the 7.40% rate elevates monthly costs compared to historical environments, but the low absolute loan amounts mitigate this impact. For instance, a 1% rate reduction would lower the FHA payment by approximately $6, offering modest relief but not fundamentally altering affordability. The practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that while the 7.40% rate increases monthly obligations, the low median price of $121,400 keeps total financing costs low. Buyers should prioritize securing the lowest possible down payment to preserve liquidity, as the monthly debt-to-income ratios remain well within conventional underwriting guidelines for both FHA and conventional options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Ohio County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Ohio County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Ohio County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Ohio County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Ohio County qualifies.
