📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Washington County, Colorado, presents a distinct financing landscape where loan limits significantly exceed local property values. The 2026 FHA limit for a single-unit property stands at $541,287, which covers 239% of the median home value of $226,200. Similarly, the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 368% of the median price. Consequently, financing coverage is not a constraint for typical buyers in this market. The median home value sits comfortably within both FHA and conventional lending thresholds, ensuring that standard loan products remain accessible without requiring jumbo financing or specialized high-limit programs. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels, despite the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.32%. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with 3.5% down requires a principal and interest payment of $1,499 per month. This amount represents 26.8% of the median household income of $67,167. While this ratio is within the typical 28% threshold for maximum housing expense, it leaves limited room for property taxes, insurance, and maintenance costs. In contrast, a conventional loan with 20% down reduces the P&I payment to $1,243, or 22.2% of median income, providing greater financial flexibility. The current interest rate environment has increased monthly costs compared to historical lows, yet the affordability metrics remain stable due to the modest median home price. Given the local market dynamics, conventional financing is likely the optimal fit for qualified buyers who can secure a 20% down payment. This approach minimizes monthly outlays and avoids FHA mortgage insurance premiums, which are particularly impactful over long holding periods. For buyers with limited capital, FHA loans offer a viable alternative with a minimum down payment of $7,917 (3.5% of $226,200). This entry barrier is feasible for many households, representing less than two months of median income. However, the higher monthly payment associated with FHA financing requires careful budgeting. The rapid appreciation of home values, with a five-year CAGR of 13.5%, suggests that equity accumulation has been significant, potentially enabling existing homeowners to refinance or access home equity lines. Prospective buyers should prioritize securing a conventional loan if sufficient down payment funds are available, as the lower monthly burden enhances long-term cash flow stability in a small, appreciating market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Washington County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Washington County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Washington County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Washington County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Washington County qualifies.
