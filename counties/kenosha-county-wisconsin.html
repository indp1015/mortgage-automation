📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Kenosha County’s housing market demonstrates robust financing coverage, with the 2026 FHA loan limit of $541,287 covering 204% of the median home value of $265,500. Similarly, the conforming limit of $832,750 encompasses 314% of the median price, ensuring that typical transactions do not require jumbo financing. This ample headroom means standard loan products are sufficient for the vast majority of local purchases, eliminating the need for high-balance alternatives. Consequently, the primary constraint for buyers is not loan availability but rather the ability to meet income and down payment requirements under current market conditions. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with 3.5% down results in a principal and interest payment of $1,773, which constitutes 26.2% of the median household income of $81,239. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces this to $1,470, or 21.7% of income. Both figures fall within standard affordability guidelines, suggesting that debt-to-income ratios are the more critical hurdle than absolute payment size. Given the median home value is well below both FHA and conforming caps, conventional financing is generally the most efficient option for qualified buyers, offering lower upfront costs and no mortgage insurance premiums compared to FHA. However, for buyers with limited savings, the FHA’s 3.5% minimum down payment requirement of approximately $9,293 remains a significant barrier, whereas a 20% conventional down payment requires $53,100. The current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% significantly impacts affordability compared to lower-rate environments. At this rate, the monthly payment is substantially higher than it would be at historical averages, effectively reducing purchasing power. For instance, a 100 basis point drop in rates would reduce the FHA monthly payment by approximately $100. Prospective buyers should prioritize securing pre-approval at current rates to lock in affordability. A concrete insight for buyers is that while the median home price has risen 10.1% annually over the past five years, the stability of loan limits relative to price growth ensures that financing access remains broad. Buyers with credit scores above 740 should explore conventional options to avoid FHA mortgage insurance, while those with lower credit or minimal savings may find FHA loans more accessible despite the higher total cost over the loan term.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Kenosha County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Kenosha County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Kenosha County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Kenosha County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Kenosha County qualifies.
