📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA and conforming loan limits both comfortably exceed Montgomery County’s median home value of $171,800. The FHA 1‑unit ceiling of $541,287 is 315 % of the median price, while the conforming limit of $832,750 is 485 % of that price, confirming that virtually any buyer can obtain a loan that fully finances a median‑priced property without resorting to high‑cost or jumbo products. Monthly principal‑and‑interest (P&I) obligations for a median home are modest relative to household earnings. At a 30‑year fixed rate of 7.18 %, an FHA loan with 3.5 % down ($165,787 loan) yields a P&I payment of $1,123, representing 18.9 % of the median monthly income ($5,957). A conventional loan with a 20 % down payment ($137,440 loan) reduces P&I to $931, or 15.6 % of income. Both ratios fall well below the conventional affordability threshold of 28 % for housing costs, indicating that the current rate does not create an undue burden for median‑income households. Given the ample loan limits and the low down‑payment options, FHA financing emerges as the most accessible product for first‑time or lower‑cash buyers, requiring only $6,013 (3.5 % of $171,800). Conventional financing is attractive for borrowers who can allocate a 10 % down payment ($17,180) or more, as it eliminates mortgage‑insurance premiums and yields a lower monthly payment. USDA or VA loans could be viable for eligible rural or veteran purchasers, but the FHA’s minimal cash requirement and full coverage of the median price make it the default recommendation for the typical buyer. The 7.18 % rate, while higher than the sub‑3 % environment of the early 2020s, still permits affordable monthly payments because the loan‑to‑value ratios are low and the median home price is well under loan limits. Consequently, the primary barrier for many buyers remains the ability to amass a down payment; a 3.5 % contribution is feasible for many households, whereas a 20 % down payment ($34,360) may be prohibitive for a significant segment. **Practical takeaway:** Prospective purchasers can secure fully financed, affordable mortgages on median‑priced homes with as little as 3.5 % down, keeping housing costs under 20 % of median household income even at today’s 7.18 % rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Montgomery County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Montgomery County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Montgomery County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Montgomery County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Montgomery County qualifies.
