📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Dukes County presents a significant financing mismatch between standard loan limits and local property values. The median home value stands at $1,165,800, which exceeds the FHA 1-unit limit of $541,287 by a substantial margin. Consequently, the FHA limit covers only 46% of the median price, rendering it ineffective for typical transactions. In contrast, the conforming 1-unit limit of $1,249,125 covers 107% of the median value, indicating that conventional financing is the primary viable pathway for buyers targeting the median-priced home. While the market is not designated as high-cost, the disparity between FHA and conforming limits forces most buyers into the conventional category to access sufficient loan amounts without requiring a jumbo loan. The monthly payment burden remains severe relative to local income levels. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, the principal and interest payment for a median-priced home with a 20% down payment is $6,457. This figure represents 61.6% of the median household income of $125,786, far exceeding the standard 28-30% debt-to-income ratio threshold. Even with a 10% down payment, the payment rises to $7,264, further straining household budgets. The minimum down payment required to purchase at the median price using a conventional loan is $116,580 for a 10% down scenario, or $233,160 for a 20% down scenario. Given the median household income, accumulating such a down payment requires a substantial period of savings, creating a significant entry barrier for first-time buyers. Rate sensitivity plays a critical role in this affordability crisis. At 7.40%, the cost of debt is high, meaning that even modest price appreciation significantly increases monthly obligations. A lower rate environment would have reduced the P&I burden, but the current rates lock in high costs for the duration of the loan. For prospective buyers, the practical takeaway is that purchasing a median-priced home in Dukes County is financially prohibitive for households earning the local median income. Buyers must either seek higher-income partners, purchase significantly below the median price point, or explore alternative financing structures that do not rely solely on standard conforming limits. The data indicates that the typical market participant cannot service a mortgage on a median home without substantial external financial assistance or income well above the local median.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Dukes County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Dukes County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Dukes County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,249,125 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Dukes County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Dukes County qualifies.
