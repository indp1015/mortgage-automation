📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Berkeley County, South Carolina, presents a highly favorable financing environment where federal loan limits significantly exceed local median home values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 174% of the $310,300 median home price, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 268%. This substantial coverage ensures that virtually all standard residential properties in the county fall well within both FHA and conventional loan parameters. Consequently, borrowers are not constrained by high-cost county restrictions or limit caps, allowing for full access to standard underwriting guidelines without requiring jumbo loans or alternative financing structures for typical market transactions. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels, though the current 7.40% interest rate exerts noticeable pressure on cash flow. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with 3.5% down results in a principal and interest payment of $2,073, consuming 29.5% of the median household income of $84,358. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces this to $1,718, or 24.4% of income. While the FHA option approaches the upper threshold of typical debt-to-income ratios, the conventional option offers greater safety margin. The rapid appreciation of 13.5% CAGR over the past five years has increased entry costs, yet the strong median income supports continued affordability. The minimum down payment of approximately $10,860 for an FHA purchase is feasible for most local households, representing a modest barrier compared to the 20% conventional requirement of $62,060. Given the robust loan limit coverage and income levels, conventional financing is generally the optimal fit for buyers with sufficient savings, as it avoids FHA mortgage insurance premiums and offers lower total monthly costs. However, for first-time buyers with limited capital, FHA loans remain a viable entry point due to the low down payment requirement. The current 7.40% rate significantly impacts affordability compared to historical sub-4% environments, effectively reducing purchasing power by roughly 45% for a given income level. Prospective buyers should note that while the median home price has nearly doubled since 2017, the alignment of high incomes and high loan limits maintains market stability. A concrete takeaway for buyers is that opting for a conventional loan with 20% down, if capital permits, provides the most sustainable long-term financial position by minimizing monthly obligations and avoiding additional insurance costs in a high-interest-rate environment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Berkeley County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Berkeley County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Berkeley County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Berkeley County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Berkeley County qualifies.
