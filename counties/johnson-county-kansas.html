📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in Johnson County, Kansas, presents a robust structure for homebuyers, as the 2026 loan limits significantly exceed the local median home value of $391,200. The FHA one-unit limit of $541,287 covers 138% of the median price, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 213%. This ample coverage ensures that the typical buyer does not face high-balance loan restrictions, allowing for standard financing products without the complexities of jumbo loans. Consequently, both FHA and conventional options remain fully accessible for the majority of the market, providing flexibility in loan selection based on individual credit profiles and down payment capabilities. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to the local median household income of $109,208. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan with a 3.5% down payment results in a principal and interest payment of $2,613, representing 28.7% of monthly income. This figure sits near the upper threshold of conventional debt-to-income guidelines, suggesting that while feasible, it leaves limited room for other financial obligations. In contrast, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment reduces the monthly P&I to $2,166, or 23.8% of income, offering a more comfortable margin. The minimum down payment barrier for an FHA purchase is approximately $13,692, a sum that is generally attainable for households at the median income level, particularly when considering first-time buyer assistance programs. Rate sensitivity is a critical factor in this environment. The current 7.40% interest rate significantly elevates monthly costs compared to the sub-4% environments seen in recent years. For a median-priced home, this rate differential increases the monthly payment by roughly $1,200 compared to a 4% rate, effectively requiring a higher income level to maintain the same debt-to-income ratio. Therefore, conventional financing with a larger down payment is often the optimal recommendation for buyers with sufficient savings, as it mitigates the impact of high interest rates through a smaller loan principal. Prospective buyers should prioritize saving for a 20% down payment to secure lower monthly obligations, ensuring long-term financial stability in a market where price appreciation has outpaced income growth over the past five years.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Johnson County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Johnson County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Johnson County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Johnson County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Johnson County qualifies.
