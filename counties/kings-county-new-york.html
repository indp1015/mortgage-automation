📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Kings County presents a distinct financing landscape where the median home value of $905,000 exceeds the 2026 FHA single-unit limit of $541,287. Consequently, FHA loans cover only 60% of the median property value, rendering them insufficient for typical purchases without significant cash outlays to cover the difference. In contrast, the conforming loan limit of $1,209,750 covers 134% of the median price, ensuring that standard conventional financing remains fully accessible for the vast majority of transactions. This structural gap dictates that conventional loans are the primary vehicle for home acquisition in this market, as FHA and VA options are largely impractical for median-priced properties due to limit constraints. The monthly payment burden reveals significant affordability challenges for median-income households. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment on the median home results in a principal and interest payment of $5,012. This figure represents 74.9% of the median household income of $80,263, far exceeding standard debt-to-income guidelines. Even with a 10% down payment, the payment rises to $5,639, consuming nearly 85% of median income. For those attempting to use FHA financing, the required down payment would be substantial, and the resulting payment of $6,046 would equate to 90.4% of median income, making such a strategy financially untenable for the average resident. The high interest rate environment amplifies these costs, as a 7.40% rate significantly increases monthly obligations compared to historical lower-rate periods, further straining household budgets. Down payment barriers are the most critical obstacle for prospective buyers. To purchase at the median price with a conventional loan, a minimum down payment of $90,500 (10%) to $181,000 (20%) is required. Given the median household income, accumulating such capital is a multi-year endeavor for most residents, particularly when considering that the median monthly rent of $1,833 limits savings capacity. The low owner-occupancy rate of 29.5% reflects this structural barrier, indicating that many residents remain renters due to the high capital requirements and payment burdens. A practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that targeting homes below the $724,000 mark may allow for more manageable debt-to-income ratios with standard conventional financing. Alternatively, buyers must prioritize aggressive savings strategies to increase down payments, thereby reducing principal balances and monthly obligations. Without substantial savings or higher-than-median incomes, the combination of high home values and elevated interest rates creates a restrictive entry point for first-time buyers in Kings County.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Kings County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Kings County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Kings County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,209,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Kings County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Kings County qualifies.
