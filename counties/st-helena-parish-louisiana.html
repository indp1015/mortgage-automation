📝 Mortgage Market Notes

St. Helena Parish, Louisiana, presents a mortgage landscape where federal loan limits significantly exceed local home values, creating ample financing coverage for the typical buyer. The median home value of $77,300 is well within both the FHA 1-unit limit of $541,287 and the conforming limit of $832,750. Specifically, the FHA limit covers 700% of the median price, while the conforming limit covers 1077%. This vast disparity indicates that no buyer in this market faces a ceiling issue regarding loan eligibility based on property value alone. Consequently, the choice of loan type is driven less by limit constraints and more by down payment capability and credit profile. Given the low median price, the FHA option is likely the most accessible fit for typical buyers, as it requires only 3.5% down. In contrast, conventional loans typically require 10% to 20% down, which, while manageable in absolute terms, represents a larger upfront cash outlay relative to the median household income of $41,934. The monthly payment burden remains manageable despite the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with 3.5% down results in a principal and interest payment of $516 per month. This amount constitutes 14.8% of the median monthly household income, a ratio that falls comfortably within standard affordability guidelines. A conventional loan with 20% down yields a lower payment of $428, or 12.2% of monthly income, but requires a $15,460 down payment. The minimum down payment barrier for an FHA loan is approximately $2,706, a sum that is feasible for many local households, especially when considering potential down payment assistance programs. The high owner occupancy rate of 79.0% suggests that local buyers are successfully navigating these financial requirements. Rate sensitivity is a critical factor in this low-price market. At 7.40%, the interest cost is a significant portion of the total payment. If rates were to drop to 5.5%, the FHA payment would decrease to approximately $428, reducing the income ratio to 12.2%. Conversely, if rates rise to 8.0%, the payment increases to $537, pushing the burden to 15.5%. While the absolute dollar amounts remain low compared to national averages, the percentage impact on a $41,934 annual income is pronounced. Prospective buyers should prioritize securing a low interest rate, as even small fluctuations have a disproportionate effect on affordability relative to their income levels. The primary takeaway is that while home prices are low, the high interest rate environment necessitates careful budgeting to ensure that the total monthly housing expense, including taxes and insurance, does not exceed 30% of gross income.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in St. Helena Parish?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in St. Helena Parish is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in St. Helena Parish?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in St. Helena Parish?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm St. Helena Parish qualifies.
