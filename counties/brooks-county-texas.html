📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Financing coverage in Brooks County is ample. The Federal Housing Administration’s 1‑unit limit of $541,287 exceeds the median home value of $81,000 by 668 %, and the conforming loan limit of $832,750 exceeds it by 1,028 %, confirming that both programs can fully underwrite a typical purchase. The median household income of $47,764 translates to a monthly disposable income of roughly $3,980. The FHA‑backed payment for a median‑priced home—$541 per month principal and interest on a $78,165 loan at 3.5 % down—represents 13.6 % of monthly income. Conventional financing with 20 % down reduces the payment to $448 per month (11.3 % of income), while a 10 % down conventional loan costs $504 per month (12.6 % of income). Given the size of the median home relative to loan limits, an FHA loan is likely the most suitable option for typical buyers, as it requires only a 3.5 % down payment (≈$2,835) and keeps the debt service well below the 15 % threshold often cited for affordability. Conventional loans demand higher upfront cash—$8,100 for 10 % down and $16,200 for 20 %—which may pose a barrier for many households. The current 7.40 % interest rate inflates the FHA payment by about $74 compared with a 5 % rate ($467 versus $541), noticeably tightening cash flow. The required down payment of $2,835, or 3.5 % of the median home price, is a realistic target for buyers who can accumulate savings or qualify for local assistance, especially as home values have risen 5.5 % annually over the past five years. Practical takeaway: prospective buyers should prioritize an FHA loan to minimize down‑payment requirements while keeping monthly obligations under 14 % of income, thereby overcoming the modest down‑payment hurdle in this market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Brooks County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Brooks County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Brooks County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Brooks County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Brooks County qualifies.
