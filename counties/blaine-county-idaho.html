📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Blaine County’s housing market presents a distinct financing challenge due to the divergence between local home values and federal loan limits. The median home value of $735,300 exceeds the 2026 FHA limit of $541,287, meaning the FHA program covers only 74% of the typical property price. Consequently, buyers cannot utilize standard FHA financing for the median-priced home without significantly increasing their down payment to reduce the loan amount below the cap. In contrast, the conforming loan limit of $832,750 covers 113% of the median value, ensuring that conventional financing remains fully accessible for typical transactions. This structural mismatch effectively pushes the standard buyer profile toward conventional loans, as FHA and similar government-backed programs are financially impractical for the average property unless the buyer secures a price well below the market median. The monthly payment burden is substantial relative to local income levels. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment on the median home requires a principal and interest payment of $4,072. This figure represents 52.8% of the median monthly household income of $3,090 (derived from the $92,566 annual median). Even with a 10% down payment, the payment rises to $4,581, consuming nearly 60% of monthly income. Such ratios far exceed the standard 28% front-end debt-to-income guideline used by most underwriters, indicating that the median household income in Blaine County is insufficient to qualify for a median-priced home at current interest rates. Buyers must either secure significantly higher incomes, reduce the loan size through larger down payments, or target lower-priced properties to maintain qualifying debt ratios. Down payment barriers further complicate entry. A 20% down payment on a $735,300 home requires $147,066 in liquid assets, a sum that is difficult for many local households to accumulate. While a 10% down payment reduces this to $73,530, the resulting monthly payment remains prohibitive for the median income earner. The current 7.40% interest rate exacerbates these issues; compared to lower-rate environments, the higher cost of capital significantly increases monthly obligations, reducing purchasing power. For prospective buyers, the practical takeaway is that purchasing at the median price point is largely unattainable on median income without substantial external financial assistance or above-average earnings. Buyers should focus on properties priced closer to the FHA limit or consider jumbo loans if their income profile supports the higher debt load, while strictly evaluating their debt-to-income ratio against the 7.40% rate environment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Blaine County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Blaine County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Blaine County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Blaine County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Blaine County qualifies.
