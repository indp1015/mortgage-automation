📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Madison County, Nebraska, exhibits robust financing coverage, as both federal loan limits significantly exceed local median home values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 260% of the $208,500 median home price, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 399%. Consequently, no buyer in this market faces financing constraints due to loan ceilings. The primary affordability challenge stems from current interest rates rather than price caps. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, the principal and interest payment for a median-priced home varies by loan type. An FHA loan with 3.5% down results in a monthly payment of $1,393, which consumes 26.5% of the median household income of $63,128. In contrast, a conventional loan with 20% down reduces the payment to $1,154, or 21.9% of income. Both figures fall within standard debt-to-income guidelines, suggesting that typical buyers can qualify for financing despite the elevated rate environment. For the typical buyer, conventional financing with 20% down offers the most favorable monthly burden, though FHA remains a viable entry point for those with limited savings. The minimum down payment for an FHA loan is $7,298 (3.5% of $208,500), a relatively modest barrier for households with stable incomes. However, the 20% conventional down payment requires $41,700, which may necessitate longer savings periods or gift funds. The current 7.40% rate significantly impacts affordability compared to historical lows; a 100 basis point decrease would reduce the FHA payment by approximately $85 monthly, improving the income ratio to 25.1%. Conversely, the recent 5-year price CAGR of 10.6% indicates that home values have outpaced income growth, making the entry price point a more significant barrier than the monthly payment itself. Prospective buyers should prioritize maximizing down payments to offset the high interest rate. At the median price, moving from 3.5% to 20% down reduces the monthly payment by $239, a substantial improvement for long-term budgeting. Additionally, given the 65.4% owner occupancy rate and steady appreciation, buyers should act promptly to avoid further price increases. The data suggests that while financing limits are not a constraint, the combination of rising home values and high interest rates requires careful cash flow planning. Buyers with incomes above the median will find the 26.5% FHA payment ratio more manageable, while those at or below the median should explore local down payment assistance programs to bridge the gap between savings and the required 3.5% minimum. Ultimately, the market remains accessible, but the cost of capital is the dominant variable affecting monthly affordability.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Madison County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Madison County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Madison County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Madison County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Madison County qualifies.
