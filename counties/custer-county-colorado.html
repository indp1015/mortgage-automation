📝 Mortgage Market Insight

Custer County, Colorado, presents a favorable financing landscape where federal loan limits significantly exceed local median home values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 138% of the $392,100 median home price, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 212%. This ample coverage ensures that the typical buyer can secure financing for a median-priced property without requiring a jumbo loan. Consequently, both FHA and conventional options remain accessible, providing robust flexibility for diverse credit profiles. However, the monthly payment burden remains substantial relative to local income levels. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, the estimated principal and interest payment for an FHA loan with 3.5% down is $2,619, representing 43.2% of the median household income of $72,674. Even with a 20% down payment on a conventional loan, the payment of $2,171 consumes 35.8% of monthly income. These figures exceed the standard 28% debt-to-income threshold often preferred by lenders, indicating that affordability is primarily constrained by income levels rather than loan availability. The minimum down payment for an FHA loan at the median price is approximately $13,724, which is a significant but manageable barrier for qualified applicants. Given the high price appreciation of 9.6% annually over the past five years, the conventional loan is likely the optimal choice for buyers with sufficient savings, as it avoids FHA mortgage insurance premiums. For buyers with limited down payment funds, the FHA loan remains a viable entry point, though the higher ongoing cost due to mortgage insurance may strain budgets. The current 7.40% interest rate significantly impacts affordability; compared to historical lows, this rate increases monthly payments by hundreds of dollars, effectively reducing the maximum affordable price for a median-income household. Prospective buyers should prioritize saving for a larger down payment to reduce the loan principal, thereby lowering the monthly payment and improving debt-to-income ratios. Additionally, exploring local down payment assistance programs could mitigate the initial capital barrier, making homeownership more feasible in this rapidly appreciating market. The combination of high loan limits and rising prices suggests that while financing is available, income growth or additional savings are critical for sustainable homeownership.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Custer County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Custer County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Custer County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Custer County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Custer County qualifies.
