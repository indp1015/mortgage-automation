📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA 1‑unit loan limit of **$541,287** is 138 % of Custer County’s median home price of **$392,100**, while the conforming ceiling of **$832,750** is 212 % of that price. Both limits comfortably exceed the typical purchase price, so financing availability is not constrained by loan caps; the median home is well within the FHA ceiling and far below the conforming ceiling. At the current 30‑year fixed rate of **7.18 %**, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment on a median‑priced home is **$2,563** for an FHA loan (3.5 % down) and **$2,124** for a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment. With a median household income of **$72,674** (≈$6,056 monthly), the FHA payment represents **42.3 %** of monthly income, exceeding the conventional 20 % benchmark of **35.1 %**. Both ratios are above the traditional 28 % housing‑cost threshold, indicating a moderate affordability strain, especially for buyers relying on minimal down payments. For most purchasers, a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment is the most cost‑effective option because it reduces the loan balance to **$313,680**, lowers the P&I to **$2,124**, and eliminates the FHA mortgage‑insurance premium. However, the required down payment of **$78,420** (20 % of $392,100) may be prohibitive for many local households; the median annual income would need to be saved for more than a year without accounting for other expenses. A 10 % down conventional loan (down payment **$39,210**) yields a P&I of **$2,390**, still above the 28 % guideline but more attainable. FHA financing remains a viable fallback for buyers lacking the cash for a 10 % conventional down payment, despite the higher payment‑to‑income ratio. The 7.18 % rate inflates monthly obligations by roughly **$300–$400** compared with a 5 % environment, shrinking purchasing power and raising the minimum viable down payment to keep housing costs within affordable limits. **Practical takeaway:** Prospective buyers should target at least a 10 % down payment and consider a conventional loan; doing so keeps the P&I near the 40 % income threshold and avoids the added cost of FHA mortgage‑insurance, making the median home marginally affordable in today’s high‑rate market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Custer County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Custer County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Custer County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Custer County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Custer County qualifies.
