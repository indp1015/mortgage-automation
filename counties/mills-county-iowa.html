📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA 1‑unit loan limit of $541,287 is more than twice the median home value of $237,800, and the conforming ceiling of $832,750 exceeds it by roughly 350 %. Consequently, both programs comfortably cover the typical property in Mills County; the FHA limit is especially generous, covering 228 % of the median price, while the conforming limit provides ample headroom for higher‑priced homes or future appreciation. At the current 30‑year fixed rate of 7.24 %, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment on a median‑priced home is $1,563 under an FHA loan (3.5 % down, $229,477 loan) and $1,296 under a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment ($190,240 loan). Relative to the median household income of $90,753 (≈$7,563 monthly), the FHA payment represents 20.7 % of monthly earnings, while a conventional loan with 20 % equity consumes 17.1 % of income. Both ratios fall below the conventional affordability threshold of 28 % for housing costs, indicating that, despite the high interest rate, the median buyer can sustain the debt service without undue strain. Given the low median price, high owner‑occupancy rate (83.5 %), and ample loan limits, a conventional loan with a 10 % down payment ($214,020 loan, $1,458 P&I) is likely the most cost‑effective option for typical buyers. It avoids mortgage‑insurance premiums required on FHA loans and yields a lower monthly payment than a 3.5 % FHA down payment. The minimum cash outlay to purchase at the median price is $8,323 (3.5 % for FHA) or $23,780 (10 % conventional); the latter is more realistic for households with median income, especially when combined with available down‑payment assistance programs. The 7.24 % rate inflates monthly payments by roughly $200–$300 compared with a 5 % environment, compressing affordability margins but not pushing them beyond conventional limits because the underlying home prices remain modest. **Practical takeaway:** prospective buyers should target a 10 %–20 % conventional down payment to minimize total cost and leverage the ample conforming limit, thereby securing a payment that remains comfortably below 20 % of monthly income even at today’s elevated rates.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Mills County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Mills County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Mills County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Mills County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Mills County qualifies.
