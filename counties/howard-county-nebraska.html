📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Howard County, Nebraska, presents a distinct mortgage financing landscape where federal loan limits significantly exceed local market valuations. The 2026 FHA limit for a one-unit property stands at $541,287, covering 259% of the median home value of $209,100. Similarly, the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 398% of the median price. Consequently, no buyer in this market faces a high-cost county designation or limit constraints. The median home value is well within the threshold for standard financing programs, ensuring that typical buyers have access to all major loan types without requiring jumbo loans. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan with 3.5% down requires a principal and interest payment of $1,397 per month. This amount represents 21.8% of the median household income of $76,767. For conventional borrowers, a 20% down payment reduces the monthly P&I to $1,158, or 18.1% of income. These ratios fall within standard affordability guidelines, indicating that the current interest rate environment does not prohibit entry for median-income households. The primary barrier is not the monthly debt-to-income ratio, but rather the initial capital required. The minimum down payment for an FHA loan at the median price is $7,319, while a conventional loan with 20% down requires $41,820. Given the median household income, saving $7,319 is a feasible short-term goal, whereas the larger conventional down payment represents a significant multi-year savings target. Therefore, FHA financing is likely the most appropriate vehicle for typical buyers in Howard County. It offers the lowest barrier to entry with a minimal 3.5% down payment requirement, allowing buyers to leverage their existing equity or savings more efficiently. The high price appreciation recorded between 2019 and 2023, with a 5-year CAGR of 11.8%, has increased the absolute dollar amount needed for down payments, even as percentage-based affordability remains stable. Prospective buyers should prioritize FHA options to minimize upfront cash requirements. The practical takeaway is that while interest rates of 7.40% elevate monthly costs compared to historical lows, the low median home price in Howard County ensures that total housing costs remain proportionate to local incomes. Buyers should focus on assembling the modest down payment required for FHA loans, as this is the primary gatekeeper for market entry rather than monthly payment capacity.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Howard County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Howard County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Howard County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Howard County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Howard County qualifies.
