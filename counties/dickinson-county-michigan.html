📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Dickinson County’s housing market is characterized by significant financing headroom, as the 2026 FHA loan limit of $541,287 covers 353% of the median home value of $153,300. Similarly, the conforming limit of $832,750 exceeds the median price by 543%. This substantial coverage ensures that virtually all typical transactions in the county fall well below federal and agency caps, eliminating loan limit constraints for the average buyer. The market’s rapid appreciation, with a 10.7% five-year CAGR and a 17.7% year-over-year increase in 2024, has not pushed median prices into high-cost territory, keeping financing options broad and accessible for local residents. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan on the median-priced home requires a monthly principal and interest payment of $1,024. This figure represents 18.9% of the median household income of $65,156, a ratio that aligns with standard underwriting guidelines for debt-to-income ratios. For buyers opting for conventional financing with a 20% down payment, the monthly payment drops to $849, or 15.6% of income. While the 2024 price surge has increased absolute costs, the high owner-occupancy rate of 82.4% suggests a stable local demand base that supports these payment structures. Regarding loan type recommendations, conventional financing is generally the most cost-effective for buyers with sufficient savings, as it avoids mortgage insurance premiums associated with FHA loans. However, for those with limited cash reserves, the FHA option remains viable due to its low 3.5% down payment requirement. At the median price, this amounts to a minimum down payment of approximately $5,366. This threshold is feasible for many households, particularly given the county’s median rent of $797, which allows for rapid savings accumulation. The current 7.40% interest rate significantly impacts affordability compared to historical lows; a shift to a 6.0% rate would reduce the FHA payment by roughly $100 per month, highlighting the sensitivity of monthly obligations to rate fluctuations. Prospective buyers should prioritize building down payment capital to minimize monthly outflows, as the current rate environment amplifies the cost of borrowing. Ultimately, the combination of low median prices and high loan limits creates a favorable landscape for first-time and repeat buyers, provided they can navigate the elevated interest rate environment through careful budgeting and loan selection.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Dickinson County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Dickinson County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Dickinson County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Dickinson County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Dickinson County qualifies.
