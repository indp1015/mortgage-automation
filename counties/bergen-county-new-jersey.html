📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Bergen County’s median home value of $623,000 presents a distinct financing challenge for typical buyers. The 2026 FHA loan limit of $541,287 covers only 87% of the median price, meaning a standard FHA loan is insufficient to finance a median-priced home without exceeding the limit. Conversely, the conforming loan limit of $1,209,750 covers 194% of the median value, providing ample headroom. Consequently, conventional financing is the primary vehicle for most transactions in this market, as FHA options are largely restricted to lower-priced properties or require significant price adjustments that do not reflect the current median. Monthly payment burdens remain substantial relative to local income levels. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment on a median home results in a principal and interest payment of $3,450. This equates to 33.2% of the median household income of $124,884. While this ratio is within conventional underwriting guidelines, it leaves limited disposable income for property taxes, insurance, and maintenance. For buyers opting for a 10% down payment, the payment rises to $3,882, or 37.5% of income, further straining household budgets. The high interest rate significantly increases the cost of borrowing compared to previous years; a 100 basis point decrease would reduce the 20% down payment to approximately $3,250, highlighting the direct impact of rate sensitivity on affordability. Down payment barriers are the most significant hurdle for entry-level buyers. To purchase at the median price with a conventional loan, a minimum 20% down payment of $124,600 is required to avoid private mortgage insurance, a sum that represents nearly a full year of median household income. Even with a 10% down payment of $62,300, the barrier remains high for first-time buyers. Given that the median rent is $1,914, the jump to a mortgage payment of $3,450 or higher represents a significant increase in housing costs. Prospective buyers should prioritize conventional loans with larger down payments to mitigate monthly debt service ratios, as FHA financing is structurally misaligned with the current median price point. Strategic timing and savings accumulation remain critical, as the combination of high prices and elevated interest rates compresses the window of affordability for the average household in Bergen County.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Bergen County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Bergen County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Bergen County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,209,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Bergen County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Bergen County qualifies.
