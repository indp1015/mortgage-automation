📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Johnson County, Arkansas, presents a highly accessible financing landscape where federal loan limits significantly exceed local market values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 400% of the median home value of $135,300, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 615%. Consequently, no typical buyer in this market faces high-cost county restrictions or limit-driven financing constraints. The primary financial challenge stems not from loan availability, but from the current interest rate environment and income levels. With a median household income of $45,995, the monthly principal and interest (P&I) payment for an FHA loan at the median price is estimated at $904. This represents 23.6% of gross monthly income, a figure that sits comfortably within the standard 28% debt-to-income guideline, though it leaves less room for taxes, insurance, and other debts compared to lower-rate periods. Regarding loan type suitability, the FHA option is likely the most practical fit for typical local buyers. The minimum down payment of 3.5% requires an outlay of approximately $4,736, which is substantially more feasible than the 20% down payment ($27,060) required for optimal conventional terms. While USDA loans may also be applicable given the rural classification of parts of the county, the FHA’s broad availability and manageable down payment barrier make it the default recommendation for first-time buyers. The 10% down conventional option, requiring a $13,530 down payment, offers a slightly lower monthly P&I of $843, but the upfront capital requirement is nearly triple that of the FHA route. Rate sensitivity remains a critical factor in this analysis. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, the monthly payment burden is elevated relative to historical averages. Had rates been lower, the payment-to-income ratio would have improved, potentially allowing buyers to stretch their purchasing power or reduce the required down payment. However, even at 7.40%, the median home remains affordable for the median earner. The 7.2% five-year price CAGR indicates sustained appreciation, meaning that delaying entry increases the absolute dollar amount of the required down payment. Prospective buyers should prioritize securing pre-approval at current rates, as the low entry barrier of $4,736 for an FHA loan remains the most accessible path to homeownership in a market where prices are rising faster than incomes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Johnson County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Johnson County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Johnson County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Johnson County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Johnson County qualifies.
