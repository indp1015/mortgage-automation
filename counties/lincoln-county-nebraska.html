📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Lincoln County, Nebraska, presents a financing landscape where loan limits significantly exceed typical property values, ensuring broad eligibility for standard mortgage products. The 2026 FHA one-unit limit of $541,287 covers 286% of the median home value of $189,500, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 439%. Consequently, no borrower purchasing a median-priced home in this market faces restrictions due to loan caps. The county is not designated as high-cost, further simplifying the financing process for local residents. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels, though interest rates exert notable pressure. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, the principal and interest payment for an FHA loan with 3.5% down is estimated at $1,266, representing 23.3% of the median household income of $65,148. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces this burden to $1,049, or 19.3% of income. While both figures sit within typical debt-to-income guidelines, the higher rate environment means that the same loan amount costs substantially more than it would have in lower-rate periods. Comparing the 2017 median price of $130,800 to the current $189,500, the 7.7% annual growth rate has increased entry costs, making the current interest rate a critical factor in overall affordability. For typical buyers, the FHA loan is likely the most accessible option due to its low 3.5% down payment requirement, which amounts to approximately $6,633 at the median price. This is significantly more feasible than the $37,900 required for a 20% conventional down payment. However, buyers should note that FHA loans include mortgage insurance premiums, which can elevate the total monthly payment beyond the principal and interest figures cited. Given the stable price appreciation and strong income-to-payment ratios, the primary barrier to entry is the initial cash outlay for the down payment and closing costs rather than ongoing monthly debt service. Prospective buyers with limited savings should prioritize FHA or VA options to minimize upfront capital requirements, while those with larger reserves may benefit from the lower total interest costs associated with conventional financing. The data indicates that despite rising prices and elevated interest rates, the median household in Lincoln County retains sufficient income capacity to service standard mortgage debt without exceeding prudent financial thresholds.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Lincoln County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Lincoln County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Lincoln County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Lincoln County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Lincoln County qualifies.
