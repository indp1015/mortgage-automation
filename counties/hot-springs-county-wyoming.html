📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Hot Springs County, Wyoming, presents a financing landscape where loan limits significantly exceed local property values, ensuring broad access to standard mortgage products. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 238% of the median home value of $227,500, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 366%. Consequently, virtually all typical transactions in this market fall well within both FHA and conventional boundaries, eliminating the need for jumbo loans. This ample coverage means that financing availability is not a primary constraint for buyers seeking median-priced homes, allowing for a wide variety of loan structures to meet individual financial profiles. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels, though the current interest rate environment exerts noticeable pressure. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, the estimated principal and interest payment for an FHA loan on a median-priced home is $1,520 per month. This figure represents 29.8% of the median monthly household income of $2,042 (derived from the $61,250 annual median income), approaching the upper threshold of conventional debt-to-income ratios. In contrast, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment requires a $1,260 monthly payment, or 24.7% of income, offering a more comfortable margin. The 9.0% five-year price CAGR has driven values from $147,700 in 2017 to $227,500 in 2024, increasing the absolute dollar amount required for down payments despite stable income growth. For typical buyers, the FHA loan appears to be the most accessible entry point due to its 3.5% minimum down payment requirement, which amounts to approximately $7,963 for a median-priced home. This low barrier is feasible for many households, particularly given the moderate cost of entry compared to national averages. However, the high 7.40% interest rate significantly impacts long-term affordability compared to lower-rate periods, making the choice between FHA and conventional loans critical. Buyers with sufficient savings should consider conventional loans to reduce the loan principal and monthly outlay. A concrete takeaway for prospective buyers is that while the low down payment requirement facilitates entry, the high interest rate necessitates careful budgeting; securing a rate below 7.0% or increasing the down payment to 20% would substantially improve the debt-to-income ratio, enhancing overall financial stability in this rapidly appreciating market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Hot Springs County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Hot Springs County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Hot Springs County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Hot Springs County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Hot Springs County qualifies.
