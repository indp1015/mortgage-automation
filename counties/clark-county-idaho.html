📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA and conforming loan limits both comfortably exceed the median home price of $216,900 in Clark County. The FHA 1‑unit limit of $541,287 is 2.5 times the median price, while the conforming ceiling of $832,750 is 3.8 times higher, confirming that financing capacity is not a constraint for a typical purchase. At the current 30‑year fixed rate of 7.24 %, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) obligation for a median‑priced home is $1,426 with an FHA loan (3.5 % down, $209,308 loan) and $1,182 with a conventional loan funded at 20 % down ($173,520 loan). Relative to the median household income of $55,208 (≈$4,600 monthly), the FHA payment represents 31.0 % of monthly earnings, whereas a 20 % conventional payment is 25.7 % of earnings. Both ratios sit near the conventional 28‑30 % affordability threshold, but the conventional option leaves a larger cushion for taxes, insurance, and reserves. For most buyers, a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment is the most efficient fit because the loan amount remains well below the conforming limit and yields the lowest P&I cost. FHA financing is viable for borrowers who cannot amass the $43,380 down payment required for a 20 % conventional loan, but it carries higher monthly costs and mortgage‑insurance premiums. USDA or VA loans could be alternatives for eligible rural or veteran purchasers, yet the median price already falls under the USDA income caps, making USDA a potentially zero‑down option for qualified households. The minimum cash outlay to acquire a median home is $43,380 (20 % down) for a conventional loan or $7,592 (3.5 % down) for FHA. Given the county’s median household income, a 20 % down payment represents roughly 9 % of annual earnings, a level that may be attainable through savings or assistance programs, whereas the FHA down payment is only 1.6 % of annual income and thus more accessible for cash‑constrained buyers. Because the 7.24 % rate is more than 2 percentage points above the historically low‑rate environment of the early 2020s, monthly obligations are roughly 15 % higher than they would be at a 5 % rate, reducing overall purchasing power. **Practical takeaway:** Prospective buyers who can secure a 20 % down payment should prioritize a conventional loan to minimize monthly costs and avoid mortgage‑insurance fees; those lacking that cash should consider FHA financing as a viable bridge while planning to refinance if rates decline.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Clark County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Clark County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Clark County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Clark County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Clark County qualifies.
