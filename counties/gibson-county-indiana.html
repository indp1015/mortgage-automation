📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Gibson County, Indiana, presents a highly accessible financing landscape where federal loan limits significantly exceed local market values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 301% of the median home value of $180,000, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 463%. Consequently, no typical buyer in this market faces restrictions due to loan ceiling caps. The median home value has appreciated at an 11.2% compound annual growth rate over the past five years, rising from $105,700 in 2017 to $180,000 in 2024. Despite this rapid appreciation, the market remains firmly within standard financing parameters, ensuring that the majority of transactions can utilize conventional or government-backed loans without requiring jumbo financing. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan on the median-priced home requires a 3.5% down payment, resulting in a principal and interest payment of $1,202 per month. This figure represents 21.3% of the median household income of $67,573, a ratio that generally aligns with standard debt-to-income qualification thresholds. For buyers opting for a conventional loan with 20% down, the payment drops to $997, or 17.7% of income. The minimum down payment barrier is low; an FHA buyer needs only $6,300, while a conventional buyer with 10% down requires $18,000. Given the median income, these amounts are feasible for many local households, particularly those with savings or assistance programs. The primary challenge for prospective buyers is rate sensitivity. The current 7.40% interest rate significantly increases monthly costs compared to the sub-4% environments seen in previous years. At these rates, the cost of borrowing is the dominant factor in affordability, rather than the home price itself. A shift to conventional financing with higher equity reduces the total loan amount, thereby mitigating the impact of the high interest rate. For the typical Gibson County buyer, the FHA loan offers the lowest entry barrier due to the minimal down payment requirement, though conventional loans provide long-term savings through lower total interest costs. Buyers should prioritize maximizing their down payment to offset the high interest rate, as the local market’s affordability is less constrained by loan limits and more by the current cost of capital. The high owner-occupancy rate of 76.7% suggests strong local demand, but the financial structure remains favorable for entry-level purchases.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Gibson County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Gibson County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Gibson County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Gibson County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Gibson County qualifies.
