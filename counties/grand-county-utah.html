📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Financing coverage in Grand County is sufficient for most buyers. The Federal Housing Administration’s 1‑unit limit of $541,287 exceeds the median home value of $538,700, allowing an FHA loan to fully finance a typical property. Conforming loan limits of $839,500 represent 156 % of the median price, so both programs provide adequate coverage. Monthly payment burden is significant. For a median‑priced home with a 3.5 % down payment, the FHA loan on $519,846 carries a principal‑and‑interest payment of $3,599, equal to 64.4 % of the median household income of $67,106. A conventional loan with 10 % down on $484,830 results in a $3,356 payment (50.0 % of income), while a 20 % down conventional loan on $430,960 reduces the payment to $2,983 (44.4 % of income). The current 7.40 % interest rate raises the monthly payment by roughly $240–$300 compared with a 6 % rate, markedly reducing affordability relative to lower‑rate environments. At 7.40 %, the annual interest cost on the median‑priced home exceeds $30,000, further straining household budgets. The minimum down payment required is 3.5 % for FHA or 10 % for conventional; a 3.5 % down payment on the median price equals $18,887, which is feasible but still a substantial portion of savings. Given the price level and loan limits, a conventional loan with at least a 10 % down payment appears most suitable for typical buyers, as it avoids mortgage‑insurance premiums and stays within conforming limits. Prospective buyers should prioritize saving for this down payment to keep monthly costs near 50 % of income and preserve purchasing power in the high‑rate market. Therefore, buyers should aim to accumulate a 10 % down payment to avoid FHA mortgage insurance and keep monthly costs around half of their household income. A 20 % down payment reduces the loan‑to‑value ratio to 77 %, improving long‑term equity buildup and lowering the risk of negative amortization. This approach maximizes affordability while preserving financial flexibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Grand County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Grand County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Grand County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $839,500 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Grand County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Grand County qualifies.
