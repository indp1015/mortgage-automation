📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA loan limit for a one‑unit property ($541,287) is **195 % of the median home price** ($278,100), while the conforming ceiling ($832,750) is **300 % of that price**. Both limits comfortably exceed the typical purchase price, confirming that the statutory caps are not a constraint for most buyers in Washington County. At the current 30‑year fixed rate of **7.18 %**, a median‑priced home financed with a conventional loan and a 20 % down payment ($222,480 loan) generates a principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment of **$1,507 per month**. This represents **33.5 % of the median household income** ($54,000 annually or $4,500 monthly). An FHA loan with the minimum 3.5 % down ($268,366 loan) yields a P&I payment of **$1,818 per month**, or **40.4 % of income**. Both ratios exceed the traditional 28 % affordability threshold, indicating that even with a conventional 20 % down payment the mortgage component alone consumes a substantial share of earnings. For most local buyers, a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment is the most cost‑effective option because it avoids the FHA mortgage‑insurance premium and delivers the lowest P&I burden. However, the required **$55,620 down payment** (20 % of $278,100) may be prohibitive for many households, given the median income level. An FHA loan reduces the down‑payment hurdle to **$9,734** (3.5 %), but the higher monthly payment and insurance costs offset some of that benefit. USDA or VA financing could be viable for eligible rural or veteran purchasers, as those programs also permit low or zero down with comparable rates, further easing the upfront cash requirement. The 7.18 % rate inflates monthly obligations by roughly **$300–$350** compared with a 5 % environment, pushing the P&I share of income above the 30 % mark for conventional 20 % financing and well beyond it for FHA. **Practical takeaway:** Prospective buyers should prioritize securing a 20 % down payment to keep mortgage costs near 33 % of income; without that equity, the combination of high rates and insurance premiums makes the median home financially strained for the typical household.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Washington County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Washington County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Washington County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Washington County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Washington County qualifies.
