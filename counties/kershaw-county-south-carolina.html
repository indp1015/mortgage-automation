📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in Kershaw County, South Carolina, demonstrates robust coverage for typical home purchases, as the median home value of $217,100 sits well below both the FHA 1-unit limit of $541,287 and the conforming limit of $832,750. This significant buffer ensures that standard loan products are accessible without requiring specialized high-balance financing. Consequently, the primary constraint for local buyers is not loan eligibility limits but rather monthly payment affordability relative to household income. With a median household income of $68,231, the financial burden of mortgage payments remains manageable, though sensitive to interest rate fluctuations and down payment capabilities. Current market conditions present a challenging but viable environment for first-time buyers. An FHA loan with a 3.5% down payment on a median-priced home requires a monthly principal and interest payment of $1,450, which equates to 25.5% of the median monthly income. While this figure is near the upper threshold of traditional debt-to-income guidelines, it remains within acceptable limits for many borrowers. In contrast, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment reduces the monthly P&I to $1,202, or 21.1% of income, offering a more comfortable margin. However, the initial cash requirement of approximately $43,420 for a 20% down payment is a substantial barrier for households relying solely on median income. Conversely, the FHA minimum down payment of roughly $7,600 is significantly more accessible, making government-backed loans the most practical entry point for typical buyers in this market. The current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% exerts considerable pressure on affordability compared to historical low-rate environments. At this rate, the monthly payment for a median home is substantially higher than it would be at lower rates, effectively reducing the maximum affordable price point for a given income level. For instance, a buyer with the median income can comfortably service a $217,100 loan only if they utilize lower down payment options or have additional income sources. Practical insight suggests that prospective buyers in Kershaw County should prioritize FHA financing to minimize upfront capital requirements, as the 3.5% down payment structure aligns better with local income levels than conventional alternatives. Furthermore, given the 12.4% five-year price appreciation rate, buyers should act promptly, as continued price growth may outpace income gains, further tightening the affordability window for median-income households.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Kershaw County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Kershaw County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Kershaw County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Kershaw County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Kershaw County qualifies.
