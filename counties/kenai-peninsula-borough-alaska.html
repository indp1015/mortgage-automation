📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA 1‑unit limit of $811,931 and the conforming 1‑unit limit of $1,249,125 both exceed the median home value of $323,200 in Kenai Peninsula Borough, with the FHA limit covering 251 % and the conforming limit covering 386 % of the median price, confirming that financing ceilings are sufficient for typical purchases. For a median‑priced home, the Federal Housing Administration loan at 3.5 % down requires a $311,888 principal and yields a monthly principal‑and‑interest payment of $2,142, representing 31.9 % of the median household income of $80,538. The conventional loan with 10 % down ($290,880 loan) costs $1,998 per month (24.8 % of income), while the 20 % down loan ($258,560) costs $1,776 per month (26.5 % of income). These figures show that even the lowest‑cost conventional option places a substantial share of income toward housing. Given the median price and the availability of low‑down‑payment options, an FHA loan is likely the most suitable product for typical buyers, as it requires only $11,312 (3.5 %) down and keeps the payment burden near the upper limit of conventional affordability. A 10 % down payment of $32,320 is a sizable but potentially attainable target for households with savings, whereas a 20 % down payment of $64,640 is generally infeasible for median‑income families. The current 7.32 % interest rate amplifies monthly costs; a reduction to 5 % would lower the FHA payment to roughly $1,600, improving affordability, whereas higher rates would further strain budgets. Consequently, prospective buyers should prioritize securing an FHA loan to minimize upfront cash requirements and mitigate the impact of elevated rates on monthly cash flow. Thus, the 3.5 % down payment requirement makes FHA financing the most accessible pathway for median‑income households seeking to enter the market. At the current 7.32 % rate, the FHA monthly payment consumes over 30 % of median household income, highlighting the importance of low‑down‑payment options.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Kenai Peninsula Borough?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Kenai Peninsula Borough is $1,249,125. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Kenai Peninsula Borough?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,249,125 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Kenai Peninsula Borough?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Kenai Peninsula Borough qualifies.
