📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA loan limit for a one‑unit home ($541,287) is **433 % of the median price** ($125,000) and the conforming ceiling ($832,750) is **667 % of median price**, confirming that both programs comfortably cover the typical property in Bracken County. Because the median home sits far below any loan ceiling, borrowers are not constrained by limit caps; the primary affordability considerations are down‑payment size and monthly debt service. At a 7.24 % 30‑year fixed rate, principal‑and‑interest (P&I) on a median‑priced home is $822 for an FHA loan (3.5 % down, $120,625 loan) and $681 for a conventional loan with 20 % equity ($100,000 loan). With a median household income of $66,319 (≈$5,527 monthly), the FHA payment represents **14.9 % of monthly income**, while a 20 % conventional payment is **12.3 %**. Both ratios are well under the conventional 28 % housing‑cost threshold, indicating that, even at today’s high rates, the debt‑service burden remains moderate for the average household. For most buyers, a conventional loan with a 10 % down payment ($112,500 loan, $766 P&I) offers the lowest monthly outlay while avoiding FHA mortgage‑insurance premiums. However, borrowers lacking the cash for a 10 % down payment may find the FHA’s 3.5 % requirement more attainable. USDA eligibility could also apply, given the county’s rural status, providing 0 % down and comparable rates, but USDA limits are tied to the same conforming ceiling and thus are not a binding factor. The minimum cash needed to purchase at the median price is **$4,375** (3.5 % FHA) or **$12,500** (10 % conventional). For households with the median income, a 3.5 % down payment represents roughly **6.6 % of annual earnings**, a feasible target for many first‑time buyers; the 10 % requirement jumps to **19 % of annual earnings**, potentially limiting access for lower‑income families. A 7.24 % rate inflates monthly payments by roughly $150–$200 compared with a 5 % environment, but because the price‑to‑income ratio is low, the impact on overall affordability is modest. **Practical takeaway:** prospective buyers in Bracken County can comfortably finance a median home with as little as 3.5 % down, and even at current rates the resulting housing cost remains well within standard affordability guidelines.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Bracken County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Bracken County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Bracken County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Bracken County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Bracken County qualifies.
