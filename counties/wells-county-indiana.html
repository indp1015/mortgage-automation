📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA and conventional limits both far exceed the median home price of $197,600 in Wells County, with the FHA 1‑unit ceiling at $541,287 (274 % of the median) and the conforming ceiling at $832,750 (421 % of the median). Consequently, virtually any buyer can obtain a loan that fully finances a median‑priced property without resorting to jumbo financing, and the limits pose no constraint on typical purchases. At a 7.24 % 30‑year fixed rate, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) component for a median home is $1,299 per month with an FHA loan (3.5 % down) and $1,211 per month with a conventional loan using a 10 % down payment. A conventional loan with a 20 % down payment reduces P&I to $1,077. Relative to the median household income of $71,957 (approximately $5,996 monthly), the FHA payment represents 21.7 % of gross income, while the conventional 20 %‑down payment represents 18.0 %. Both ratios sit comfortably below the conventional 28 % affordability threshold, indicating that monthly debt service is not a primary barrier for median‑income households. For most buyers, a conventional loan with a 10 % down payment is the most cost‑effective option because it avoids the FHA mortgage insurance premium while still keeping the loan‑to‑value (90 %) well within the conforming limit. Buyers with limited cash may prefer the FHA route, which requires only $6,916 (3.5 % of $197,600) upfront, but they must factor in ongoing mortgage‑insurance costs. USDA or VA loans could be viable for eligible rural or veteran borrowers, yet the conventional market already offers competitive terms without the additional insurance fees. The required down payment ranges from $6,916 (FHA) to $39,520 (20 % conventional). Given the median household’s disposable income, a 10 % down payment ($19,760) is generally attainable, whereas a 20 % payment may be challenging for first‑time buyers without savings assistance. A 7.24 % rate inflates monthly payments by roughly $150–$200 compared with a 5 % environment, reducing purchasing power by about $15,000 in home price. Nonetheless, because loan limits are generous and the median price remains well below those caps, affordability remains strong for qualified borrowers. **Practical takeaway:** prospective buyers should target a 10 % down payment to secure a conventional loan, balancing lower monthly costs with manageable upfront cash requirements.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Wells County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Wells County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Wells County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Wells County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Wells County qualifies.
