📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Sullivan County, New York, presents a favorable financing environment where loan limits significantly exceed typical property values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 217% of the county’s median home value of $249,000, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 334% of that figure. Consequently, no typical buyer in this market faces loan limit restrictions. This ample coverage ensures that standard financing products are accessible for the vast majority of properties, eliminating the need for jumbo loans or alternative high-cost financing structures. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels. For a median-priced home, a 30-year fixed mortgage at the current 7.40% rate results in a principal and interest payment of $1,663 for an FHA loan with 3.5% down, or $1,379 for a conventional loan with 20% down. These figures represent 27.6% and 22.9% of the median household income of $72,382, respectively. While the FHA ratio approaches the upper threshold of conventional debt-to-income guidelines, the conventional 20% down payment option provides a more comfortable margin. The primary barrier to entry is the down payment itself; the minimum 3.5% for an FHA loan requires approximately $8,715, whereas a conventional 20% down payment requires $49,800. Given the median income, saving the lower FHA threshold is more feasible for first-time buyers, though it remains a significant hurdle compared to rental costs of $1,106 per month. The current interest rate environment significantly impacts affordability compared to historical lows. At 7.40%, monthly payments are substantially higher than they would be at rates below 5%, effectively reducing purchasing power by several thousand dollars. However, the strong price appreciation, with a five-year CAGR of 8.2%, suggests that buyers are paying premium prices that may not be fully offset by lower rates. For prospective buyers, the most practical strategy involves utilizing FHA financing to minimize initial cash requirements, thereby preserving liquidity for closing costs and reserves. This approach aligns with the county’s high owner-occupancy rate of 67.4%, indicating a stable market where long-term holding is common. Buyers should prioritize maximizing their down payment to the extent feasible to reduce total interest paid over the life of the loan, as the high current rate amplifies the cost of carrying debt.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Sullivan County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Sullivan County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Sullivan County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Sullivan County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Sullivan County qualifies.
