📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA loan limit of $541,287 for a single‑unit property is more than 1.7 times the Camarilla County median home price of $304,000, and the conforming ceiling of $832,750 is 2.7 times that price. Both limits therefore comfortably encompass the typical home, eliminating ceiling constraints for most buyers. At the median price, a 30‑year FHA loan with a 3.5 % down payment ($293,360 financed) produces a principal‑and‑interest (P&I) obligation of $1,987 per month. That amount represents 41.1 % of the county’s median monthly household income ($4,830), exceeding the conventional 28‑30 % affordability threshold. A conventional loan with a 20 % down payment ($243,200 financed) lowers the P&I to $1,647, or 34.1 % of income—still above the optimal range but markedly better than the FHA scenario. A conventional loan with only 10 % down yields a P&I of $1,853 (38.4 % of income). Given the price level and loan‑size flexibility, conventional financing with a 20 % down payment is the most cost‑effective option for typical buyers, because it avoids the FHA mortgage insurance premium and reduces the debt‑to‑income (DTI) burden. Buyers lacking the $60,800 required for a 20 % down payment (20 % of $304,000) may consider an FHA loan, but must be prepared for a higher monthly outlay and the ongoing mortgage‑insurance cost. USDA or VA loans could be viable for eligible rural or veteran purchasers, as the property price is well below USDA’s $726,200 rural ceiling, but eligibility is limited to qualifying households. The prevailing 7.18 % 30‑year rate inflates monthly payments by roughly $200–$300 compared with a 5 % environment, pushing all financing scenarios further above the 30 % income guideline. Consequently, the primary barrier for most households is the down‑payment size rather than loan‑size limits. **Practical takeaway:** prospective buyers should aim to save at least 20 % of the purchase price (~$61 k) to secure a conventional loan that keeps monthly housing costs nearer to the 30 % affordability benchmark.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Camas County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Camas County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Camas County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Camas County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Camas County qualifies.
