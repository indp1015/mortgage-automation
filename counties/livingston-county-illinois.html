📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Livingston County’s median home value of $142,200 sits comfortably within both the 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 and the conforming limit of $832,750. The FHA limit alone covers 381% of the median price, indicating that financing constraints are not a barrier for typical transactions in this market. Consequently, borrowers have ample access to standard loan products without requiring jumbo loan structures, which are unnecessary given the significant gap between local prices and federal lending caps. The affordability profile is supported by strong income-to-payment ratios. With a median household income of $73,790, the estimated principal and interest payment for an FHA loan at the median price is $950 per month, representing 15.4% of monthly income. This figure falls well below the 28% debt-to-income threshold typically used by lenders. For conventional loans, the burden is even lower; a 20% down payment results in a $787 monthly payment, or 12.8% of income. These metrics suggest that the median buyer can comfortably service debt, provided they maintain stable employment and limited other liabilities. FHA financing is likely the most accessible option for first-time buyers due to its 3.5% minimum down payment requirement, which amounts to approximately $4,977 at the median price. This low entry barrier is more feasible than the $28,440 required for a 20% conventional down payment. While USDA loans may be available in specific rural tracts within the county, FHA offers broader eligibility and lender participation. The current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% significantly impacts purchasing power compared to historical lows. At this rate, a $100,000 loan costs roughly $632 per month, whereas a 4% rate would have cost only $477. This rate sensitivity means that buyers are effectively paying a premium for the same home compared to 2020-2021 environments. Prospective buyers should prioritize securing a pre-approval at current rates while focusing on the FHA option to minimize initial cash outlay. Given the 5.4% five-year price CAGR, the market remains appreciating, but the high interest rate environment necessitates careful budgeting. The primary constraint for local buyers is not the loan limit or home price, but rather the total monthly outgo, which includes property taxes, insurance, and PMI. Buyers should ensure their total debt obligations, including the $950 P&I estimate, remain under 43% of gross income to maintain financial resilience against potential rate fluctuations or income disruptions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Livingston County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Livingston County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Livingston County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Livingston County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Livingston County qualifies.
