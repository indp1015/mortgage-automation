📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Scott County’s residential mortgage landscape is characterized by strong financing coverage relative to local property values. The 2026 FHA loan limit of $541,287 covers 129% of the median home value of $419,400, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 199%. Consequently, typical buyers in this market do not face high-balance loan restrictions, as standard financing products comfortably accommodate purchases at the median price point. This ample headroom ensures that most households can secure standard mortgage terms without requiring jumbo loan structures or alternative financing mechanisms. Monthly payment burdens remain significant but manageable for the median household, which earns $119,314 annually. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment on the median-priced home results in a principal and interest payment of $2,323 per month. This represents 23.4% of the median monthly income, falling well within the general 28% debt-to-income threshold used by underwriters. Conversely, an FHA loan with a 3.5% down payment yields a higher monthly obligation of $2,802, or 28.2% of income, which sits at the upper limit of conventional affordability guidelines. The 20% down payment option is the most financially prudent for median-income earners, as it minimizes monthly outflows and avoids mortgage insurance premiums typically associated with lower equity positions. The primary barrier to entry is the initial capital required rather than monthly debt service. A 20% down payment on a $419,400 home requires approximately $83,880 in cash, a substantial sum that may exceed the liquid savings of many first-time buyers. While an FHA loan reduces this barrier to roughly $14,679 (3.5% down), the higher monthly payment and mandatory mortgage insurance increase the long-term cost of ownership. The current 7.40% interest rate significantly impacts affordability compared to historical lows; a reduction to 6.5% would lower the 20% down payment monthly cost by approximately $150, highlighting the sensitivity of debt service to rate fluctuations. For prospective buyers, the most practical insight is that while financing limits are not a constraint, the high interest rate environment makes maximizing down payment size critical. Achieving a 20% equity position not only reduces the monthly payment to a sustainable 23.4% of income but also eliminates the need for mortgage insurance, thereby optimizing total housing costs in a market where prices have grown at a 9.0% annual rate over the last five years.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Scott County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Scott County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Scott County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Scott County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Scott County qualifies.
