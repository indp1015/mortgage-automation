📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA and conforming loan limits both far exceed the median home price in Clay County. The FHA 1‑unit ceiling of $541,287 is 347 % of the median value ($155,900), while the conforming limit of $832,750 is 534 % of that price, confirming that virtually any buyer can finance a typical home within the statutory caps. At the median household income of $66,176 ($5,515 monthly), the estimated principal‑and‑interest (P&I) obligations translate to modest income shares. An FHA loan with 3.5 % down ($1,025/month) consumes 18.6 % of monthly earnings, whereas a conventional loan with 20 % down ($849/month) represents 15.4 % of income. Even the higher‑priced conventional 10 %‑down scenario ($956/month) remains under 20 % of earnings, well within the conventional affordability threshold of 28 % for housing costs. Given the low price level relative to loan limits, conventional financing is the most efficient option for typical buyers. A 20 % down payment ($31,180) eliminates mortgage‑insurance premiums and yields the lowest P&I burden. For borrowers lacking that capital, the FHA’s 3.5 % requirement ($5,456) provides a viable alternative, though it adds insurance costs and a slightly higher monthly payment. USDA eligibility is possible for rural‑area applicants, but the median price already sits comfortably below the USDA guarantee ceiling, making the program redundant for most. VA financing, where applicable, would further reduce cash‑outlay and eliminate mortgage insurance, but its use is limited to eligible veterans. The prevailing 30‑year fixed rate of 7.24 % inflates monthly payments by roughly 1.5 %‑2 % compared with a 5‑% environment, raising the FHA payment from $1,025 to $1,025 (current) versus an estimated $880 at 5 %. This rate uplift modestly tightens affordability but does not push payments above the 20 % income threshold for conventional 20 % down. **Practical takeaway:** With median homes well under both FHA and conforming limits, a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment delivers the lowest monthly cost and avoids mortgage‑insurance fees, making it the most cost‑effective path for the average Clay County buyer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Clay County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Clay County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Clay County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Clay County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Clay County qualifies.
