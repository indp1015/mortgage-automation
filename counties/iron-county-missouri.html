📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Iron County, Missouri, presents a distinct financing landscape where federal loan limits significantly exceed local property values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 454% of the median home value of $119,300, while the conforming limit covers 698%. Consequently, no typical buyer in this market faces a loan limit constraint, as both FHA and conventional options comfortably accommodate the median-priced home. This ample coverage ensures that financing availability is not a barrier, allowing buyers to focus primarily on income qualification and down payment requirements rather than loan cap restrictions. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local earnings, despite the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with a 3.5% down payment results in a principal and interest payment of $797. This amount represents 19.6% of the median household income of $48,850. A conventional loan with a 20% down payment lowers the monthly obligation to $660, or 16.2% of income. While these figures fall within standard affordability guidelines, the higher interest rate environment increases the total cost of borrowing compared to historical lows. Specifically, the 7.40% rate elevates monthly costs by approximately 25% compared to a 5.5% rate, making the initial down payment amount a critical factor in determining overall affordability. Regarding loan type suitability, FHA financing is likely the most accessible option for typical buyers due to its lower minimum down payment requirement of 3.5%. At the median price, this requires a cash outlay of $4,176. This amount is feasible for many local households, particularly given the low absolute price point of homes in Iron County. Conventional loans require higher equity stakes, with a 20% down payment totaling $23,860, which may be prohibitive for entry-level buyers. USDA loans could also be relevant if the property is located in an eligible rural area, potentially offering zero-down options, though this depends on specific parcel eligibility rather than county-wide averages. The practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that affordability in Iron County is driven more by down payment capacity than by loan limit restrictions. Because the median home value is well below all major loan caps, buyers should prioritize saving for the minimum 3.5% down payment to unlock FHA financing. This strategy minimizes upfront capital requirements while keeping monthly payments under the 20% income threshold. Additionally, buyers should recognize that while the low home prices mitigate the impact of high interest rates, the total monthly cost still consumes nearly one-fifth of median income, necessitating careful budgeting for taxes, insurance, and maintenance to maintain long-term financial stability.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Iron County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Iron County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Iron County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Iron County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Iron County qualifies.
