📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Coryell County’s housing market exhibits strong financing coverage, with the 2026 FHA one-unit limit of $541,287 covering 277% of the median home value of $195,300. The conforming limit of $832,750 provides even greater headroom, covering 426% of the median price. Because the county is not designated as high-cost, standard loan limits apply, ensuring that typical residential properties fall well within regulatory caps for both government-backed and conventional financing. This ample coverage means that price-based restrictions do not hinder access to mainstream mortgage products for the average buyer in this region. The primary challenge for affordability lies in the current interest rate environment rather than loan limits. With a 30-year fixed rate at 7.40%, the estimated principal and interest payment for an FHA loan on a median-priced home is $1,304 per month. This figure represents 21.9% of the median monthly household income, derived from an annual income of $71,301. While this ratio remains below the 28% threshold often used for conventional qualification, it leaves limited room for taxes, insurance, and other living expenses. A conventional loan with a 20% down payment reduces the monthly P&I to $1,081, or 18.2% of income, offering a more manageable debt-to-income ratio. However, the rapid appreciation of home values, which grew at a 12.6% compound annual rate over the last five years, has significantly increased the capital required for entry. For typical buyers, the FHA loan presents the most accessible entry point due to its 3.5% minimum down payment requirement, totaling approximately $6,835 for a median home. This low barrier to entry is feasible for many households, especially given the stable income levels. In contrast, conventional loans require higher upfront capital, with a 10% down payment costing $19,530 and a 20% payment costing $39,066. The current 7.40% rate significantly impacts affordability compared to lower-rate environments; a reduction to 6.00% would lower the FHA payment by approximately $150 per month, substantially improving cash flow. Prospective buyers should prioritize FHA financing to minimize initial capital outlay, as the high interest rates make larger down payments less critical for qualification but more impactful for long-term equity growth. The combination of low down payment requirements and robust loan limits makes Coryell County a viable market for first-time buyers, provided they can sustain the current monthly payment burden.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Coryell County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Coryell County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Coryell County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Coryell County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Coryell County qualifies.
