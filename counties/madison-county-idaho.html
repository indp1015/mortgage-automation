📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Madison County, Idaho, presents a distinct financing landscape where loan limits comfortably exceed local price points, yet affordability remains constrained by high interest rates. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 130% of the median home value of $414,800, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 201%. Consequently, the typical buyer is not restricted by loan ceilings but rather by debt-to-income ratios. With a median household income of $60,160, the monthly principal and interest payment for an FHA loan at 7.40% is $2,771, representing 55.3% of monthly income. Even with a 20% down payment on a conventional loan, the payment of $2,297 consumes 45.8% of income. These figures significantly exceed the standard 28-30% housing expense threshold, indicating that the median-priced home is financially inaccessible for the average local household without substantial additional income or assistance. Given the current rate environment, conventional financing with a 20% down payment offers the lowest monthly burden, yet the required $82,960 down payment is a significant barrier for households earning the median income. An FHA loan requires a minimum down payment of $14,518, which is more accessible but results in a higher monthly payment due to mortgage insurance premiums. The rapid appreciation of 16.8% annually since 2017 has outpaced income growth, creating a widening gap between asset values and purchasing power. The current 7.40% fixed rate exacerbates this strain; had rates been in the 6.0-6.5% range, the FHA payment would have been approximately $2,400, reducing the income percentage to roughly 48%, though still above conventional underwriting guidelines. The primary constraint for Madison County buyers is not loan eligibility but cash flow capacity. Prospective buyers should prioritize maximizing down payments to reduce principal balances, as the high interest rate makes the initial loan size critical to monthly affordability. Additionally, exploring shared equity programs or multi-unit properties, where FHA limits extend to $693,062 for two units, may provide pathways to ownership that single-family homes do not. The data suggests that typical buyers must seek incomes well above the county median or utilize significant down payments to achieve sustainable mortgage terms in this high-growth, high-rate market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Madison County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Madison County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Madison County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Madison County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Madison County qualifies.
