📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA loan limit of $541,287 and the conforming limit of $832,750 both exceed the median home value of $168,700, covering 321% and 494% of the typical price respectively, so financing coverage is adequate for most buyers. Monthly payment estimates for a median‑priced home at a 30‑year fixed rate of 7.40% show an FHA payment of $1,127 (3.5% down, $162,796 loan) and a conventional 20%‑down payment of $934 ($134,960 loan). The FHA payment represents 24.2% of the median household income of $55,819, which is close to the 30% affordability threshold, while the 20% conventional payment is 20.1% of income, indicating that both options are financially feasible but the FHA loan carries a higher payment burden. At this rate, the payment consumes nearly a quarter of median income, approaching the 30% guideline for housing affordability, and suggests that buyers may need to adjust down payment or consider a lower‑rate loan to improve cash flow. With a median home price of $168,700, the minimum down payment required is 3.5% for FHA ($5,900) and 10% for conventional ($16,900), rising to 20% ($33,700) for the lowest‑rate conventional option. A 10% down payment would require roughly $16,900, which may be challenging for first‑time buyers, whereas the 20% down option reduces the monthly payment to $934, keeping the payment‑to‑income ratio at 20% and providing greater financial flexibility. The current 7.40% interest rate markedly reduces affordability compared with lower‑rate environments; a 1% reduction would lower the FHA payment to roughly $950 and improve payment efficiency. Given the limited down payment options and the high price appreciation observed in the 5‑year CAGR of 11.7%, an FHA loan is likely the best fit for typical buyers, as it aligns with the median price, requires the smallest down payment, and keeps the payment‑to‑income ratio within a manageable range. Prospective purchasers should prioritize securing an FHA loan with the 3.5% down payment to maintain payment efficiency under present rate conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in San Saba County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in San Saba County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in San Saba County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in San Saba County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm San Saba County qualifies.
