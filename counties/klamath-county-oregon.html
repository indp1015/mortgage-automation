📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Klamath County’s median home value of $280,400 sits comfortably within both federal financing thresholds, ensuring broad access to standard loan products. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 193% of the median price, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 297%. Consequently, no buyer in the typical price range faces high-cost county restrictions or loan cap issues. This ample coverage means lenders can extend full-value financing without requiring jumbo loans, simplifying the underwriting process for the majority of local transactions. Monthly payment burdens remain significant relative to local income levels. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan with 3.5% down results in a principal-and-interest payment of $1,873. This figure represents 38.2% of the median household income of $58,830, exceeding the conventional 36% debt-to-income guideline for mortgage-only obligations. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces the payment to $1,553, or 31.7% of median income, offering a safer margin for debt qualification. The 12.3% five-year compound annual growth rate in home values has outpaced income growth, tightening affordability for first-time buyers who lack substantial savings for larger down payments. For the typical Klamath County buyer, the FHA loan is the most structurally viable option due to its 3.5% minimum down payment requirement, which amounts to approximately $9,814 at the median price. While this barrier is lower than the $28,040 required for a 10% conventional down payment, it still represents a significant cash reserve hurdle for households earning the median income. USDA loans may be relevant for specific rural tracts within the county, but their income eligibility caps often exclude higher-earning professionals. The current 7.40% rate significantly erodes purchasing power compared to the sub-4% environments of recent years, effectively reducing the maximum affordable loan amount by nearly 20% for the same monthly budget. Prospective buyers should prioritize saving for a 10-20% down payment to mitigate monthly debt ratios, as relying solely on minimum down payment options leaves little room for property taxes, insurance, and maintenance costs within the median income bracket.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Klamath County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Klamath County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Klamath County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Klamath County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Klamath County qualifies.
