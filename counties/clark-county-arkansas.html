📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA and conforming loan limits both comfortably exceed the median home price of $155,000 in Clark County, Arkansas. The FHA 1‑unit limit of $541,287 covers 349 % of the median price, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 537 %. Consequently, virtually any buyer can obtain a loan that does not require a “jumbo” product, and the primary constraint is down‑payment size rather than loan size. At the current 30‑year fixed rate of 7.18 %, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment on a median‑priced home is $1,013 for an FHA loan (3.5 % down) and $945 for a conventional loan with 10 % down. With a median household income of $52,034, the monthly gross income is $4,336; the FHA payment represents 23.4 % of income and the conventional 10 %‑down payment represents 21.8 % of income. Even the most favorable conventional scenario (20 % down, $840 P&I) is 19.4 % of income, well within the conventional 28 % front‑end affordability guideline. Renters paying the local median rent of $793 already allocate roughly 22 % of income to housing, indicating that the mortgage burden is comparable to the rental market. The minimum down payment to purchase at the median price is 3.5 % for FHA ($5,425) and 10 % for conventional ($15,500). For a typical household, the FHA option requires a modest cash outlay, while the conventional 10 % down may be attainable for buyers with modest savings. The 20 % conventional down payment ($31,000) would further reduce monthly obligations but may be a barrier for many. A 7.18 % rate inflates monthly payments by roughly $150–$200 compared with a 5 % environment, shrinking purchasing power by about 5 % of the home price. Nonetheless, because loan limits are not restrictive, the primary affordability challenge remains the down‑payment requirement rather than the interest rate. **Practical takeaway:** Prospective buyers in Clark County can secure financing for the median home with either FHA or conventional loans; focusing on saving a 3.5 %–10 % down payment will unlock affordable monthly payments that remain below the 28 % front‑end threshold despite the elevated 7.18 % rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Clark County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Clark County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Clark County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Clark County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Clark County qualifies.
