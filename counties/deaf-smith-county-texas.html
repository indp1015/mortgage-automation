📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Financing coverage: The FHA 1‑Unit limit of $541,287 and the conforming conventional limit of $832,750 both exceed the median home value of $121,300, covering 446% and 687% respectively, so the typical property is fully within eligible loan amounts. Monthly payment burden: With a median household income of $60,799 (approximately $5,066 per month), the estimated P&I payment for an FHA loan (3.5% down, $117,054 principal) is $810, representing 16.0% of monthly income, while a conventional loan with 20% down ($97,040 principal) costs $671, or 13.2% of income. Both figures fall within conventional affordability thresholds, though the FHA payment consumes a larger share of earnings. Loan type recommendation: Given the median price and the availability of FHA financing up to the county’s limit, an FHA loan is likely the most suitable for typical buyers, as it requires the lowest down payment and aligns with the existing loan ceiling. VA and USDA options are not quantified here, but conventional 10% down loans also remain viable for those able to meet the higher down payment. Down payment barriers: The minimum down payment for FHA is 3.5% of the median price, equating to $4,246, a figure that may be attainable with savings or assistance. Conventional loans demand 10% ($12,130) or 20% ($24,260), presenting a substantial barrier for many households. Rate sensitivity: At the current 7.40% 30‑year fixed rate, the FHA payment of $810 is higher than it would be at lower rates; for example, a 5% rate would reduce the payment to roughly $610, improving affordability by about $200 per month. The elevated rate therefore increases the payment‑to‑income ratio, especially for borrowers relying on the lower‑down‑payment FHA product. Practical takeaway: Prospective buyers should prioritize FHA financing to minimize upfront cash requirements while staying within the county’s loan limits, as the median home price comfortably fits within the FHA ceiling and the payment, though a notable share of income, remains manageable.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Deaf Smith County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Deaf Smith County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Deaf Smith County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Deaf Smith County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Deaf Smith County qualifies.
