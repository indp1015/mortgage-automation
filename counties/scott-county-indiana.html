📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Scott County, Indiana, presents a financing landscape where federal loan limits significantly exceed local housing costs, ensuring broad eligibility for standard mortgage products. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 342% of the $158,400 median home value, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 526%. Consequently, virtually all typical residential transactions in this market fall well within both FHA and conventional loan parameters, eliminating high-balance financing complexities. This ample coverage means that loan type selection is driven primarily by credit profiles and down payment availability rather than property value ceilings. The primary constraint for affordability remains the monthly debt service relative to local income levels. With a median household income of $56,352, a 30-year fixed mortgage at the current 7.40% rate creates a significant payment burden. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with a 3.5% down payment requires a monthly principal and interest (P&I) payment of $1,058, consuming 22.5% of median monthly income. A conventional loan with a 20% down payment reduces this to $877, or 18.7% of income. While these figures are within traditional underwriting guidelines, the high interest rate amplifies the cost of capital. Compared to lower-rate environments, the 7.40% rate increases the total interest paid over the life of the loan, effectively reducing purchasing power. A buyer who could afford a $180,000 home at a 4.0% rate faces a substantially higher monthly obligation at current rates, narrowing the range of accessible price points for the average household. Regarding down payment barriers, the minimum FHA requirement of 3.5% equates to $5,544 for a median-priced home. This amount is accessible for many households, particularly those utilizing first-time homebuyer assistance programs, whereas the 20% conventional threshold of $31,680 represents a substantial barrier for the median income earner. Given the local market dynamics, FHA financing is likely the most suitable option for typical buyers, as it minimizes upfront capital requirements while remaining comfortably within loan limits. The rapid price appreciation, evidenced by a 9.9% five-year CAGR, further underscores the importance of securing financing early. Prospective buyers should prioritize improving credit scores to qualify for the lowest possible rate within the FHA framework, as even small rate reductions significantly lower the monthly payment burden and enhance long-term affordability in this appreciating market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Scott County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Scott County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Scott County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Scott County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Scott County qualifies.
