📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in Marlboro County, South Carolina, presents a distinct contrast between national loan limits and local market realities. The 2026 FHA limit for a single-unit property is set at $541,287, which covers approximately 660% of the county’s median home value of $82,000. Similarly, the conforming limit of $832,750 exceeds the median price by over 1,000%. Consequently, loan limits are not a barrier to entry; virtually all typical transactions in this market fall well below federal caps, allowing buyers full access to standard financing programs without requiring jumbo loans. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels, though they require careful budgeting. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with 3.5% down results in a principal and interest payment of $547, representing 19.1% of the median monthly household income derived from an annual median income of $34,301. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces this to $454, or 15.9% of income. While these figures are within general affordability guidelines, the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% significantly impacts long-term cost. Compared to lower-rate environments, this rate increases the total interest paid over the life of the loan, making the initial down payment size a critical factor in mitigating overall debt burden. Given the low median home value, the primary barrier to homeownership is not loan eligibility but rather the upfront capital required. The minimum down payment for an FHA loan is $2,870, while a conventional loan with 20% down requires $16,400. For households earning the median income, saving the conventional down payment may take several years, whereas the FHA requirement is more attainable. Therefore, FHA financing is likely the most suitable option for typical buyers in Marlboro County, offering lower down payment thresholds and broader qualification flexibility. Prospective buyers should prioritize maximizing their down payment savings to reduce principal balances, as the high interest rate amplifies the cost of carrying larger loans. Ultimately, the affordability of housing in this region is less constrained by regulatory loan limits and more dependent on the buyer’s ability to accumulate sufficient cash reserves for closing costs and down payments.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Marlboro County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Marlboro County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Marlboro County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Marlboro County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Marlboro County qualifies.
