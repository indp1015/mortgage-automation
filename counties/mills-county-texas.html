📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in Mills County, Texas, demonstrates robust coverage for typical buyers, as the median home value of $223,700 sits well below both the FHA one-unit limit of $541,287 and the conforming limit of $832,750. The FHA limit covers 242% of the median price, while the conforming limit covers 372%, ensuring that no local purchaser faces loan size restrictions. This ample headroom allows borrowers to access various loan programs without triggering high-cost county adjustments, which do not apply to this region. Consequently, the primary constraint on affordability is not the loan ceiling but rather the monthly debt service relative to household income. Regarding monthly payment burden, the estimated principal and interest (P&I) for a median-priced home is significant when viewed against the local median household income of $67,620. An FHA loan with a 3.5% down payment requires a monthly P&I of $1,494, which consumes 26.5% of monthly income. In contrast, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment reduces the P&I to $1,239, or 22.0% of income. While the FHA ratio approaches the upper limits of standard debt-to-income guidelines, the conventional option offers a more comfortable margin. Given the low median home value and the substantial gap between local prices and national loan limits, conventional financing is likely the most efficient fit for qualified buyers, as it avoids FHA mortgage insurance premiums and aligns better with the lower price point. Down payment barriers remain the most accessible hurdle in this market. The minimum 3.5% FHA down payment amounts to approximately $7,830, a feasible figure for many households compared to higher-cost metros. However, the 20% conventional down payment of $44,740 represents a more substantial capital requirement. Rate sensitivity is a critical factor; at the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, monthly payments are elevated compared to historical averages. A drop to 6.0% would reduce the 20% down payment scenario’s P&I by roughly $150, significantly improving cash flow. Prospective buyers should note that while the 15.0% five-year price CAGR has increased the absolute cost of entry, the relative affordability remains strong due to the low baseline home values. The concrete takeaway is that buyers with savings for a 20% down payment can achieve a sustainable debt-to-income ratio, whereas those relying on minimum down payments must carefully budget for additional monthly insurance costs to maintain financial stability.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Mills County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Mills County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Mills County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Mills County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Mills County qualifies.
