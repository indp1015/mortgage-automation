📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA loan limit of $541,287 exceeds the median home price of $344,000 by 157%, and the conforming limit of $832,750 exceeds it by 242%, confirming that both programs fully cover typical purchases in Burnet County. For a median‑priced home, the FHA‑backed loan requiring a 3.5% down payment results in a principal‑and‑interest payment of $2,298 per month, which equals 35.0% of the median household income of $78,732. The conventional loan with 20% down yields a payment of $1,905, representing 29.0% of income, while the 10% down option is $2,143 (27.3% of income). These percentages indicate a substantial share of earnings devoted to housing costs. Given the price level and available limits, FHA financing is likely the most accessible option for typical buyers because it requires the smallest down payment and aligns with the median income share, though the high monthly payment relative to income remains a constraint. A buyer seeking lower long‑term costs might opt for a conventional loan with 20% down, but that demands a $68,800 down payment, which is often unattainable. The current 7.40% interest rate amplifies affordability challenges; lower rates would reduce the $2,298 FHA payment by roughly 15% at 6%, lowering the payment‑to‑income share to about 31%. The $12,000 down payment for an FHA loan, representing 3.5% of the median price, may still be difficult for first‑time buyers without savings or assistance programs. Therefore, prospective purchasers should prioritize securing an FHA loan with assistance for the down payment, as this represents the most realistic entry point for median‑priced homes in Burnet County. VA and USDA financing are not relevant for this analysis because the median home price exceeds the typical eligibility thresholds and the county does not qualify as a rural area for USDA loans. Moreover, the 7.40% interest rate makes the monthly payment for a 20% down conventional loan about $1,905, which consumes nearly 29% of median household income, indicating that even with a larger down payment the burden remains high. Consequently, buyers should aim to secure down‑payment assistance or consider a smaller‑priced property to keep housing costs within a sustainable range of income. A practical takeaway for prospective buyers is to prioritize FHA financing with assistance for the $12,000 down payment, as this keeps monthly housing costs near 35% of income while enabling homeownership in a rapidly appreciating market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Burnet County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Burnet County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Burnet County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Burnet County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Burnet County qualifies.
