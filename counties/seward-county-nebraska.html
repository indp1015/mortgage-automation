📝 Mortgage Market Notes

In Seward County, Nebraska, the 2026 financing landscape offers substantial coverage for the typical buyer. The median home value of $241,300 represents only 45% of the FHA limit of $541,287 and 29% of the conforming limit of $832,750. Consequently, loan limits do not constrain purchasing power in this market, as both FHA and conventional options comfortably accommodate median-priced properties. This ample headroom ensures that price appreciation, including the recent 8.6% five-year compound annual growth rate, will not push typical transactions above standard loan ceilings in the near term. The primary challenge for buyers is the monthly payment burden relative to local income levels. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan on the median home requires a minimum down payment of $8,450 (3.5%), resulting in a principal and interest payment of $1,612. This amount consumes 23.5% of the median household income of $82,444, a figure that sits near the upper threshold of conventional debt-to-income guidelines. In contrast, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment of $48,260 reduces the monthly P&I to $1,336, or 19.4% of income. While the conventional option offers better monthly cash flow, the upfront capital requirement is significantly higher. For buyers with limited savings, the FHA option provides a lower barrier to entry, though the higher monthly outlay may strain budgets if other housing costs, such as taxes and insurance, are factored in. The current interest rate environment significantly impacts affordability compared to historical lows. At 7.40%, a buyer pays substantially more in interest over the life of the loan than they would have during the sub-4% rate periods of the early 2020s. This rate sensitivity means that the effective cost of homeownership is elevated, making the choice between a smaller down payment with FHA and a larger down payment with conventional loans a critical financial decision. For the typical Seward County buyer, the most practical path involves leveraging the FHA’s low down payment requirement to preserve liquidity, provided that the 23.5% income ratio remains manageable after accounting for property taxes and insurance. Buyers should prioritize saving for a 10% or 20% down payment if possible, as this mitigates the impact of high interest rates by reducing the total loan principal and monthly obligation. Ultimately, the market remains accessible, but affordability is heavily dependent on income stability and the ability to manage higher monthly debt service costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Seward County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Seward County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Seward County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Seward County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Seward County qualifies.
