📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Bond County's mortgage landscape presents favorable financing coverage relative to local pricing. The FHA 1-unit limit of $541,287 exceeds the median home value of $145,700 by 372%, while the conforming limit of $832,750 provides 572% coverage—both comfortably accommodating typical purchases without requiring jumbo financing. At the current 7.40% rate, monthly principal and interest payments consume a moderate share of median household income ($5,497/month): FHA financing with 3.5% down ($140,600 loan) requires $973/month (17.7% of income), conventional 10% down ($131,130 loan) requires $907/month (16.5%), and conventional 20% down ($116,560 loan) requires $807/month (14.7%). All scenarios remain well below the 28% front-end DTI threshold. Given the median price point and strong limit coverage, conventional financing with 10–20% down emerges as the optimal fit for qualified buyers, avoiding FHA's lifetime mortgage insurance premiums while maintaining manageable payments. USDA eligibility should be verified for rural properties, potentially offering 0% down alternatives. The minimum down payment barrier is low: FHA requires $5,100 (3.5%), conventional 10% requires $14,570, and conventional 20% requires $29,140. With 76.6% owner occupancy and a 4.8% five-year price CAGR, accumulation of these amounts is feasible for median-income households, particularly given the county's relatively affordable rental baseline ($785/month). Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Bond County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Bond County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Bond County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Bond County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Bond County qualifies.
