📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in Plymouth County presents a distinct structural challenge for typical homebuyers. The 2026 FHA loan limit for a one-unit property stands at $541,287, which covers only 97% of the county’s median home value of $556,000. Consequently, the median-priced home exceeds the FHA ceiling, rendering this loan type insufficient for the average transaction without a price reduction. In contrast, the conforming loan limit of $962,550 covers 173% of the median price, ensuring that conventional financing remains the primary viable option for standard purchases. Because the market is not designated as high-cost, no special adjustments apply to these limits, leaving a narrow gap between typical home prices and government-backed loan caps. Monthly payment burdens are significant relative to local income levels. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with 3.5% down results in a principal and interest payment of $3,714 per month. This figure consumes 39.0% of the median household income of $114,201, a ratio that exceeds the standard 36% debt-to-income threshold often preferred by lenders. Even with a 20% down payment on a conventional loan, the monthly payment is $3,079, representing 32.4% of median income. While this latter figure falls within conventional affordability metrics, it leaves limited discretionary income for property taxes, insurance, and maintenance. The minimum down payment barrier is substantial; at 3.5% for FHA, a buyer needs approximately $19,460, but since the FHA limit is insufficient, a conventional loan with 5% down requires $27,800. This capital requirement is feasible for many households but demands significant savings accumulation. The current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% exacerbates these affordability constraints. Compared to the sub-4% rates observed in prior years, the current interest environment increases monthly payments by over 80% for the same loan amount. This rate sensitivity means that the 10.1% five-year price CAGR has compounded the difficulty of entry, as both asset values and borrowing costs have risen simultaneously. For prospective buyers, the practical takeaway is that purchasing at the median price requires a conventional loan with a minimum of 5% down and a household income significantly above the median to maintain a sustainable debt-to-income ratio. Buyers should prioritize larger down payments to reduce principal balances, thereby mitigating the impact of elevated interest rates on long-term affordability.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Plymouth County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Plymouth County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Plymouth County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $962,550 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Plymouth County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Plymouth County qualifies.
