📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA loan limit for a one‑unit property in Ada County ($541,287) exceeds the 2024 median home value of $512,300, providing 106 % coverage, while the conforming loan ceiling ($832,750) reaches 163 % of the median price. Thus, both FHA and conventional financing are technically capable of covering a typical purchase, but the FHA limit is the tighter constraint and is still sufficient for the median home. At a 7.18 % 30‑year fixed rate, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) component for a median‑priced home is $3,349 / month under an FHA loan (3.5 % down, $494,370 loan) and $2,776 / month under a conventional loan with 20 % down ($409,840 loan). With a median household income of $91,502, the monthly income is $7,625. The FHA payment represents 43.9 % of income, whereas a conventional loan with 20 % equity consumes 36.4 % of income. Both ratios exceed the conventional affordability benchmark of 28 % for housing costs, indicating a notable payment burden for median earners. For most buyers, a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment yields the lowest monthly obligation and stays within the conforming limit, making it the preferred product when sufficient cash is available. FHA financing remains viable for borrowers who cannot amass a 20 % down payment, but the higher loan‑to‑value ratio pushes the P&I share of income close to 44 %. VA or USDA options are not broadly applicable in this urban market, given the median price exceeds typical USDA eligibility and the VA pool is limited to eligible veterans. The minimum cash required to purchase at the median price is $17,931 (3.5 % down for FHA) or $51,230 (10 % down for a conventional loan). The FHA down‑payment is relatively modest, yet the resulting debt service may be unsustainable for many households. The 7.18 % rate adds roughly $600‑$800 per month compared with a 5 % environment, eroding affordability and raising the income‑to‑payment ratios by 10‑15 percentage points. **Takeaway:** Buyers who can secure a 20 % down payment and lock a conventional loan stay closest to traditional affordability thresholds; otherwise, the high rate and elevated payment‑to‑income ratios make median‑priced homes financially strained for average earners.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Ada County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Ada County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Ada County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Ada County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Ada County qualifies.
