📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Andrew County, Missouri, presents a financing landscape where federal loan limits significantly exceed local median home values, ensuring broad eligibility for standard mortgage products. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 251% of the $215,400 median home value, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 387%. Consequently, no price ceiling restricts typical buyers in this market. The primary constraint is not loan availability but rather the monthly debt service burden relative to household income. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, a median-priced home requires a monthly principal and interest payment of $1,439 for an FHA loan with 3.5% down. This amount represents 22.8% of the median household income of $75,625. While this ratio is within the 28% threshold often used for qualified mortgage guidelines, it leaves limited margin for taxes, insurance, and other living expenses. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces the payment to $1,193, or 18.9% of income, offering greater financial flexibility but requiring a substantial upfront capital outlay of $43,080. Given the local market dynamics, conventional financing is likely the optimal choice for buyers with sufficient savings, as it minimizes monthly obligations and avoids FHA mortgage insurance premiums. However, for buyers with limited down payments, the FHA option remains viable and accessible. The minimum down payment for a median-priced home is $7,539 for an FHA loan (3.5%) compared to $21,540 for a 10% conventional loan. The lower barrier to entry for FHA financing makes it a practical fit for first-time buyers or those with modest savings, particularly given the county’s 76.7% owner-occupancy rate, which suggests a stable, established housing market. Rate sensitivity remains a critical factor in this analysis. The current 7.40% interest rate significantly elevates monthly costs compared to historical averages. For instance, had the rate been 6.0%, the FHA monthly payment would have been approximately $1,256, reducing the income allocation from 22.8% to roughly 19.8%. This 300-basis-point differential translates to a $183 monthly difference, or over $2,196 annually. Prospective buyers should prioritize securing competitive rates and consider making larger down payments to offset the impact of high interest rates. The concrete takeaway is that while loan limits are non-binding in Andrew County, the interplay between high interest rates and median income levels necessitates careful budgeting. Buyers should target a 20% down payment to reduce monthly strain, or utilize FHA options while planning for additional reserves to cover the higher total cost of ownership associated with current market rates.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Andrew County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Andrew County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Andrew County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Andrew County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Andrew County qualifies.
