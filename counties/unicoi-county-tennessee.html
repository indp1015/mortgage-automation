📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Financing coverage is robust: the FHA 1‑unit limit of $541,287 exceeds the median home price of $189,600 by 285%, while the conforming limit of $832,750 exceeds it by 439%, confirming that both loan programs can fully finance a typical purchase. Because the FHA limit is more than double the median price, buyers can also consider modestly larger properties without needing a larger loan. The median‑priced home therefore falls comfortably within FHA eligibility and well within conforming capacity. For an FHA loan requiring 3.5% down ($182,964 principal), the estimated principal‑and‑interest payment is $1,266 per month, which equals 29.1% of median household income ($52,218 annually). A conventional loan with 20% down ($151,680 principal) yields a payment of $1,050, or 24.1% of income. Given the price level and available limits, FHA financing is likely the best fit for typical buyers because it requires the smallest down payment and aligns with the median price. A 3.5% down payment equals $6,636, which, while challenging, is more attainable than the 10% ($18,960) or 20% ($37,920) required for conventional loans. While conventional loans provide higher loan amounts up to the conforming limit, the lower down‑payment requirement of FHA makes it the pragmatic choice for most median‑price buyers. Although VA and USDA loans offer zero‑down options, eligibility restrictions and the modest median income make FHA the most accessible conventional‑mortgage alternative for most households. At the current 7.40% rate, the FHA payment of $1,266 consumes nearly a third of median income, indicating reduced affordability compared with lower‑rate environments; a 1% decrease to 6.40% would lower the payment to approximately $860, a 32% reduction. Consequently, prospective buyers should prioritize securing FHA financing and budgeting for mortgage insurance to overcome the down‑payment barrier, as the present rate environment makes monthly costs a significant share of household earnings.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Unicoi County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Unicoi County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Unicoi County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Unicoi County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Unicoi County qualifies.
