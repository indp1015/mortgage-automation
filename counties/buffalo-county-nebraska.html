📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Buffalo County, Nebraska, presents a distinct financing profile where loan limits significantly exceed typical property values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 220% of the median home value of $246,400, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 338%. Consequently, no buyer in this market faces loan limit constraints, ensuring that both FHA and conventional financing options are fully accessible for median-priced properties. This ample coverage means that financing capacity is not a barrier; rather, affordability is driven by income and interest rates. The monthly payment burden remains manageable relative to local income levels. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan on the median home requires a minimum down payment of $8,624 (3.5%), resulting in a principal and interest payment of $1,646. This figure represents 26.0% of the median monthly income derived from the $75,911 annual household income. While this ratio is within the standard 28% debt-to-income threshold, it leaves limited room for property taxes, insurance, and other housing costs. A conventional loan with a 20% down payment reduces the principal and interest to $1,364, or 21.6% of monthly income, offering greater financial flexibility. Given the low entry threshold, FHA loans are likely the most accessible fit for typical buyers seeking to minimize upfront capital, whereas conventional loans serve those with stronger cash reserves who wish to optimize long-term costs. Rate sensitivity significantly impacts affordability in this market. The current 7.40% rate is substantially higher than the sub-4% environments seen in previous years, increasing the monthly payment burden by nearly 50% compared to historical lows. For a buyer with the median income, the difference between a 7.40% rate and a hypothetical 5.0% rate would alter the monthly payment by approximately $350, a material amount for a household earning $75,911 annually. The primary barrier to entry is not the loan limit but the initial cash required for down payments and closing costs. A concrete insight for prospective buyers is that while the median home value of $246,400 is well below national averages, the high interest rate environment necessitates rigorous budgeting. Buyers should prioritize saving for a larger down payment to reduce the loan principal, thereby mitigating the impact of elevated rates and ensuring long-term payment stability.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Buffalo County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Buffalo County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Buffalo County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Buffalo County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Buffalo County qualifies.
