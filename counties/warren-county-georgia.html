📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA and conforming loan limits both far exceed the median home price of $67,100 in Warren County, with the FHA 1‑unit ceiling at $541,287 (807 % of the median) and the conforming ceiling at $832,750 (1241 %). Consequently, virtually any buyer can obtain a loan that meets the loan‑size requirement, eliminating a ceiling‑related barrier that exists in higher‑priced markets. At a 30‑year fixed rate of 7.24 %, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment on a median‑priced home is $441 for an FHA loan (3.5 % down, $64,752 loan) and $365 for a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment ($53,680 loan). Relative to the median household income of $44,289 (≈$3,691 monthly), the FHA payment represents 11.9 % of monthly income, while the conventional 20 % payment is 9.9 %. Both ratios sit comfortably below the 28 % front‑end affordability threshold commonly used by lenders, indicating that the monthly debt service is not a primary affordability constraint for median‑income households. Given the low price level, conventional financing with a 20 % down payment is the most cost‑effective option because it avoids mortgage‑insurance premiums and yields the lowest P&I. However, the required $13,420 down payment (20 % of $67,100) may be prohibitive for many local buyers, especially when the median net worth is modest. An FHA loan reduces the down‑payment floor to $2,349 (3.5 %), which is far more attainable and still results in a payment well within income limits. USDA or VA loans could also be viable for eligible rural or veteran borrowers, offering zero‑down options, but eligibility constraints limit their universal applicability. The 7.24 % rate inflates monthly payments by roughly $70–$80 compared with a 5 % environment, raising the cost of a 20 % conventional loan from $306 to $365 per month. While the rate increase modestly tightens affordability, the low home price cushions the impact. **Practical takeaway:** For most median‑income purchasers in Warren County, an FHA loan with a 3.5 % down payment provides the most realistic path to homeownership, delivering a payment that is only 12 % of monthly income while sidestepping the sizable cash hurdle of a conventional 20 % down payment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Warren County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Warren County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Warren County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Warren County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Warren County qualifies.
