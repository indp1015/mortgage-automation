📝 Mortgage Market Notes

San Mateo County’s housing market presents significant financing challenges, primarily because the median home value of $1,559,600 exceeds the 2026 FHA and conforming loan limit of $1,249,125. Consequently, the standard loan limit covers only approximately 80% of the median property price. This gap forces buyers to either assume a jumbo loan for the remaining balance or purchase homes priced below the limit. For the typical median-priced home, the financing structure is constrained by high-cost county regulations, limiting the utility of government-backed programs for the average buyer unless they target lower-priced inventory. The monthly payment burden remains severe relative to local incomes. At a current 30-year fixed rate of 7.32%, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment results in a principal and interest payment of $8,570. This figure represents 64.7% of the median monthly household income of $158,855, which is well above the standard 30% debt-to-income threshold used by lenders. Even with a 10% down payment, the payment rises to $9,642, further straining affordability. The high interest rate significantly amplifies this burden; compared to historical low-rate environments, the current 7.32% rate increases monthly obligations by thousands of dollars, effectively reducing the maximum affordable home price for median-income households. Given these constraints, conventional financing with a substantial down payment is the most viable path for buyers at the median price point, as FHA and VA loans are insufficient to cover the full loan amount without a jumbo rider. The minimum down payment required to keep the loan within conforming limits is approximately $310,475, or 20% of the median price. This capital requirement is a substantial barrier, requiring a savings pool that exceeds the median annual household income. Prospective buyers should recognize that purchasing at the median price requires either a high-income profile, a co-borrower, or a significant down payment. A concrete takeaway is that buyers targeting the median price must secure at least $310,475 in liquid assets to avoid jumbo loan complexities, while those with less capital should focus on homes priced near the $1,249,125 limit to utilize standard FHA or conforming loan structures more effectively.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in San Mateo County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in San Mateo County is $1,249,125. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in San Mateo County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,249,125 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in San Mateo County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm San Mateo County qualifies.
