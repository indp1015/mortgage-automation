📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Grant County, Wisconsin, presents a financing landscape where loan limits significantly exceed local property values, ensuring broad eligibility for standard mortgage products. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 270% of the $200,700 median home value, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 415%. Consequently, virtually all residential transactions in the county fall well below regulatory caps, eliminating high-cost county restrictions and allowing buyers to access standard FHA and conventional financing without jumbo loan complexities. This ample coverage ensures that the typical Grant County buyer is not constrained by loan maximums, permitting flexibility in property selection across various price points within the median range. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels, though the current 7.40% interest rate imposes a higher cost of capital than in prior years. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with 3.5% down results in a principal and interest payment of $1,340, equating to 24.1% of the $66,858 median household income. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces this burden to $1,111, or 19.9% of income. While these figures are within the general 30% debt-to-income guideline for housing costs, the 7.40% rate significantly increases monthly obligations compared to the sub-4% environments of the early 2020s. This rate sensitivity means that buyers with lower incomes or higher existing debt may find the 24.1% FHA payment ratio challenging, particularly when accounting for property taxes, insurance, and FHA mortgage insurance premiums, which are not included in the principal and interest estimates. Regarding loan type recommendations, conventional financing is likely the optimal fit for buyers with sufficient savings for a 10% to 20% down payment, as it avoids monthly mortgage insurance costs that further erode the affordability margin of FHA loans. For buyers with limited capital, the FHA option remains viable due to the low 3.5% down payment requirement, which amounts to approximately $7,025 for a median-priced home. This initial outlay is feasible for many local households, especially given the county’s high owner-occupancy rate of 69.8%. However, the rapid price appreciation, with a 7.9% five-year CAGR, suggests that entry-level buyers should prioritize securing financing early to mitigate the risk of further price increases. The practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that while loan limits are non-restrictive, the primary barrier to affordability is the combination of high interest rates and rising home values, necessitating careful budgeting for total monthly housing costs rather than just principal and interest.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Grant County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Grant County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Grant County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Grant County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Grant County qualifies.
