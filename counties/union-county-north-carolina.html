📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Union County, North Carolina, presents a financing landscape where loan limits provide substantial coverage relative to local property values. The 2026 FHA one-unit limit of $541,287 covers approximately 130% of the median home value of $417,300, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 200%. Consequently, the typical buyer is not constrained by maximum loan caps, allowing for flexible financing structures. However, affordability metrics reveal significant pressure. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, the estimated principal and interest payment for an FHA loan at the median price is $2,788 per month. This amount represents 32.5% of the median household income of $102,900, exceeding the conventional 28% debt-to-income guideline for housing costs alone. A conventional loan with 20% down payment reduces the monthly burden to $2,311, or 27.0% of income, which aligns more closely with standard underwriting thresholds. Regarding loan type recommendations, conventional financing is likely the most suitable option for qualified buyers in this market. Given that the median home price sits well below both FHA and conforming limits, the primary differentiator is the total cost of borrowing. FHA loans, while offering lower down payment requirements, often carry mortgage insurance premiums that increase the overall monthly outlay. For buyers with sufficient savings, a conventional loan minimizes long-term interest costs and avoids mandatory insurance, provided the credit profile supports it. Down payment barriers remain a critical hurdle. An FHA loan requires a minimum 3.5% down payment, equating to approximately $14,606 at the median price. While this is lower than the 20% required for conventional loans without private mortgage insurance, it still represents a significant upfront capital requirement for households earning the local median. The current interest rate environment significantly impacts affordability compared to historical lows. A 7.40% rate increases monthly payments by roughly 20-25% compared to a 5.0% rate, directly reducing the price range a median-income household can afford. For instance, at 5.0%, the same FHA loan would cost approximately $2,250 per month, a difference of over $500. Prospective buyers should recognize that waiting for rate reductions may not be a viable strategy if home prices continue their recent trajectory, as the 14.2% five-year compound annual growth rate has outpaced income growth. The practical takeaway is that buyers must prioritize saving for a larger down payment to offset high interest rates, as reducing the loan principal is the most effective method to lower monthly payments in a high-rate environment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Union County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Union County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Union County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Union County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Union County qualifies.
