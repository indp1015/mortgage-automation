📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The financing landscape in Lewis and Clark County, Montana, demonstrates robust coverage for the median-priced home. With a 2024 median home value of $393,500, the property sits well within both the FHA 1-unit limit of $541,287 and the conforming limit of $832,750. Specifically, the FHA limit covers 138% of the median price, while the conforming limit covers 212%, ensuring that standard loan products are accessible without requiring jumbo financing. This ample buffer allows typical buyers to utilize conventional or government-backed loans without hitting maximum loan caps, provided they meet standard credit and income requirements. Monthly payment burdens remain significant relative to local income levels. At a current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, a median-priced home financed with an FHA loan (3.5% down) results in a principal and interest payment of $2,629 per month. This figure represents 40.3% of the median monthly household income derived from the $78,237 annual median. Even with a 20% down payment on a conventional loan, the payment is $2,179, or 33.4% of monthly income. These ratios exceed the traditional 28% debt-to-income guideline often used by underwriters, suggesting that many median-income households may struggle to qualify for standard financing without additional income sources or lower-priced properties. For typical buyers, the choice between FHA and conventional loans hinges on down payment feasibility. The minimum FHA down payment on a median home is approximately $13,773, whereas a 20% conventional down payment requires $78,700. Given the median household income, saving $13,773 is more attainable in the short term than accumulating nearly $79,000. However, the higher interest rates and mortgage insurance costs associated with FHA loans may offset the lower initial equity requirement over time. If buyers can save for a 10% down payment, a conventional loan offers a lower monthly P&I of $2,452, reducing the income burden to 38.3%. Rate sensitivity is a critical factor in this high-appreciation market. The 12.3% five-year price CAGR has outpaced income growth, tightening affordability. At 7.40%, a one-percentage-point drop in rates would reduce the FHA monthly payment by approximately $140, significantly improving debt-to-income ratios. Prospective buyers should prioritize increasing down payments to lower loan amounts, as this provides the most direct lever to improve qualification metrics in a high-rate environment. Ultimately, the market supports ownership, but requires careful financial planning to manage the elevated monthly obligations relative to median incomes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Lewis and Clark County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Lewis and Clark County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Lewis and Clark County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Lewis and Clark County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Lewis and Clark County qualifies.
