📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The mortgage financing landscape in Pulaski County, Missouri, demonstrates significant alignment between loan limits and local market values. The 2026 FHA one-unit limit of $541,287 covers 285% of the median home value of $189,800, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 439%. Consequently, the median-priced property falls well below both thresholds, ensuring that standard financing options are accessible without requiring jumbo loan structures. This ample coverage implies that loan limits are not a constraining factor for typical transactions in this region. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan with 3.5% down on a median-priced home results in a principal and interest payment of $1,268 per month. This amount represents 23.6% of the median household income of $64,466. While this ratio is within the standard 28% guideline for front-end debt-to-income ratios, it leaves limited room for property taxes, insurance, and other housing costs. Conversely, a conventional loan with 20% down reduces the monthly P&I to $1,051, or 19.6% of income, offering greater financial cushion. Given the low barrier to entry, FHA loans are likely the most suitable fit for typical buyers seeking minimal down payment requirements, whereas conventional loans serve those with stronger liquidity. Down payment feasibility is a critical consideration. The minimum FHA down payment of 3.5% on a $189,800 home requires approximately $6,643. This sum is less than one month’s median household income, making it a feasible barrier for many local residents. However, the current 7.40% interest rate significantly impacts affordability compared to lower-rate environments. At this rate, the total monthly payment including estimated taxes and insurance will likely exceed 30% of gross income for the median buyer, necessitating careful budgeting. Prospective buyers should prioritize securing pre-approval with a solid credit profile to ensure they can meet lender requirements despite the higher interest environment. The practical takeaway is that while Pulaski County remains an affordable market relative to national averages, the combination of high interest rates and modest incomes means that minimizing down payment through FHA financing while maintaining a debt-to-income ratio below 43% is essential for long-term stability. Buyers should also consider that the 6.0% five-year price CAGR suggests continued appreciation, which may increase entry costs in the near term if rates remain elevated.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Pulaski County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Pulaski County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Pulaski County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Pulaski County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Pulaski County qualifies.
