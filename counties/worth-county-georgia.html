📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA and conforming loan limits both comfortably exceed Worth County’s median home price of $104,900. The FHA 1‑unit ceiling of $541,287 is 516 % of the median value, while the conforming limit of $832,750 is 794 % of that price, confirming that virtually any buyer can obtain a loan within the statutory caps. At the current 30‑year fixed rate of 7.18 %, a median‑priced home financed with an FHA loan (3.5 % down, $101,228 loan) yields a principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment of $685 per month. A conventional loan with 20 % equity ($83,920 loan) reduces P&I to $568 per month. Both figures translate to modest shares of the median household income ($58,694 annual, $4,891 monthly): the FHA payment is 14.0 % of monthly income, and the conventional 20 % payment is 11.6 %. These ratios sit well below the conventional 28 % housing‑cost threshold, indicating that mortgage service is not a primary affordability constraint for the average household. For most buyers, a conventional loan with a 10–20 % down payment is the most cost‑effective option. The FHA’s low‑down requirement (3.5 %) is unnecessary given the low price point and the availability of conventional financing at comparable rates, and it introduces mortgage‑insurance premiums that increase overall cost. USDA eligibility is likely, but the median price already falls well within conventional limits, making the USDA program less compelling. VA financing would be optimal for eligible veterans, offering zero‑down options and lower insurance costs. The minimum cash outlay to purchase at the median price is $3,672 (3.5 % for FHA) or $10,490 (10 % for conventional). For households with the median income, a 10 % down payment represents roughly 21 % of one year’s earnings, a feasible target for many savers; the FHA’s lower requirement eases entry for cash‑constrained buyers but adds insurance costs. Because rates are 7.18 %, monthly payments are about 30 % higher than they would be at a 5 % rate, reducing purchasing power by roughly $80–$100 per $100,000 financed. Nevertheless, the low median home price keeps overall affordability strong. **Practical takeaway:** With median‑price homes well under both FHA and conforming limits, a conventional loan with a 10 % down payment delivers the lowest monthly cost while remaining comfortably affordable for the typical Worth County household.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Worth County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Worth County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Worth County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Worth County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Worth County qualifies.
