📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Houston County, Minnesota, presents a stable financing environment where federal loan limits significantly exceed local market values. The 2026 FHA single-unit limit of $541,287 covers 226% of the median home value of $239,000, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 348%. Because the county is not designated as high-cost, standard national limits apply, ensuring that the typical buyer can secure financing for a median-priced property without encountering ceiling constraints. This ample coverage indicates that loan availability is not a barrier to entry for the average household in this market. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with 3.5% down requires a principal and interest payment of $1,596, which constitutes 24.0% of the median household income of $79,825. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces this burden to $1,323, or 19.9% of income. While the FHA ratio approaches the upper threshold of conventional affordability guidelines, it remains within acceptable limits. The current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% introduces significant cost sensitivity; compared to lower-rate environments, this rate increases monthly obligations by several hundred dollars, making down payment size a critical lever for improving debt-to-income ratios. Given these dynamics, conventional financing is likely the optimal fit for buyers with sufficient savings, as the 20% down payment option offers the lowest monthly cost. However, for first-time buyers with limited capital, FHA loans provide a viable alternative with a minimal 3.5% down payment requirement. At the median price, this equates to approximately $8,365, a feasible amount for many households given the high owner-occupancy rate of 83.4%. USDA eligibility should also be considered for eligible rural properties, potentially offering zero-down options. The primary practical takeaway is that while loan limits are non-restrictive, the 7.40% interest rate necessitates careful budgeting. Buyers should prioritize maximizing down payments to offset high interest costs, ensuring that total housing expenses, including taxes and insurance, remain below 35% of gross income to maintain long-term financial stability in this appreciating market, which has seen a 7.8% five-year compound annual growth rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Houston County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Houston County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Houston County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Houston County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Houston County qualifies.
