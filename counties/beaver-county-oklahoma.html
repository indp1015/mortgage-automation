📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Financing coverage: The FHA 1‑Unit limit of $541,287 exceeds the median home price of $130,100 by 416%, confirming that the typical property is well within FHA eligibility. The conforming 1‑Unit limit of $832,750 covers 640% of the median price, also ample. Monthly payment burden: Using the median‑priced home, an FHA loan with 3.5% down requires a $125,546 loan and a P&I payment of $869, representing 16.2% of median household income ($64,276). A conventional 20% down loan on a $104,080 principal costs $720 per month, or 13.4% of income, while a 10% down conventional loan at $117,090 is $810 per month (12.6% of income). These figures indicate that even the highest‑cost conventional option remains below the 30% threshold commonly used to gauge affordability. Loan type recommendation: Given the median price and the availability of FHA financing, FHA loans are likely the most accessible for typical buyers, especially those with limited down payment savings. Conventional loans remain viable for higher‑income or lower‑debt borrowers, but the 7.40% interest rate reduces monthly payments compared with lower‑rate environments; at 5% the FHA payment would fall to roughly $620 (≈9.7% of income), highlighting the sensitivity to rate changes. At the current 7.40% rate, the FHA payment consumes 16.2% of median household income, while a 1% reduction would lower the payment by over $150 per month, underscoring the material impact of interest rate fluctuations. Down payment barriers: The median home requires a minimum down payment of $4,550 for a 3.5% FHA loan, $13,010 for 10% conventional, and $26,020 for 20% conventional. The 3.5% requirement is feasible for many households, whereas the 20% down payment may be challenging without substantial savings. Practical takeaway: Prospective buyers should prioritize FHA financing to minimize upfront cash needs while monitoring rate fluctuations, as a 1% rate reduction can lower monthly payments by over $150, improving affordability substantially.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Beaver County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Beaver County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Beaver County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Beaver County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Beaver County qualifies.
