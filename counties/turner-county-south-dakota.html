📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Turner County’s financing limits comfortably exceed the median home price of $222,900. The Federal Housing Administration’s 1‑unit limit of $541,287 covers 243 % of the median value, while the conforming loan limit of $832,750 represents 374 % of the median, confirming that both programs can fully fund a typical purchase. For a median‑priced home, the FHA estimate of $215,098 principal (3.5 % down) yields a monthly principal‑and‑interest payment of $1,489, which equals 23.6 % of the median household income of $75,771. The conventional 20 % down loan of $178,320 results in a $1,234 payment, or 16.3 % of income, and the 10 % down conventional loan of $200,610 costs $1,388 per month, or 18.3 % of income. At the current 7.40 % 30‑year fixed rate, affordability is constrained; a 1 % reduction to 6.40 % would lower the FHA payment to roughly $1,330 (a 10.8 % reduction) and the 20 % down conventional payment to about $1,100, markedly improving the debt‑to‑income ratio. Given the price level and the availability of high‑loan‑to‑value financing, a conventional loan with a 10 % down payment is the most practical fit for typical buyers, as it avoids the mortgage‑insurance premium required by FHA while still requiring a down payment that is attainable for households with median savings. The minimum down payment to purchase at the median price is 3.5 % for FHA (≈$7,850) or 10 % for conventional (≈$22,300). With a median household income of $75,771, a 10 % down payment is feasible for most owners, especially when combined with the 79 % owner‑occupancy rate that indicates strong local savings capacity. However, the 7.40 % rate raises the monthly burden; lowering the rate by even 0.5 % would reduce the FHA payment by roughly $75 per month, highlighting the sensitivity of affordability to interest‑rate fluctuations. Prospective buyers should therefore prioritize securing a conventional loan with a 10 % down payment and monitor rate trends, as a modest decrease can significantly improve payment capacity.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Turner County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Turner County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Turner County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Turner County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Turner County qualifies.
