📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Teton County’s mortgage financing landscape is characterized by robust loan limit coverage, which decouples typical buyers from high-cost county restrictions. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 205% of the median home value of $264,000, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 315%. Consequently, the median-priced property falls well within standard financing parameters, eliminating the need for jumbo loans or non-conforming products. This ample coverage ensures that typical buyers can access major government-backed and conventional financing channels without encountering ceiling constraints that often restrict purchasing power in higher-priced markets. Despite favorable limit coverage, the monthly payment burden remains significant relative to local income levels. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan with 3.5% down results in a principal and interest payment of $1,763, consuming 30.0% of the median household income of $70,430. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces this to $1,462, or 24.9% of income. While the conventional option stays within the generally recommended 25-30% debt-to-income threshold, the FHA option sits at the upper boundary of traditional affordability metrics. The minimum down payment for an FHA purchase at the median price is approximately $9,240, a figure that is accessible for many households but still represents a substantial upfront capital requirement. Conversely, the 20% down payment for a conventional loan totals $52,800, which may present a higher barrier to entry for first-time buyers despite the lower monthly outlay. The current interest rate environment significantly impacts affordability compared to historical lows. The 7.40% rate increases the absolute dollar cost of monthly payments, effectively reducing the maximum loan amount a buyer can service with the same income. For instance, the difference between the FHA and 20% conventional payments highlights the trade-off between initial capital deployment and long-term monthly obligations. Given the 11.0% five-year price CAGR, rising home values compound the pressure on affordability, as buyers must secure larger loans to maintain purchasing power. Prospective buyers should prioritize maximizing down payments to reduce loan principal, thereby mitigating the impact of elevated interest rates. Specifically, moving from 3.5% to 20% down reduces the monthly P&I by $301, offering a tangible improvement in cash flow stability. Buyers should evaluate their long-term income stability against these payment structures, ensuring that total debt service, including taxes and insurance, remains manageable within the median income framework.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Teton County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Teton County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Teton County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Teton County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Teton County qualifies.
