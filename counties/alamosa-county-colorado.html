📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA 1‑unit loan limit of **$541,287** is more than twice the median home price of **$239,100**, providing ample coverage (226 % of the typical purchase price). The conforming limit of **$832,750** exceeds the median value by 348 %, so both programs comfortably accommodate the local market; the primary constraint is not the loan ceiling but the borrower’s ability to meet down‑payment and income‑qualification thresholds. At the median price, a conventional loan with 20 % down ($191,280 loan) yields a principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment of **$1,303/month**. This represents **28.2 %** of the median household income (**$55,397/yr → $4,617/month**). An FHA loan with the minimum 3.5 % down ($230,732 loan) requires **$1,572/month**, or **34.1 %** of monthly income. Both ratios fall below the conventional 28‑30 % affordability benchmark for conventional financing, but the FHA option pushes the burden toward the upper end of acceptable limits, especially when property taxes, insurance, and HOA fees are added. For most buyers, a conventional loan with a 10‑20 % down payment is the most cost‑effective choice because it avoids the FHA mortgage‑insurance premium and results in a lower P&I payment. USDA eligibility is limited to designated rural areas, and while Alamosa County contains qualifying tracts, the median home price already sits well within conventional limits, reducing the need for USDA financing. VA loans are only available to eligible veterans, so they are not a universal solution. The minimum cash outlay to purchase the median home is **3.5 % ($8,369)** for FHA or **10 % ($23,910)** for a conventional loan. The FHA down‑payment is modest, yet the higher monthly payment and mortgage‑insurance costs may offset the advantage for higher‑income households. A 20 % conventional down payment ($47,820) cuts the loan balance by $23,910 and reduces the P&I payment by roughly $269/month, improving affordability. At a 7.24 % 30‑year rate, monthly payments are roughly 30‑40 % higher than they would be at a 5 % rate, tightening the income‑to‑payment ratio. Consequently, buyers with limited cash reserves should prioritize larger down payments or seek rate‑buy‑down options to mitigate the impact of today’s elevated rates. **Practical takeaway:** With median home values well under both FHA and conforming limits, the decisive factor is down‑payment size; securing at least a 10‑20 % conventional down payment keeps the P&I burden comfortably below 30 % of household income, even in the current high‑rate environment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Alamosa County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Alamosa County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Alamosa County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Alamosa County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Alamosa County qualifies.
