📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Hawaii County’s mortgage landscape is characterized by robust financing coverage relative to local property values. The 2026 FHA limit of $811,931 covers 156% of the median home value of $519,300, while the conforming limit of $1,249,125 covers 241%. Consequently, the typical median-priced home falls well within standard loan limits, eliminating the need for jumbo financing for most buyers. However, despite this ample coverage, affordability remains a significant challenge due to high interest rates and income constraints. With a median household income of $78,639, the estimated monthly principal and interest payment for an FHA loan at 3.5% down is $3,469, representing 52.9% of monthly income. Even with a 20% down payment on a conventional loan, the payment of $2,876 consumes 43.9% of monthly income. These figures significantly exceed the recommended 28-30% debt-to-income ratio, indicating that the median buyer faces substantial financial strain without additional income sources or assistance. Given the local market dynamics, conventional loans with higher down payments appear to be the most viable path for qualified buyers, as they reduce the overall loan balance and monthly obligations compared to FHA options. The minimum down payment for an FHA loan is approximately $18,176 (3.5% of $519,300), which is a feasible barrier for many households. However, the critical barrier is not the down payment amount, but rather the monthly carrying cost. The current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% exacerbates affordability issues; in lower-rate environments, such as 5.0%, the same FHA payment would drop to approximately $2,800, reducing the income burden to roughly 42.5%. The high rate environment effectively reduces purchasing power, making the median home price of $519,300 difficult to sustain on a single median income. The practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that purchasing at the median price point requires either a household income significantly above the median or the utilization of multi-income households. Buyers should prioritize conventional financing with maximum feasible down payments to lower the principal balance and mitigate the impact of high interest rates. Additionally, exploring local down payment assistance programs or considering properties below the median value may be necessary to achieve a sustainable monthly payment. The 10.4% five-year price CAGR suggests that waiting for price corrections is not a reliable strategy, as appreciation has outpaced income growth in recent years. Therefore, immediate financial planning focused on debt reduction and income enhancement is essential for entry into the Hawaii County housing market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Hawaii County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Hawaii County is $811,931. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Hawaii County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,249,125 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Hawaii County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Hawaii County qualifies.
