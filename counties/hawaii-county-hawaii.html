📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA and conforming loan limits for 2026 are both $1,249,125 for a single‑family unit, which is **241 % of the median home price of $519,300** in Hawaii County. Consequently, the typical home is comfortably within both limits, eliminating a ceiling constraint for most first‑time and move‑up buyers. At a 7.24 % 30‑year fixed rate, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment on a median‑priced home is $3,415 for an FHA loan (3.5 % down, $501,124 loan) and $2,831 for a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment ($415,440 loan). With a median household income of $78,639, the monthly income is $6,553. The FHA payment consumes **52.1 % of monthly income**, while a conventional 20 % down payment consumes **43.2 %**, well above the conventional affordability threshold of 28–30 % for housing costs. Even the conventional 10 % down scenario ($3,185 P&I) represents 48.6 % of income, indicating a substantial burden for the median household. Given the price‑to‑limit ratio and the high monthly cost relative to income, a **conventional loan with the largest feasible down payment** (ideally 20 %) is the most appropriate product for typical buyers who can meet the equity requirement, because it avoids mortgage‑insurance premiums and yields the lowest P&I. FHA financing remains viable for borrowers with limited cash, but the resulting payment-to‑income ratio exceeds 50 %, suggesting a higher risk of financial strain. USDA and VA loans are not applicable to most buyers in this county because the area is classified as high‑cost and USDA eligibility is limited to lower‑income rural zones. The minimum cash needed to purchase at the median price is **$18,176** (3.5 % FHA) or **$51,930** (10 % conventional) for down payment alone, not including closing costs. For the median household, the 3.5 % down payment represents 23 % of annual income, a sizable hurdle for many renters. A 7.24 % rate inflates monthly obligations by roughly 1.5 %–2 % compared with a 5.5 % environment, pushing the median buyer’s housing cost well above conventional affordability metrics. **Practical takeaway:** prospective buyers should aim to save at least a 20 % down payment to keep P&I below 45 % of income; otherwise, they will need to target homes substantially below the median price or seek supplemental income to achieve sustainable affordability.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Hawaii County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Hawaii County is $1,249,125. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Hawaii County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,249,125 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Hawaii County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Hawaii County qualifies.
