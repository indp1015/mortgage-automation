📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Johnson County, Nebraska, presents a distinct financing landscape where loan limits significantly exceed local property values. The 2026 FHA single-family limit of $541,287 covers 348% of the median home value of $155,500, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 536%. Consequently, no buyer in this market faces high-cost county restrictions or limit constraints for standard single-family homes. This ample coverage ensures that typical transactions remain well within federal and agency guidelines, simplifying the underwriting process for both lenders and borrowers. The primary affordability challenge stems from the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, which elevates monthly obligations relative to local income levels. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with a 3.5% down payment results in a principal and interest payment of $1,038. This figure represents 21.6% of the median household income of $57,632. While this ratio is below the 28% threshold generally considered manageable for front-end debt, it leaves limited room for property taxes, insurance, and other housing costs. In contrast, a conventional loan with 20% down reduces the payment to $861, or 17.9% of income, offering greater flexibility. The rapid appreciation rate of 13.6% annually over the past five years has outpaced income growth, meaning that while home values have risen sharply, the fixed income base has not kept pace, intensifying the payment burden. Given these dynamics, conventional financing with a higher down payment is likely the most prudent recommendation for qualified buyers, as it minimizes monthly outlays and avoids FHA mortgage insurance premiums. For those unable to save 20%, the FHA option remains viable, provided total debt-to-income ratios stay within lender guidelines. The minimum down payment barrier is low in absolute terms; an FHA buyer needs only $5,443, while a 10% conventional buyer needs $15,550. These amounts are feasible for many households, though the 3.5% requirement is the most accessible entry point. However, the 7.40% interest rate significantly impacts affordability compared to lower-rate environments; a 100-basis-point drop would reduce the FHA payment by approximately $75 monthly. Prospective buyers should prioritize maximizing down payments to offset the high interest rate, as this directly reduces the loan principal and long-term interest costs, ensuring that housing expenses remain sustainable within the county’s moderate income profile.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Johnson County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Johnson County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Johnson County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Johnson County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Johnson County qualifies.
