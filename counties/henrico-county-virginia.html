📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Henrico County’s housing market presents a favorable financing environment where standard loan limits comfortably exceed median property values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 151% of the $359,200 median home value, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 232%. Consequently, no buyer purchasing at the median price faces high-cost county restrictions or limit caps, ensuring full eligibility for standard government-backed and conventional financing options. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels. For a median-priced home, an FHA loan with 3.5% down results in a principal and interest payment of $2,399, which constitutes 32.4% of the median monthly household income derived from $88,783. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces this burden to $1,989, or 26.9% of income. Given that the current 30-year fixed rate stands at 7.40%, these ratios indicate that Henrico County remains within typical affordability thresholds, though the higher interest rate significantly increases monthly costs compared to historical low-rate environments. Conventional financing is likely the optimal fit for typical buyers in this market. Since the median home value is well below both FHA and conforming limits, buyers with credit scores above 620 and the ability to save for a 5% to 20% down payment can access conventional loans, which often offer lower overall costs than FHA due to the absence of upfront and annual mortgage insurance premiums. The minimum down payment barrier is relatively low; at 3.5% for FHA, the required cash is approximately $12,572, while a 20% conventional down payment requires $71,840. The feasibility of these amounts is supported by the county’s robust median household income, which is nearly double the national average. The primary constraint for prospective buyers is not loan availability but the rapid appreciation of home values. With a 5-year price CAGR of 9.9%, the median home value has increased from $223,900 in 2017 to $359,200 in 2024. This sustained growth, coupled with a 7.40% interest rate, means that monthly payments are significantly higher than they would have been in a 3% to 4% rate environment. A concrete insight for buyers is that delaying purchase to save for a larger down payment may be counterproductive, as continued price appreciation could outpace savings accumulation, effectively increasing the total cost of entry and locking in higher principal balances.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Henrico County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Henrico County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Henrico County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Henrico County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Henrico County qualifies.
