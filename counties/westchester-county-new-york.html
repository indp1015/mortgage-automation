📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Westchester County’s housing market presents a distinct financing landscape where the median home value of $663,200 exceeds the 2026 FHA single-family limit of $541,287. Consequently, FHA financing covers only 82% of the median price, forcing most buyers to seek conforming loans. The conforming limit of $1,209,750 comfortably covers 182% of the median value, ensuring that standard agency-backed financing remains accessible without requiring jumbo loan qualifications. This structural gap means that the typical Westchester buyer cannot utilize FHA’s lower down payment options for a median-priced home unless the property is significantly below the market median. The monthly payment burden is substantial relative to local income levels. With the 30-year fixed rate at 7.40%, an FHA loan on a median-priced home requires a principal and interest payment of $4,431, consuming 44.7% of the median household income of $118,976. This ratio exceeds the conventional 43% debt-to-income guideline, rendering FHA financing prohibitively expensive for the average household. Conversely, a conventional loan with a 20% down payment reduces the principal and interest to $3,673, or 37.0% of median income. While this is more manageable, it still requires a substantial cash outlay of $132,640 upfront. A 10% down payment on a conventional loan results in a $4,132 monthly payment, which remains high relative to income and may still strain debt-to-income ratios when combined with other obligations. Given these constraints, conventional financing is the most viable option for typical buyers, as it aligns with the conforming limit and avoids the premium costs associated with jumbo loans. However, the high entry barrier is pronounced. The 7.40% interest rate significantly amplifies the monthly cost; compared to a hypothetical 5.0% rate, the 20% down payment scenario would see monthly payments drop by approximately $500, highlighting the sensitivity of affordability to interest rate fluctuations. The primary barrier to entry is not just the price, but the combination of high interest rates and the need for significant equity. Prospective buyers should focus on saving for a 20% down payment to minimize monthly debt load, as lower-down-payment options either exceed income limits or result in unaffordable monthly obligations. Without substantial cash reserves, accessing the median Westchester market remains difficult, suggesting that buyers may need to target lower-priced segments or increase household income to meet standard lending criteria.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Westchester County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Westchester County is $786,337. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Westchester County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $1,209,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Westchester County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Westchester County qualifies.
