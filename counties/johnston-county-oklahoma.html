📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Johnston County, Oklahoma, presents a financing landscape where federal loan limits significantly exceed local price points. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 465% of the $116,500 median home value, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 715%. Consequently, no typical buyer in this market faces a loan limit constraint. The primary financial challenge is not loan eligibility but monthly payment burden relative to income. With a median household income of $52,688, the estimated principal and interest (P&I) payment for an FHA loan stands at $778, representing 17.7% of monthly income. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces this to $645, or 14.7% of income. These figures remain within standard debt-to-income guidelines, indicating that the median-priced home is financially accessible for the average household in the county. Regarding loan type recommendations, the FHA option offers the lowest barrier to entry due to its 3.5% minimum down payment requirement. For a median-priced home, this amounts to approximately $4,078, a sum that is more feasible for first-time buyers than the $23,300 required for a 20% conventional down payment. While USDA loans may be available depending on specific rural tract eligibility, the FHA program provides broader applicability across the county’s 72.2% owner-occupied housing stock. The current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40% significantly impacts affordability compared to historical low-rate environments. At this rate, the monthly P&I for a $112,422 FHA loan is higher than it would be at 6.0% or 5.0% rates, effectively increasing the required income threshold to qualify. However, because local home values have stabilized with a 5.2% year-over-year increase in 2024, the payment shock is mitigated by the relatively low absolute loan amounts. The practical takeaway for prospective buyers is that down payment capital, rather than credit score or loan limits, remains the primary hurdle. Buyers should prioritize saving for the 3.5% FHA minimum to leverage the low entry point. Given that the median home value has risen from $79,500 in 2017 to $116,500 in 2024, the 7.9% five-year CAGR suggests that waiting may increase the total capital required. Therefore, securing financing at the current 7.40% rate on a median-priced property remains a viable strategy, provided the borrower’s debt-to-income ratio allows for the $778 monthly P&I obligation. The market’s affordability is driven by low asset prices rather than favorable interest rates, making the absolute dollar cost of entry the critical variable for household budgeting.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Johnston County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Johnston County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Johnston County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Johnston County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Johnston County qualifies.
