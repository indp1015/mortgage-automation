📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Perry County, Kentucky, presents a distinct financing landscape where federal loan limits significantly exceed local market values. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 590% of the median home value of $91,800, while the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 907%. Consequently, no buyer in this market faces loan limit restrictions, as even the most expensive typical properties fall well within both FHA and conventional caps. This ample coverage ensures that financing availability is not constrained by regulatory ceilings, allowing borrowers to access standard mortgage products without requiring jumbo loan structures or high-cost county adjustments. Monthly payment burdens remain manageable relative to local income levels, though interest rates exert significant pressure. With a 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, the principal and interest payment for an FHA loan at the median price is approximately $613 per month. This represents 17.4% of the median household income of $42,181, a figure that sits comfortably within the recommended 28% debt-to-income threshold for housing costs. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces this burden to $508, or 14.5% of income. However, the primary barrier to entry is the down payment requirement. For the median-priced home, an FHA borrower requires a minimum of $3,213 (3.5% down), while a conventional borrower needs $9,180 (10% down). Given the median household income, saving $3,213 is feasible for many residents, whereas the $9,180 conventional threshold poses a more substantial hurdle for first-time buyers with limited liquid assets. The current interest environment significantly impacts affordability compared to lower-rate periods. At 7.40%, monthly payments are substantially higher than they would be at historical lows, directly increasing the total cost of ownership over the life of the loan. Despite this, the low absolute price point of the median home mitigates the impact of high rates, keeping monthly obligations within reach for average earners. For prospective buyers, the optimal strategy involves prioritizing FHA financing to minimize upfront capital requirements. The 3.5% down payment requirement aligns best with the local income profile, allowing buyers to enter the market with minimal savings. Additionally, the stable price trend, with a 5-year CAGR of 4.6%, suggests that while appreciation is occurring, it is not outpacing income growth to the extent that affordability is being rapidly eroded. Buyers should focus on securing stable employment and building a modest cash reserve to meet the low FHA down payment threshold, ensuring they can qualify for the most accessible loan products available in this low-cost housing market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Perry County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Perry County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Perry County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Perry County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Perry County qualifies.
