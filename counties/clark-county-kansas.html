📝 Mortgage Market Notes

The FHA and conforming loan limits both far exceed the median home price in Clark County. With a 2026 FHA 1‑unit ceiling of $541,287, the limit is 618 % of the median $87,600 value, while the conforming ceiling of $832,750 is 951 % of that price. Consequently, virtually any single‑family home in the county can be financed under either program without encountering a loan‑size ceiling, eliminating a common barrier in higher‑priced markets. At a 30‑year fixed rate of 7.24 %, the principal‑and‑interest (P&I) payment on a median‑priced home is $576 for an FHA loan (3.5 % down) and $477 for a conventional loan with a 20 % down payment. Both figures represent modest shares of the median household income of $63,043 ($5,254 monthly). The FHA payment equals 11.0 % of monthly income, and the conventional 20 % payment equals 9.1 %. When combined with typical property‑tax and insurance costs (estimated 1.2 % of value annually, or $88 monthly), total monthly housing costs rise to roughly $664 (FHA) and $565 (conventional), representing 12.6 % and 10.8 % of income respectively—well within the conventional 28 % affordability threshold. Given the low price point and ample loan limits, conventional financing with a 20 % down payment is the most cost‑effective option, delivering the lowest P&I and eliminating mortgage‑insurance premiums required for FHA loans. For buyers lacking sufficient cash, the FHA program remains viable; its 3.5 % down requirement translates to a $3,066 down payment on a median home, which is attainable for many local households. USDA eligibility is also possible for rural parcels, offering zero‑down options, but USDA limits are tied to income caps that may exclude higher‑earning households. The 7.24 % rate inflates monthly payments by roughly $150 compared with a 5 % environment, reducing purchasing power by about $20,000 of home price. Nonetheless, because the median home price is only $87,600—well below national averages—the rate impact is muted relative to higher‑priced markets. **Practical takeaway:** Prospective buyers can comfortably afford a median‑priced home with a 20 % conventional down payment, keeping total housing costs under 11 % of household income even at today’s 7.24 % rates.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Clark County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Clark County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Clark County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Clark County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Clark County qualifies.
