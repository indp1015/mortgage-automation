📝 Mortgage Market Notes

Coconino County’s housing market presents a distinct affordability challenge, primarily driven by rapid price appreciation that has outpaced income growth. The 2026 FHA loan limit of $541,287 covers 121% of the median home value of $448,000, ensuring that standard financing options remain accessible for the typical property. Similarly, the conforming limit of $832,750 covers 186% of the median price, indicating that high-balance loans are rarely necessary for median-priced homes. Consequently, the primary barrier to entry is not loan limit restrictions but rather the steep monthly payment burden relative to local median household income of $72,966. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.24%, the financial strain is significant. An FHA loan on the median home requires a 3.5% down payment of $15,680, resulting in a principal and interest payment of $2,946. This amount consumes 48.4% of the median monthly income, a ratio that exceeds the traditional 30% debt-to-income guideline for mortgage-only payments. Even with a 20% down payment on a conventional loan, the monthly payment of $2,442 still represents 40.2% of median income. These figures suggest that the median household income is insufficient to support a median-priced home without substantial supplemental income or a larger household size. The 13.2% five-year price CAGR has significantly eroded affordability, as home values have nearly doubled since 2017 while incomes have grown more modestly. For prospective buyers, the conventional 20% down option offers the lowest monthly outlay but requires a substantial $89,600 upfront investment, which may be prohibitive for first-time buyers. Conversely, the FHA option lowers the down payment barrier to $15,680, making it the most accessible entry point despite the higher monthly payment percentage. Given that the median home is well within both FHA and conforming limits, loan type selection should be driven by down payment capacity rather than limit constraints. Buyers with limited cash reserves should prioritize FHA financing to minimize initial capital requirements, while those with greater savings might opt for conventional loans to reduce long-term interest costs and avoid mortgage insurance. The high interest rate environment amplifies the need for careful budgeting, as even a 100-basis-point decrease in rates would only reduce the FHA payment to approximately $2,870, still leaving a heavy burden on median earners. Ultimately, success in this market depends on leveraging higher income brackets or multi-income households to bridge the gap between median prices and sustainable payment levels.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Coconino County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Coconino County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Coconino County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Coconino County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Coconino County qualifies.
