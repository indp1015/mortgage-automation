📝 Mortgage Market Notes

In Davison County, South Dakota, the current mortgage landscape offers robust financing coverage for the typical homeowner. The 2026 FHA limit of $541,287 covers 263% of the median home value of $205,800, while the conforming limit of $832,750 exceeds the median price by 405%. Consequently, loan limits do not constrain purchasing power for the majority of buyers in this market. With a median household income of $66,208, the affordability of a median-priced home remains viable despite elevated interest rates. At the current 30-year fixed rate of 7.40%, an FHA loan with 3.5% down requires a principal and interest payment of $1,375 per month. This figure represents 24.9% of the median monthly income, a ratio that sits near the upper threshold of conventional affordability metrics but remains within standard qualification guidelines. A conventional loan with 20% down reduces this burden to $1,139 per month, or 20.6% of income, offering a more comfortable margin for debt-to-income calculations. For typical buyers in Davison County, the FHA loan represents the most accessible entry point due to its low down payment requirement of 3.5%. At the median price, this amounts to approximately $7,203, a sum that is significantly more attainable than the $41,160 required for a 20% conventional down payment. While USDA loans may be available if specific rural eligibility criteria are met, the FHA program provides broader applicability across the county. The recent price appreciation, with a 5-year CAGR of 7.9%, has increased the initial capital required for entry, yet the substantial gap between local home values and federal loan limits ensures that financing availability is not a primary barrier. The current 7.40% interest rate significantly impacts monthly cash flow compared to historical lows; a 1% decrease in rates would reduce the FHA payment by approximately $100, highlighting the sensitivity of affordability to monetary policy. Prospective buyers should prioritize securing pre-approval with FHA financing to leverage the low down payment structure, which preserves liquidity for closing costs and initial home maintenance. Given that the median home value has risen from $141,000 in 2017 to $205,800 in 2024, buyers must account for the increased total cost of entry. However, because the median price remains well below both FHA and conforming limits, the primary constraint on purchasing power is not the loan ceiling but rather the borrower’s income and credit profile relative to the current 7.40% interest rate environment. Maintaining a debt-to-income ratio below 25% through careful budgeting will ensure long-term financial stability in this appreciating but affordable rural market.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FHA loan limit in Davison County?

The 2026 FHA limit for a single-family home in Davison County is $541,287. This is above the national floor of $524,225.

What is the conforming loan limit in Davison County?

The 2026 conforming loan limit is $832,750 for a single-family home. Loans above this amount are considered "jumbo" and typically require stricter credit and larger down payments.

Can I use a VA or USDA loan in Davison County?

VA loans have no county limit for eligible veterans with full entitlement. USDA loans are available in eligible rural areas—check the USDA's eligibility map to confirm Davison County qualifies.
